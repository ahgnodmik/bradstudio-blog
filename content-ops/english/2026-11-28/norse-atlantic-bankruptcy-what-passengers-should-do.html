If you hold a Norse Atlantic Airways ticket, your flight is not going to operate. The low-cost long-haul airline suspended all flights on 26 November 2026, and on 27 November the board of its Norwegian operating company decided to file for bankruptcy. This guide explains what happened in the Norse Atlantic bankruptcy, which parts of the group are affected, and the steps that give you the best chance of getting your money back or getting home.

What happened to Norse Atlantic

The collapse took three days.

  • 25 November: Norse said its financial situation was "critical" and that it needed new financing for short-term liquidity. Its shares closed down about 85% on the Oslo stock exchange, according to Reuters and CNBC.
  • 26 November: Norse suspended all flights while it looked for a financing solution, and warned there was no assurance one would be found. Reuters reported the airline said it would file for bankruptcy if short-term financing was not secured urgently.
  • 27 November: The board of Norse Atlantic Airways AS, the Norwegian operating company, decided to petition for bankruptcy at the Agder District Court in southern Norway. The court appoints a trustee to run the process. Al Jazeera, Dagens Næringsliv and E24 reported the filing. All flights are cancelled and ticket sales have stopped.

Which Norse companies are in bankruptcy and which are not

This matters for refunds, so read it carefully.

  • Norse Atlantic Airways AS, the Norwegian operating company, is the entity filing for bankruptcy. E24 and DN, citing the Norwegian business register, put its workforce at about 300 employees.
  • Norse Atlantic ASA, the parent listed on the Oslo exchange, is not part of the petition.
  • Norse Atlantic UK, the British subsidiary that holds its own air operator certificate and flew services from London Gatwick, is also not part of the petition. Chief executive Eivind Roald said the parent and the UK company were working to find a way to keep part of the business flying.

Do not read too much into the UK unit being outside the filing. The UK Civil Aviation Authority (CAA) has said that all Norse flights are cancelled and that passengers should not go to the airport. Norse's own website gives the same advice unless you have already booked with another airline.

Why the airline failed

Norse had been under financial pressure for months. It reported a first-half 2026 net loss of US$94.6 million and launched a formal process in the summer that could lead to a sale, merger or partnership. Two developments then came together:

  • Fuel costs. Reuters and others linked the sharp rise in jet fuel prices to the war involving Iran. Roald said the company could not absorb the extra cost.
  • Aircraft leases. Norse said its lessors had moved to terminate the leases on all 12 of its Boeing 787-9s and demanded the aircraft back. The company disputes the terminations. Forbes Middle East, Aerotime and Travel Radar reported this.

Routes affected

Reports from Al Jazeera, GB News and Travelers Today describe cancellations on Norse's routes from London Gatwick and Manchester to Thailand, from London to South Africa and to the United States, including Orlando, and on its Norwegian network. Norse has not published a count of stranded passengers. One British estimate circulated in the media, but we could not confirm it in a second source, so we do not repeat it.

What passengers should do now

1. Do not go to the airport

There is no Norse flight to catch and no Norse desk to help you. The CAA and Norse both say this. If you are abroad and need to get home, book a new flight with another airline first, then pursue the money later.

2. Save every document

Norse told customers to keep the booking confirmation, ticket number, proof of payment and receipts for any replacement travel. Take screenshots of your booking page now, before the website changes. Keep receipts for hotels, meals and transport caused by the cancellation.

3. Work out how you paid

Your options depend almost entirely on how and where you bought the ticket.

4. Understand the card routes

Section 75 makes a UK credit card provider jointly liable with the seller for purchases between £100 and £30,000, even if only part of the price went on the card. Chargeback is different. It is a scheme run by card networks, not a legal right, and each bank sets its own deadlines. Contact your bank this week rather than waiting for news from the trustee. US cardholders should also act quickly, because dispute windows are counted from your statement date.

5. Check your travel insurance

Many policies exclude airline failure unless you bought specific cover, sometimes called scheduled airline failure or end supplier failure cover. Read your policy wording or call the insurer. Norse itself has told customers to check their insurance and contact their banks.

6. Claim in the bankruptcy only as a last resort

Norse says refunds can only be claimed in the insolvency process and that payment is not guaranteed. Customer service is no longer available. Ordinary passengers usually rank behind secured creditors in a bankruptcy, so expect a long wait and a partial payment at best. Watch for claim instructions from the court-appointed trustee.

Questions passengers are asking

Is Norse Atlantic bankrupt?

Its Norwegian operating company, Norse Atlantic Airways AS, filed for bankruptcy on 27 November 2026. The listed parent and the UK subsidiary are not part of that filing, but all Norse flights are cancelled.

Can I claim EU261 or UK261 compensation?

These rules give rights against the operating airline. With the airline in bankruptcy and flights cancelled, any claim would go into the insolvency process. Focus first on refund routes that do not depend on the airline paying.

Will other airlines offer rescue fares?

Some airlines have offered discounted seats to stranded passengers after past airline collapses. Check the websites of carriers on your route, and watch CAA updates if you are travelling to or from the UK.

What if my Norse flight was months away?

Your options are the same. Start a Section 75 or chargeback claim now. Do not wait to see whether Norse returns, because deadlines run from your payment or statement date.

Your next step today

Find your booking confirmation and the card statement showing the payment. If you paid by UK credit card, open a Section 75 claim with your card provider today. If you paid any other way, call your bank or travel company today. Then check the CAA and Norse websites once a day for trustee updates.
