If you saw a viral post saying Kylian Mbappé lost "two Chinese sponsors," the count is already out of date, and several key details remain unverified.

Three Chinese brands ended their deals with the Real Madrid and France forward on October 27, 2026. They are beverage maker Dongpeng, dairy group Mengniu and cheese maker Milkground. The trigger was a resurfaced video clip about a meme. Mbappé apologized the same day. This article sticks to what several outlets report in common and marks the rest as unconfirmed.

1. The clip and what he said

The video appears to come from The Bridge, a French-language talk show. Franceinfo and Yahoo Sports report that his Real Madrid teammate Aurélien Tchouaméni hosts it. Mbappé was asked which meme about himself made him laugh the most. He described one showing "some guy," adding that he thought it was "a Chinese dictator." The clip then cuts to his face edited onto a photo of Mao Zedong.

He did not say Mao's name. Outlets quote his words with small differences. The recording date is unclear. Sports Illustrated placed it in April. ABC News in Australia described it as a backstage interview from earlier this year. ABC also said it could not independently verify the video. The clip spread again on X in recent days.

Mao, who founded the People's Republic and died in 1976, remains a sensitive figure in China. Jokes about him are widely seen there as disrespectful.

2. Three brands, not two

Early coverage counted two companies. Franceinfo's headline spoke of "two major Chinese companies." Yahoo Sports also opened with two brands. Milkground announced its exit later that day. France 24, ESPN and Sports Illustrated then reported three.

  • Dongpeng. The drinks maker had used Mbappé to promote an electrolyte drink. It said it has "zero tolerance" for any behavior that offends national sentiment. Some English reports call the company Eastroc.
  • Mengniu. China's largest dairy producer announced the end of the relationship on Weibo.
  • Milkground. The cheese maker, also known as Miaokelanduo, said it was ending all cooperation "effective immediately."

None of the three disclosed the contract value or the time left on its deal. None said which clause it relied on.

3. State TV pulls La Liga

China Media Group (CMG) oversees the state broadcaster CCTV. It decided on Monday, October 26, to suspend La Liga broadcasts. It also stopped ads that feature Mbappé. CMG called apologies from La Liga, Mbappé and Real Madrid "an absolute prerequisite" for further talks. Al Arabiya and the Global Times both reported that condition.

La Liga issued a statement. It said it "deeply regrets the offense caused to Chinese fans." It added that the remarks were made in a personal capacity and do not represent the league. No date has been given for the broadcasts to resume.

4. The apology

Mbappé posted a statement on Tuesday, October 27, in three languages. He called his comment "ill-judged." He said he now understood it had hurt many people in China and in Chinese communities abroad. He said he deeply respects the Chinese people and their history, and that he was sincerely sorry. His statement did not mention the sponsors. No outlet has reported any deal being restored.

Past cases with the same pattern

This is not the first time an athlete's words in China moved from social media to sponsors and TV schedules.

  • Mesut Özil, December 2019. The Arsenal midfielder criticized China's treatment of Uyghurs. CCTV dropped Arsenal's match against Manchester City from its schedule. Arsenal said Özil's post was his personal opinion.
  • NBA, October 2019. Houston Rockets general manager Daryl Morey tweeted support for Hong Kong protesters. CCTV said it would not air NBA preseason games played in China. SportBusiness later covered the league's efforts to return to CCTV.
  • BTS, October 2020. RM mentioned the suffering of the Korean War in an award speech. UPI reported that BTS-related posts for brands, including Samsung, vanished from Chinese shopping sites. The China Project noted it was unclear who removed them.

How morals clauses work

Global endorsement deals often include a morals clause. It lets a brand end or change a deal when an athlete's conduct harms its image. The Fashion Law and Lexplug both describe the clause as an exit tool for the company.

  • Narrow clauses need a criminal conviction.
  • Middle-ground clauses use an arrest or a charge as the trigger.
  • Broad clauses cover any conduct that brings the brand into "disrepute." Public backlash alone can be enough.

Remedies vary. They can include termination, fines, clawback of fees already paid, or a halt to image use. High-profile athletes sometimes negotiate a "reverse" morals clause. It lets the athlete leave if the brand damages his or her image.

Whether the three Mbappé deals contained such a clause is not known. The legal basis for each exit, and any penalty, has not been published.

What is still unconfirmed

  • The original video. The source upload and exact recording date have not been verified. ABC said it could not confirm the clip's authenticity.
  • Real Madrid. The Global Times reported that the club had made no official statement as of Tuesday. Reports of a club apology could not be confirmed from an original club statement.
  • Chinese government comment. Reports differ on whether China's foreign ministry addressed the case. One report said the Chinese Football Association lodged a protest with Real Madrid. Both points remain unconfirmed.
  • Broadcast return. CMG has given no timeline.
  • Contract terms. Values, remaining terms and termination clauses are all undisclosed.
