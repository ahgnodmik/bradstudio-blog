If you have booked a room in Korea for mid-November or later, the listing could disappear before you arrive.

From November 12, 2026, online booking platforms in Korea must not broker lodging that has no verifiable business registration. A platform that breaks the rule faces a fine of KRW 5 million. The rule covers listings that were already online before that date. Travelers are not fined. But an unregistered place you booked may be pulled from the app. This guide explains the change and what to check now.

1. What changes on November 12

Until now, Korean platforms had no separate legal duty to check a host's lodging registration. The Herald Business reported this gap. The new rule closes it.

A platform must check the business registration certificate (yeongeop singojeung) before it brokers a room. If it cannot verify the certificate, it must not sell the room. The Ministry of Health and Welfare said unregistered lodging sold through booking platforms has been growing. SBS reported the same reasoning: the state wants a minimum check by the platform.

The law itself caps the fine at KRW 10 million. The decree sets the actual amount at KRW 5 million. The Korea Herald reported the figure when the ministry first proposed it in July. Korean outlets including Asia Economy and Etoday confirmed the final version in October.

2. Which apps are covered

The first group is OTAs that take listings directly from hosts and process bookings. The second group is wider. It covers platforms that pull rooms from other agencies or suppliers and show them on their own screens. The Herald Business and Etoday both describe this two-part scope.

For a traveler, that can mean most apps that show Korean rooms. Korean apps such as Yanolja and Yeogi Eottae are in scope. Daily Dian and Sukbak Magazine named Airbnb as well. The exact edge cases, such as foreign sites that resell Korean rooms, depend on the ministry's guidelines. Treat coverage of any specific foreign site as unconfirmed until those guidelines are public.

3. Old listings get no free pass

The rule applies to rooms listed before November 12. A platform that wants to keep selling an existing room must confirm its registration. If it cannot, it must stop brokering that room from the start date. Local governments can also flag unregistered hosts and ask platforms to remove them. A platform that finds an unregistered room on its own must take it down.

Airbnb has run its own version since 2024. It announced in September 2024 that Korean hosts had to submit licence details, with a grace period for existing listings. Its newsroom said non-compliant listings would be removed from the end of 2025. Airbnb also does not allow officetels or goshiwons as listings in Korea. The new law turns this kind of check into a legal duty for every platform.

4. How fines will be decided

Some Korean reports say a single slip will not trigger an automatic fine. Officials would weigh whether the platform checked, when it learned of the problem, how fast it removed the listing, and whether it acted on purpose. The ministry says it will send operating guidelines to local governments and platforms. We have not seen the final text. Treat these details as unconfirmed.

What travelers should check now

  • List your stays from November 12 onward. Autumn foliage weekends and winter trips may fall in this window.
  • Look for registration details on the listing. A registration number or licence information on the page is a good sign. On Airbnb Korea, hosts must display it.
  • Check the property type. Hotels, guesthouses and licensed homestays each need registration. Korea has a category for city homestays that host foreign tourists (oegugin gwangwang dosi minbak). A room in an officetel is a warning sign.
  • Recheck your booking after November 12. If the listing vanished or the status changed, contact the platform at once. Asking early gives you more time to find another room.
  • Never move payment off the app. A host whose listing was removed may offer a direct bank transfer. You would lose the platform's refund protection.
  • When booking new stays, favor clear registration. At similar prices, a room with visible licence details carries less risk of cancellation.

What happens to your money

The fine falls on the platform, not on you. Refunds are a separate matter. No government-wide refund standard for cancelled unregistered stays has been reported. Each platform will apply its own policy. Airbnb's help center says that when a listing is removed, the host cannot accept new reservations, and bookings that start 30 or more days after removal are cancelled. Other platforms' handling is unconfirmed.

What is still open

  • Ministry guidelines. The full criteria for fines have not been published.
  • Refunds and compensation. No common rule; it depends on each platform.
  • Scale of removals. No reliable count of affected listings has been reported by two or more outlets.

Open your booking app today and check every Korean stay dated November 12 or later. If a listing shows no registration details, message the host or platform now and ask what happens to your reservation.
