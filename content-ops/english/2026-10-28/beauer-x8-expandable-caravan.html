Big families want a caravan that sleeps everyone, but a big caravan is hard to tow, store and park.

French maker Beauer says its new X8 solves that with a telescoping body. On the road it is a compact twin-axle trailer. At camp, a button slides out two side modules in about 60 seconds. The living space roughly triples to about 30 square meters, and up to eight people can sleep inside. The starting price is €109,990. Here is what multiple outlets and Beauer's own page agree on, and what a buyer should check first.

Small differences exist in coverage. Gear Patrol gives the length as about 21.5 ft. One report swapped the closed width and height. A few outlets printed a much heavier weight, which looks like a unit error. The table uses the figures that Beauer, New Atlas, autoevolution and The Autopian share.

1. How the expansion works

Think of a telescope. Two side modules sit nested inside the central body for travel. Once parked, electric actuators push them out to the left and right. The trailer grows from 2.05 m to about 4.8 m wide. Beauer says the process takes about a minute. Gear Patrol, New Atlas and The Autopian all describe the space as roughly tripling.

Beauer showed the X8 at the 2026 Caravan Salon in Düsseldorf. It is the largest model the company has built. Beauer first became known for a much smaller expanding trailer, the 3X. The X8 applies the same idea at family scale.

2. The layout sleeps eight

Open, the X8 feels closer to a small cabin than a caravan.

  • Two bedrooms, each with a 140 × 200 cm double bed.
  • A living area with two sofas that convert into beds.
  • A dining table for the whole group.
  • A kitchen and a separate bathroom.

Two double beds and two sofa beds give the eight berths. That count assumes some sleepers are children or happy to share.

3. Weight is the real limit

The headline number for towing is not 30 m². It is 2,700 kg. That is the maximum permissible weight. A typically equipped X8 already weighs about 2,600 kg. That leaves about 100 kg for everything you load.

For eight people, 100 kg goes fast. Bedding, food, water, bikes and camp chairs can use it up on day one. Options also add weight. Germany's promobil and the Dutch site Campingtrend both flag air conditioning, a motor mover and solar panels as extras that eat into payload. Check the weight of your exact build before you order.

Your tow vehicle needs a rated towing capacity of at least 2,700 kg. Many midsize SUVs fall short. Full-size SUVs and pickups are the more likely match.

Licensing depends on where you live. In Europe, the car plus a 2.7-tonne trailer will usually exceed 3,500 kg combined. Dutch coverage notes that this generally means a BE licence. Rules vary by country, so check with your local licensing authority. Outside Europe, check your state or national towing rules and any trailer-brake requirements for this weight.

4. Price, ordering and delivery

The base price is €109,990. Beauer's site, Gear Patrol and promobil all print that figure. Dollar conversions in US coverage range from roughly $122,900 to about $128,000, depending on the exchange rate on the day. Autoevolution put it at about $127,400.

Gear Patrol says the X8 is open for reservations now. Some outlets list it as a 2027 model. The first delivery date is unconfirmed. Beauer has not published a firm date in the materials we checked.

For buyers outside Europe, the sticker price is not the landed price. The Autopian notes that shipping and import duties are not included for US buyers. It also points out that Beauer has no real service network in the United States. The Autopian reports a US version with 115-volt wiring and US outlets; that detail is unconfirmed by a second source.

Who the X8 suits, and who it does not

  • Good fit: a large family that stays at one campsite for a week or more and wants real bedrooms.
  • Good fit: buyers with a strong tow vehicle and room to store a 6.65 m trailer.
  • Poor fit: travelers who move every night. The expansion is quick, but packing for eight is not.
  • Poor fit: anyone who needs to carry a lot of gear inside the trailer, given the small payload.
  • Poor fit: buyers far from Europe who need local service and parts.

Questions to ask before you reserve

  • What is the empty weight of my exact configuration, with every option?
  • What payload is left after those options?
  • Is the electrical system built for my country's voltage and plug type?
  • Who repairs the slide mechanism if it fails, and how fast can parts arrive?
  • What are shipping, duty and tax costs to my door?
  • When is the confirmed delivery date, in writing?

Start with your tow vehicle's manual: if its rated towing capacity is under 2,700 kg, stop there. If it clears that bar, request a full spec sheet for your build from Beauer before you place a reservation.
