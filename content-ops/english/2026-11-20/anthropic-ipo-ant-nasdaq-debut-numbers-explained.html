Anthropic, the company behind the Claude AI models, is now a public company. Its shares began trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday 19 November 2026 under the ticker ANT, and the first day produced a confusing spread of numbers: an $83 offer price, a $111.50 first trade, a $97.17 close, $81.3 billion raised, and company valuations anywhere from about $1.27 trillion to $1.76 trillion. This piece sorts out what each number actually measures, what the debut did and did not settle, and what remains unknown as the stock heads into its second session.

Not investment advice. This article explains reported facts and does not recommend buying, selling or holding any security, and it makes no price forecast.

The Anthropic IPO in brief

  • Exchange and ticker: Nasdaq Global Select Market, ticker ANT (Class A common stock).
  • Marketed range: $70 to $78 per share for 825 million shares, set at the start of the roadshow.
  • Final offer price: $83 per share, above the top of the range (Anthropic's own announcement, CNBC, TechCrunch).
  • Shares sold: about 980 million, after the deal was upsized.
  • Money raised: roughly $81.3 billion (CNBC, CBS News, NPR).
  • First trade: $111.50, about 34% above the offer price (Forbes, Benzinga, CNBC).
  • First-day close: $97.17, up 17.07% from $83 (CNBC, Forbes, ZDNet Korea).

How the first day unfolded

A newly listed stock does not trade the moment the market opens. Nasdaq first runs an opening process that matches buy and sell orders, and the first print only appears once a clearing price is found. For Anthropic, that print came in at $111.50, a pop of roughly a third over what IPO buyers had paid the night before.

The stock gave back a good part of that gain as the session went on. It closed at $97.17. Anyone who bought at the opening trade was sitting on a loss of about 12.9% by the close, while anyone who received shares at $83 in the offering was up about 17%. Same stock, same day, very different outcomes depending on the entry price. Reported intraday highs differ slightly between outlets, so we have left that figure out.

The wider market had a rough day. CNBC reported that the Nasdaq Composite fell 2.25%, and NBC News described Anthropic's record IPO as unable to halt a broader slide. A Reuters report carried by The Korea Times said the debut failed to revive optimism around the AI trade.

$81.3 billion raised is not the company's value

The single most common mix-up in coverage of this listing is treating the amount raised as the size of the company. They are different things.

What the $81.3 billion measures

The proceeds figure is simply the number of shares sold in the offering multiplied by the offer price: roughly 980 million shares at $83. According to Anthropic's pricing announcement, most of those shares were newly issued by the company, while a smaller block was sold by existing stockholders. Money from the stockholder sales goes to those sellers, not to Anthropic.

What the trillion-dollar figures measure

Market value is the share price multiplied by all of the company's shares, not just the slice sold this week. Founders, employees and earlier investors hold far more stock than the offering put on the market. That is why the valuation is more than ten times the size of the raise.

The spread between outlets comes from how the shares are counted. A fully diluted count includes stock options and other equity awards that could become shares in the future. A basic count includes only shares already issued. Using the $97.17 close, CNN put Anthropic's fully diluted value at $1.76 trillion, while Yahoo Finance and Forbes reported about $1.27 trillion. At the $83 offer price, CNBC and CBC reported a fully diluted valuation of about $1.5 trillion. Some reports of a "$2 trillion" company were based on the $111.50 opening trade, a price the stock did not hold.

That last line matters. Alongside the offering, Nvidia, Alphabet and several other technology companies agreed to buy non-voting shares at the IPO price in a private placement reported at about $26 billion. That money is counted separately. Adding it to the $81.3 billion to claim a "$100 billion-plus raise" mixes two different transactions.

Is it really the biggest IPO on record?

CNN and CNBC both described the $81.3 billion raise as the largest on record, ahead of the $75 billion SpaceX raised in its June 2026 listing. That comparison is based on the base offering size. Underwriters in large deals often hold an option to sell extra shares if demand is strong, and the final totals including those extra shares can change the ranking. Whether Anthropic's underwriters exercised that option, and the final all-in total, was unconfirmed at the time of writing. The safest description is that Anthropic's base offering was the largest reported to date.

Who raised bubble concerns, and what they said

The debut did not end the argument about AI valuations. The views below are attributed to the outlets that reported them; this article does not endorse either side.

  • The Associated Press noted that Anthropic, OpenAI and SpaceX's AI division have all lost more money than they have made because of the cost of building AI infrastructure, and that this has fuelled concern about a bubble in the sector.
  • CNBC ran an analysis asking why Anthropic, which many had hoped would "save" the AI trade, failed to lift tech stocks on its first day. The same outlet noted that many investors and analysts remain confident in AI.
  • Reuters, in a report carried by The Korea Times, framed the session as one where Wall Street slid because the debut failed to revive optimism about AI.

A large valuation and a profitable business are different things. Readers comparing the debut with earlier technology listings may want to read at least one skeptical and one optimistic analysis before drawing conclusions.

Access for international investors: the South Korea example

Not every country was included in the offering. South Korean outlets, including The Korea Daily and The Fact (both in Korean), reported that South Korea was left out of the public offering, so individual investors there could not subscribe for shares at $83. Korean brokerages did not join the underwriting group, according to The Fact.

That leaves Korean retail investors with one route: buying ANT on the open market after listing, through a domestic brokerage's overseas-stock service, the same way they buy any other US share. With US daylight saving time now over, the Nasdaq's regular session runs from 11:30 p.m. to 6:00 a.m. Korea Standard Time. When each Korean brokerage enabled orders for ANT was not confirmed by two sources, so check your own broker's app.

Investors elsewhere face a similar picture: if your country was not part of the offering, you are buying at the market price, not the IPO price. The general practical points apply everywhere. A limit order caps what you pay, which matters for a stock that moved more than 10% within its first session. Extended-hours trading is thinner and can swing more than the regular session. Currency conversion and trading fees add to your cost on both the way in and the way out. Taxes on gains depend on where you live.

What is still unknown

  • Day two: At the time of writing, the second session had not yet taken place. One report said the stock slipped in after-hours trading on Thursday; that is from a single source and is unconfirmed.
  • Over-allotment: Whether the underwriters' option to sell extra shares was exercised, and the final total raised, is unconfirmed.
  • Lock-up expiry: When existing shareholders can begin selling was not confirmed by two sources for this piece.
  • Broker availability by country: Which overseas brokers offered ANT from the first minute of trading varies and was not independently confirmed.

What to do now

Before reading the next headline about Anthropic's share price, note the two anchors: the $83 offer price and the $97.17 first-day close. When an article says the stock is "up" or "down," check which of those it is measuring from, and when it quotes a valuation, look for the words "fully diluted" or "market cap." If you are considering buying, confirm in your broker's app that ANT is tradable, check the currency and trading fees, and decide on a maximum price before you place a limit order.
