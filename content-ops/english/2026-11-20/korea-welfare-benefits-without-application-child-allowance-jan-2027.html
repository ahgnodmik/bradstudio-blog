If you are expecting a baby in Korea, or you have just had one, you have probably been told that you must file paperwork within 60 days or lose part of your child benefits. That rule is about to change. From 21 January 2027, Korea plans to start paying the child allowance, the parental benefit and the newborn "First Meeting" voucher without a separate application, and to treat older and disabled residents as having applied for their pensions once government records show they qualify. This explainer covers what the new system does, what it does not do, why families with babies due this winter should not wait for it, and the naming contest the Ministry of Health and Welfare opened today.

What "application-based welfare" means

Korean welfare has long worked on what officials call the application principle, or sincheongjuui. You can be fully eligible for a benefit, but if you never file the form, you never get paid. That design made sense when local offices had no way of knowing who had just had a baby or who had turned pension age. Today most of that information already sits in government databases: birth registrations, resident records, income and property data. The gap between "eligible" and "actually receiving" is what Korean media call the welfare blind spot, and the amendments passed on 1 October are meant to shrink it.

What changes on 21 January 2027

Universal child benefits: no application form

Three benefits that every family with a newborn can receive are moving to a no-application model: the child allowance, the parental benefit, and the First Meeting voucher (cheonmannam iyonggwon). Under the new process, once a birth is registered, the local government asks the parent or guardian to sign a consent form confirming the bank account for payment. When that form comes back, the money starts flowing. The application form itself disappears.

The start date for payments also moves. Today the clock starts in the month you apply, unless you apply within 60 days of the birth, in which case payments are backdated to the birth month. Under the new rules, the reference point becomes the month of birth itself. A parent who is slow to return the account form should not lose the first month or two of payments.

Basic Pension and Disability Pension: "deemed to have applied"

Means-tested benefits work differently, because officials still need to check income and assets. For the Basic Pension (for older residents) and the Disability Pension, if government records show a person may qualify, the person will be treated as having applied. The usual income and asset review then follows. This is an important nuance. Being "deemed to have applied" opens the file; it does not guarantee a payment. Someone whose income or property is above the threshold will still be turned down.

Households in crisis: officials can apply on their behalf

Under current rules, a public official can file for livelihood benefits without the person's consent only in very narrow cases, such as when the person is mentally incapacitated. The amendments widen that power so that officials can apply on behalf of people in crisis who cannot or do not ask for help themselves. Korean reports describe this as a shift toward the state finding at-risk households first, rather than waiting for them to come forward.

What does not change

The amendments change the process, not the amounts. As of November 2026, the main benefits for a newborn are:

Why you should not wait if your baby is due this winter

The new system is planned, not live. Until 21 January 2027, the old rules apply in full, which means you must apply, and the 60-day window matters. With the parental benefit at 1,000,000 won a month for a baby under one, missing the window by a month can cost a family around 1.1 million won once the child allowance is added.

Babies born in December 2026 or January 2027 sit in an awkward spot, because their 60-day window will straddle the start date. The ministry has not said how those cases will be handled (unconfirmed). The safe course is simple: if your child is born before 21 January, apply under the current rules within 60 days. The easiest route is the one-stop service that lets you apply for several benefits at the same time as registering the birth, either online through Government24 or in person at your local community center (jumin center). If you are unsure, the Ministry of Health and Welfare call center at 129 can point you to the right office.

Do not confuse this with the July 2027 overhaul

A separate government proposal would merge the First Meeting voucher, the parental benefit and the child allowance into two new programs for babies born from 1 July 2027, provisionally called the newborn welcome payment (aimaji jiwongeum) and the basic child allowance. That plan is a government proposal still tied to the 2027 budget, and English-language Korean outlets have reported that the government is considering backdating it to babies born from January 2027. None of that is final (unconfirmed). The January change is about how you receive benefits; the July proposal is about how much and under which program names.

The naming contest that opened today

"Improving the application principle" is a mouthful, and the ministry is asking the public for a better name. From Friday 20 November to Friday 4 December 2026, the Ministry of Health and Welfare is running a public policy-branding contest. Anyone can take part. Entrants can propose:

  • a new name for the policy,
  • a slogan, and
  • other welfare programs whose application process should be simplified next.

Entries are submitted through a Naver Form reached by the QR code on the official contest poster. Korean reports say prizes are awarded by a draw among participants rather than as cash for winning entries; the number of prizes and their value should be checked on the official notice (unconfirmed). The form is in Korean, so non-Korean speakers may want a Korean-speaking friend to help.

Who this matters most to

  • Expecting parents: know your 60-day deadline and apply now if the birth comes before 21 January.
  • Families with parents near pension age: run the Basic Pension estimate on Bokjiro and apply now if your parents already qualify, rather than waiting for automatic processing.
  • Anyone worried about a neighbor in difficulty: you can contact the local community center or call 129; from next year officials will have more room to act.

What to do now

If a baby is on the way, put the date 60 days after the due date in your calendar today and decide which parent's bank account will receive the payments. If the birth falls between December and late January, ask your community center which procedure will apply and keep the answer in writing. If you have an idea for the new policy name, or a welfare program you wish you had not needed to apply for, submit it before Friday 4 December.
