If you grew up on Mass Effect or Dragon Age, the headlines this week were hard to read, and harder to sort into fact and rumor.

On October 21, 2026, Electronic Arts told staff it is closing BioWare, the studio behind Mass Effect and Dragon Age. Early work on the next Mass Effect game has stopped. BioWare opened in Edmonton, Canada, in 1995, so the closure ends a 31-year run. Here is what EA actually said, what has only been reported, and what it means for you as a player.

What EA confirmed

The news came in an internal memo from two EA executives: Cam Weber, president and chief studios officer, and David Tinson, president and chief operating officer. Variety, Game Developer and Video Games Chronicle all reported its contents.

  • BioWare is closing. This is not a downsizing. The memo says EA is shutting the studio and its Edmonton office.
  • The next Mass Effect is cancelled. A small BioWare team had the game in early development. EA said it decided "not to move forward" with it.
  • BioWare said goodbye itself. The studio posted on its official X account that "after more than three decades of shared adventures, BioWare is closing."
  • Some projects survive. EA will keep investing in EA Sports, Battlefield, Apex Legends, a Marvel Iron Man game and the next Star Wars Jedi game.
  • Other cuts landed too. EA cancelled Project Lotus, a planned game in The Sims series, and will focus on another Sims project code-named Project Evergreen. The team behind skate will shrink, but the game continues.

The memo called BioWare "a legendary studio that helped define the RPG genre" and said EA does "not take this decision lightly."

What is reported, not confirmed

Several numbers in the headlines did not come from EA. Keep them in a separate box.

The 400 job cuts

Bloomberg reported that EA is cutting about 400 jobs worldwide, roughly 3% of its staff. It said about 50 of those jobs are at BioWare. Bloomberg cited people familiar with the matter. EA's memo gave no total and no per-studio breakdown, as Newsweek and Variety noted. Most outlets repeating "400" are repeating Bloomberg. Treat it as a single-source figure until EA or a second independent outlet confirms it. One outlet also put the share at 2.7%, so even the percentage varies.

Star Wars: Battlefront 3

The memo says Respawn and DICE, two EA studios, are working together on a "future project." Variety reported that this project is Star Wars: Battlefront 3. EA has not named the game.

The leaked Mass Effect trailer

On October 22, a cinematic video of about two minutes appeared on the r/masseffect subreddit. It shows a new human lead, not Commander Shepard, talking with the asari character Liara T'Soni in a bar. It ends with a "Mass Effect: Universe" logo. Kotaku, Video Games Chronicle and Push Square reported that Bloomberg's Jason Schreier and IGN each verified the footage as real.

Three cautions apply. It is a cinematic trailer, not gameplay. Nobody has explained how it leaked. And "Mass Effect 5" is a nickname used by the press, not a title EA has used. Claims about when the trailer was finished rest on unclear sourcing.

Why it happened: the money and the track record

EA has not given a detailed reason beyond restructuring. Two facts frame the decision.

EA is now a private company

In August 2026, EA went private. A group made up of Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (PIF), private equity firm Silver Lake and Affinity Partners bought the company in a deal valued at about $55 billion. Shareholders got $210 per share in cash. TheWrap reported the deal included $20 billion in debt financing from J.P. Morgan, with $18 billion funded at closing. EA left the Nasdaq after 36 years as a public company.

The BioWare closure is the first large cut since the deal, about two months later. Defector called BioWare the "first major casualty" of the takeover. GamesRadar+ put "after being bought by Saudi Arabia" in its headline. That link is the outlets' framing. EA has not tied the closure to the buyout. Reports of a $700 million annual cost-saving target exist, but the sourcing is indirect, so it remains unconfirmed.

BioWare's recent games struggled

  • Anthem (2019): The online-only shooter shut down its servers on January 12, 2026. People who bought it can no longer play it.
  • Dragon Age: The Veilguard (2024): EA said it reached about 1.5 million players in its launch quarter. That was about half of EA's expectations.
  • Staff losses before the closure: Bloomberg reported in early 2025 that BioWare had fallen from more than 200 developers to fewer than 100, after EA moved staff to other teams permanently.

Before all that, BioWare built a reputation few studios match. Its credits include Baldur's Gate, Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic, the Mass Effect trilogy and Dragon Age. EA bought the studio in 2007.

What it means for players

This is the part most readers care about. Here is the honest answer: EA has not announced any change to existing BioWare games. No delisting, no server shutdown and no end of support has been announced for any title as of October 25, 2026. Coverage we reviewed found no official statement on these points either way.

  • Games you own: Nothing official says they will stop working. EA, not BioWare, publishes these games, and EA still owns the Mass Effect and Dragon Age brands.
  • Online features: The Anthem case is the precedent to remember. EA gave about six months' notice before switching off its servers. An online-only game can become unplayable when servers close. Watch EA's official announcements for any similar notice.
  • The franchises: EA cancelled the next Mass Effect. It did not say Mass Effect or Dragon Age are finished. It also did not say whether another studio could take over. Inven Global noted that EA's ownership of the brands leaves room for a sale or an outside developer, but that is speculation, not a plan.

The wider cuts: from Edmonton to Seoul

BioWare is the biggest name in a long run of games industry cuts. Korea, home to some of the world's largest game companies, is going through its own version.

XL Games

XL Games, a game studio within the Kakao Games group, ran a voluntary retirement program (huimang toejik) in May 2026. Money Today Broadcasting (MTN) and Sisa Journal-e both reported that the company then told worker representatives it would begin consultations required before layoffs for urgent business reasons. The Kakao union opposed the restructuring, and Inven Global reported that it threatened a strike.

NCSoft

In October 2024, NCSoft spun off three new-game development studios and one AI unit into separate companies. The Kyunghyang Shinmun and Electronic Times reported that it also offered company-wide voluntary retirement at the same time, its first since 2012.

How Korea differs

Korean cuts so far mostly take the form of voluntary retirement and spin-offs. In our search, we found no Korean case of a famous studio being closed outright, as BioWare was. The ownership story differs too. EA's cuts follow a change of owner to a fund-backed group. The Korean cases come from companies under the same owners, cutting after weak results.

What is still unknown

  • The official number of jobs cut, company-wide and at BioWare.
  • Whether any BioWare staff will move to other EA teams, and on what severance terms.
  • The future of the Mass Effect and Dragon Age brands.
  • The official name and progress of the game in the leaked trailer.
  • Whether the Respawn and DICE project is Battlefront 3.

If you play BioWare games, follow EA's official news and support channels for any notice about online services. Check the original reports below before sharing a number or a rumor. And if a BioWare game shaped your taste in stories, now is a good time to play it again.
