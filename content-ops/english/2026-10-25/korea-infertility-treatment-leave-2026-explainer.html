If you work in Korea and have fertility appointments coming up, you may be burning annual leave on days the law already covers.

From November 27, 2026, Korea's infertility treatment leave (nanim chiryo hyuga) gets more generous. The yearly total stays at 6 days. The paid part doubles, from the first 2 days to the first 4 days. The government cap on wage support for workers at smaller companies doubles too, from KRW 168,420 to KRW 336,840. The change comes from amendments to the Equal Employment Opportunity and Work-Family Balance Assistance Act and the Employment Insurance Act. The Ministry of Employment and Labor released a guide for employers and HR managers on August 23, 2026.

This explainer covers who can take the leave, how the pay works, how to ask for it, and what is still unclear for foreign employees. It is about employment rights only. It is not medical advice.

The remaining 2 days of the 6 stay unpaid. The leave has grown before. In February 2025 it went from 3 days a year (1 paid) to 6 days a year (2 paid). November's change is the second step.

Who can take the leave

Any employee, men included

The leave is a legal right under Article 18-3 of the Equal Employment Opportunity and Work-Family Balance Assistance Act. It is not limited to permanent staff. Men can take it too. A husband can request it for days when he has his own fertility tests or procedures. The law covers treatments such as artificial insemination and in vitro fertilization.

Preparation days count

The scope is wider than it used to be. The leave covers the procedure day and the rest right after it. It also covers essential preparation steps before a procedure, such as fertility tests and ovulation induction. That matters if your treatment needs several clinic visits in a short window.

Foreign employees: unconfirmed

We could not find two independent sources that say directly whether foreign employees covered by employment insurance qualify for the leave or the government benefit. One expat guide notes that employment insurance is optional for some foreign workers, which may affect the benefit. Treat your eligibility as unconfirmed. Ask your HR team, or call the Ministry of Employment and Labor customer center at 1350. Mention your visa type and whether you pay employment insurance.

How the pay works

Smaller companies: the government chips in

Workers at "priority support companies" (usoen jiwon daesang gieop) get a government benefit through employment insurance. Most small and medium-sized firms fall into this group. From November 27, the government covers the first 4 paid days, up to KRW 336,840 in total.

Large companies: the employer pays

Large companies get no government support. They still owe you the 4 paid days. They pay your ordinary wage for those days out of their own pocket. Your right to paid days is the same. Only the payer differs.

A worked example

The cap works out to KRW 84,210 per day (336,840 divided by 4). Say your ordinary daily wage is KRW 120,000. You should get KRW 480,000 for 4 paid days. The government covers up to KRW 336,840. Your employer covers the gap of KRW 143,160. If your daily wage is below KRW 84,210, the cap does not bite.

How to request it

  • Put it in writing. Submit a request with the dates you want and the date you are applying. Email or another electronic document works.
  • Be ready for proof. Your employer can ask for documents showing you are getting fertility treatment. The government says the Ministry of Health and Welfare's notice of approval for fertility treatment cost support can replace a medical certificate when claiming the benefit. Requirements may vary, so ask HR first.
  • Ask early. Procedure dates can move at short notice. Send the request as soon as a date is set.
  • Keep records. Save your request and the company's reply. You may need them later.

Can the company say no?

The law says the employer must grant the leave on the days you request. The only exception is when those days would seriously disrupt normal business. Even then, the employer may only move the dates, and only after talking with you. A busy week alone is generally not enough. An employer that refuses the leave faces a fine of up to KRW 5 million.

Retaliation and privacy

From November 27, firing, disciplining or otherwise punishing a worker for using the leave becomes a crime. The penalty is up to 3 years in prison or a fine of up to KRW 30 million. Employers also may not disclose what they learn about your treatment through the request process against your wishes.

Your company's work rules may still say "2 paid days." The law sets the minimum. If HR says the rules have not been updated, write that answer down too.

What is still unconfirmed

  • Leave used before November 27. If you already used 2 days this year, it is not settled how many later days become paid. We could not check the transition clause of the amended law. Most explanations say unpaid days already taken will not turn into paid days retroactively. Treat this as unconfirmed.
  • Insurance period. Some guidance says you need a minimum period of employment insurance coverage to get the government benefit. The exact number of days and the claim deadline are unconfirmed. Check with Work24 (Goyong 24) or your local employment center.
  • Where to claim. The benefit is described as a claim made after the leave, through an employment center or Work24. Menus and forms may change after November 27.
  • Foreign employees. As noted above, eligibility is unconfirmed.

What to do this week

Do you have treatment planned after November 27? Email HR now. Ask them to confirm three things in writing: that you get 4 paid days, who pays (the company or the government benefit), and which documents they need. If you are a foreign employee, add a fourth question about your eligibility, or call 1350 before you book your dates.
