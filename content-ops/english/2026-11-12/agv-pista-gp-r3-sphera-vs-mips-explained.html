If you are shopping for a new motorcycle helmet, you have probably seen rotational impact protection on spec sheets, usually under the MIPS name. AGV has now taken a different route with its Pista GP R3 racing helmet, moving the slip layer from inside the helmet to the outside of the shell and building it from tiny spheres. This explainer covers how the Sphera system is meant to work, how it compares with the MIPS approach, and which claims rest only on the maker's own testing.

How we checked: the details below appear in at least two independent reports, including New Atlas, RideApart, autoevolution, GPOne, Visordown and TopSpeed. Test figures come from AGV and are attributed to AGV. Anything carried by only one outlet is marked "unconfirmed." This article does not rate the helmet's safety, and no independent test data comparing Sphera with other systems was found. Details are current as of 11 November 2026.

What AGV announced

AGV, the Italian helmet maker, unveiled the Pista GP R3 at the MotoGP San Marino Grand Prix at Misano in September 2026. It replaces the Pista GP RR as the brand's top racing helmet. RideApart and autoevolution both reported that AGV-sponsored riders in MotoGP, Moto2 and Moto3 wore it during that race weekend. Multiple outlets describe the shell as full carbon fiber.

The headline feature is a system AGV calls Sphera RLS, short for Release Layer System. According to GPOne and Theuth, the helmet has a second outer shell sitting on top of the main shell, with a layer of small spheres separating the two. New Atlas and the Portuguese site Motojornal both put the count at more than 1,600 spheres, each 2mm in diameter. Other outlets simply describe hundreds of microspheres.

How the Sphera layer is meant to work

Most motorcycle crashes do not drive the head straight down onto the road. The rider is usually moving forward, so the helmet strikes the surface at an angle. Friction can grab the shell and twist the head suddenly. Research on rotational impacts suggests that this twisting, where the brain moves at a different rate from the skull, is a major factor in concussion and other brain injuries.

Sphera targets that oblique impact. GPOne and Visordown explain that when the helmet strikes at an angle, the outer shell is designed to roll over the sphere layer and release in a controlled way. Because the outer layer moves first, less rotational force is meant to reach the head. Think of it as a sacrificial skin that takes the twist so the inner shell and the rider's head do not take all of it.

The six-times figure, and what it does not say

AGV says the Sphera system makes concussion six times less likely, and Visordown and autoevolution among others repeated that figure. It is important to read it carefully. The number comes from AGV's own testing. The comparison is with the same helmet built without Sphera, not with rival helmets or with MIPS-equipped models. We found no independent laboratory result that reproduces it under the same conditions. It is a maker's claim, and real-world crash performance cannot be judged from it.

A correction worth knowing

Some social media posts described the spheres as a golf-ball dimple pattern that cuts drag. That is wrong. The spheres sit between the two shells as an impact-management layer, not on the surface as an aerodynamic feature. AGV did separately describe aerodynamic work on the helmet, which may be where the confusion came from.

Sphera versus MIPS

MIPS, short for Multi-directional Impact Protection System, is the best-known rotational protection technology. It adds a thin low-friction layer inside the helmet, between the rider's head and the energy-absorbing liner. In an angled impact, this layer lets the helmet shift slightly relative to the head, reducing the rotation passed on. It began in bicycle helmets and has spread widely into motorcycle helmets.

Sphera chases the same goal from the opposite side. The slip layer sits on the outside, between two shells, and it is made of loose spheres rather than a continuous film. New Atlas summed it up in its headline by saying AGV had flipped MIPS-style technology inside out. Racing helmets are fitted very snugly and have little spare room inside, but we found no statement from AGV explaining why it chose the outside, so we will not speculate.

Weight, aerodynamics and certification

Adding a second shell and a sphere layer would normally add weight. AGV says the Pista GP R3 is no heavier than the Pista GP RR, and several outlets repeated that, but no exact weight has been published, so the figure remains unconfirmed. On aerodynamics, the helmet was reportedly developed in a wind tunnel and refined with MotoGP riders on track, looking at both straight-line drag and side forces through corners. The ventilation was also reworked.

On certification, RideApart and autoevolution both report the helmet was designed to meet FIM FRHPhe-02, the second edition of the International Motorcycling Federation's racing helmet standard, which puts added emphasis on oblique impact testing. Whether road versions will carry ECE 22.06 or DOT approval, and which models those would be, could not be confirmed.

Price and availability

AGV has not announced a price. TopSpeed noted that pricing was not disclosed. One outlet suggested a figure just under $1,800, but it conflicts with other reports and is unconfirmed. TopSpeed also cited an AGV US representative saying sales would begin in the first quarter of 2027; no second source confirmed this, so the date is unconfirmed. European and Asian timing, colorways and size range were not found in public material.

Should you wait for it?

A new idea in rotational protection is worth following, but a helmet is safety equipment, and one technology should not decide the purchase alone. Fit matters at least as much as features. A racing helmet that is the wrong shape for your head will not perform as designed, whatever sits between its shells. If you need a helmet now, models with MIPS or other rotational systems are already on sale from several brands. Whatever you consider, look for certification labels and independent test results rather than marketing figures.

Your next step: write down your head circumference and the certification your riding requires, whether that is ECE 22.06 or DOT for the road or FIM FRHPhe-02 for racing. Try on two or three current helmets with rotational protection at a local dealer to learn which shell shape fits you. Then check AGV's official site or your dealer for confirmed pricing and dates before making plans around the Pista GP R3.
