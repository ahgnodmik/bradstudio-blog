Sony says the PlayStation 2 sold about 160 million units, more than any other console, yet it never made a portable version. One hobbyist decided not to wait any longer.

A maker who goes by LCLDIY has built the "PlayStation 2 DUO," a clamshell handheld that folds shut like an old flip phone. It is built from the boards of three broken PS2s and a flexible screen salvaged from an early foldable phone. It is not a Sony product, it is not for sale, and it is not an emulator. Here is what the build is, what has been reported about it, and what you can realistically do if it makes you want PS2 games on the go.

What the PlayStation 2 DUO is

The story starts at a flea market. According to TechEBlog and Retrododo, LCLDIY picked up three non-working PS2 consoles for about $20. Rather than repair one, the maker cut up the motherboards and combined working sections into a single board small enough for a handheld.

The rest of the device was built around that board.

  • Screen: a flexible display taken from an early foldable phone, which lets the whole device fold in half.
  • Shape: a clamshell that opens top to bottom. Open, you get a full screen. Closed, it is easier to carry and the screen is protected.
  • Case: designed in 3D modeling software and printed on a 3D printer.
  • Controls: new circuit boards and PlayStation-style buttons meant to recreate the feel of the original DualShock 2.
  • Hinges: reports say LCLDIY made special hinges to hold the flexible panel in place while it folds.

The name is a nod to dual-screen and folding phones. iXBT Games even joked that it is "the new iPhone Duo killer." It is a one-off hobby build, not a consumer device.

Why real hardware matters

Most retro handhelds sold today run emulators on smartphone-class chips. That works well for many games, but emulation is software pretending to be the original console. Accuracy varies from title to title, and some games still have glitches.

The DUO takes the other route. Coverage from TechEBlog, Retrododo, Handheld Guru and iXBT Games all describes it as running on genuine PS2 hardware, the console's own chips moved into a new body. For a PS2 fan, that is the appeal: the games run the way they did in 2002, just on a screen you can fold.

One report mentions titles such as Grand Theft Auto: Vice City and God of War running on it, though we could not confirm the game list with a second source. How games are loaded is less clear. One outlet says it plays discs or their digital equivalents, but we did not find that detail confirmed by a second independent source.

How it compares with earlier PS2 handheld builds

LCLDIY is not the first person to shrink a PS2. Back in 2019, Liliputing and CNX Software covered "PiS2," a DIY portable PS2 with a small screen. In 2026, German outlets GameStar and PC Games Hardware covered another modder who reverse-engineered the PS2 board to build a handheld that runs games without emulation. The DUO's twist is the folding display.

Can you buy one?

No. There is no price, no store page and no announced plan to sell it. The name includes "PlayStation," but Sony has nothing to do with it.

What LCLDIY did share is the design. Multiple reports say the PCB files and the 3D-printable case data are posted on the project's Hackaday.io page, and a build video is on YouTube. That means a skilled person could, in theory, make one.

Be realistic about what that involves.

  • Board surgery: cutting and rewiring PS2 motherboards takes serious soldering skill and patience. One slip can ruin a board.
  • Parts hunting: a usable flexible display from an older foldable is not something you can pick up at an electronics shop.
  • Donor consoles: $20 for three PS2s was a lucky find. Expect to pay more, and expect some donors to be beyond saving.
  • Time: this is a project measured in weeks or months, not a weekend kit.

Other ways to play PS2 games today

If the DUO mostly made you nostalgic, there are easier paths.

  • PlayStation Plus Premium: Sony's top PS Plus tier includes a Classics catalog with some PS2 titles. TechRadar and TheSixthAxis keep lists, but the lineup changes, so search the PlayStation Store for the exact game first. These run through emulation, not original hardware.
  • Your original console: if you still have a PS2, cleaning the laser and replacing worn cables often brings it back to life. A small portable monitor can make it semi-portable for a trip.
  • Used hardware: working PS2 Slim units still turn up secondhand. Test the disc drive before you pay.
  • Keep your discs legal: whatever route you take, play games you own. Downloading copies you do not own is piracy in most countries.

What is still unconfirmed

  • Screen size: one report gives a size for the panel, but we could not confirm it with a second source.
  • Battery capacity and play time: not disclosed in the coverage we checked.
  • Game list: specific titles shown running come from a single report.
  • Game loading: whether it reads discs, USB storage or both is not confirmed by two sources.
  • Wireless controller support: reported by a single outlet only.
  • Fold angle and hinge details: reports describe special hinges, but specific figures, such as how far the screen can safely bend, come from single sources.

Start with LCLDIY's build video on YouTube to see the device in motion. If you are tempted to build one, open the "PlayStation 2 DUO" page on Hackaday.io and read the parts list before buying a single donor console. If you only want to play, check whether your favorite PS2 game is in the PS Plus Classics catalog tonight.
