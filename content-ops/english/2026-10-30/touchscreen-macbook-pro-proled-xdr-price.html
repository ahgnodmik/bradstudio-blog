For years, the answer to "Will Apple ever put a touchscreen on a Mac?" was a polite no. On October 28, 2026, that answer changed.

Apple announced a redesigned MacBook Pro with what it calls a PROLED XDR display, a tandem OLED panel with the touch sensor built into the screen itself. It is the first Mac you can tap, swipe and pinch. It is also the most expensive MacBook Pro Apple has ever launched at its entry price. Here is what was announced, what it costs, and what is still unknown before it ships on November 6.

What Apple announced

The new MacBook Pro comes in 14-inch and 16-inch sizes. Both use an OLED panel instead of the mini-LED screens on recent MacBook Pros. Apple says the touch layer sits inside the display panel, so there is no extra glass sheet and, by its account, no added latency. CNBC, 9to5Mac, MacRumors and Tom's Guide all covered the launch on the same day and describe the same core design.

  • Display: tandem OLED, branded PROLED XDR, with built-in touch.
  • Chips: M5 Pro or M5 Max. Apple did not move this model to its newer M6 chips.
  • Glowing logo: the Apple logo on the lid lights up again. CNBC notes no Apple laptop has offered that since the 2017 MacBook Air, and a MacRumors hands-on found the logo's brightness follows the display brightness.
  • Release: pre-orders opened right after the announcement, and the laptops go on sale on Friday, November 6.

Touch is optional. CNBC's hands-on summed it up in its headline: touch is "totally optional." The trackpad and keyboard work exactly as before. Tom's Guide said touch felt smooth while moving between apps. Treat that as a first impression from a short demo, not a full review.

US prices at a glance

The headline number is $2,899 for the 14-inch model with M5 Pro. That figure appears in Apple-focused and general tech coverage alike, including The Mac Observer, CNN Underscored, MacRumors and Hardware Busters. Bloomberg had reported the day before the launch that Apple was weighing that price, and the final number matched.

Why the price is a record

No MacBook Pro has ever started higher than $2,899. The previous 14-inch M5 Pro model started at $2,499, so the touch version adds about $400 at the bottom of the range. MacRumors reported a similar $400 gap when you match chip, memory and storage between the old and new 16-inch models.

The bigger shock is at the top. Shacknews, IBTimes and MacRumors forum coverage all point to a maximum of $14,549 for the 16-inch model. That build has the M5 Max with an 18-core CPU and 40-core GPU, 128GB of unified memory, a 16TB SSD and nano-texture glass. The 14-inch equivalent is $14,249.

Where does that money go? Mostly memory and storage. Reports say moving from 48GB to 128GB of memory adds $2,000, while nano-texture glass adds just $150. The 16TB SSD tier is new, and it accounts for much of the jump. For context, 9to5Mac reported in June that the previous top 16-inch MacBook Pro already cost $10,149 with 128GB and an 8TB SSD. Configured with the same 8TB, the new 16-inch comes to $10,549.

IBTimes links the steep upgrade pricing to a memory supply crunch, and 9to5Mac had already flagged in June that Apple's memory and storage upgrades cost far more than a year earlier. That is a useful reminder: the touchscreen is the headline, but the upgrade boxes are where your bill grows fastest.

Touch model or the M6 model?

Apple released a 14-inch MacBook Pro with the M6 chip the same week. It keeps a mini-LED screen without touch and starts at $1,999. That is $900 less than the cheapest touch model, a gap CNN Underscored pointed out directly.

The choice comes down to how you work.

  • Pick the touch model if you want an OLED panel for photo or video work, or if you already reach for the screen on other laptops and tablets.
  • Pick the M6 model if you mostly type, code or browse, and the trackpad is fine for you. You get a newer chip generation for less money.
  • Wait for reviews if battery life matters. OLED panels can behave differently from mini-LED on battery life and long-term image retention, and neither has been tested in real-world reviews yet.

Practical buying tips

Before you click "Buy," a few habits can save hundreds of dollars.

  • Price the build you need, not the build you want. Run the configurator twice, once with your real needs and once with a "nice to have" spec, and compare the totals side by side.
  • Be honest about storage. An external SSD for archives usually costs far less than Apple's internal storage tiers. Keep internal storage for active projects.
  • Check your return window. Touch on a Mac is new. If you are unsure, buy from a seller with a clear return policy and test touch, battery and screen in your own routine during that window.
  • Watch for trade-in offers. If you are replacing an older MacBook, get a trade-in quote before you order.
  • Mind the ship date. Launch-week orders can slip. If you need the machine for a deadline, check the delivery estimate at checkout rather than assuming November 6.

What is still unconfirmed

  • Exact weight: early reports describe the new models as lighter, around three pounds for the smaller size, but we could not match a precise figure across two independent sources.
  • 14-inch M5 Max price: most coverage says $4,499, while one outlet lists $4,449. Check Apple's store.
  • Display brightness and contrast figures: we did not find these confirmed by two independent sources in our check, so we have left them out.
  • Battery life and OLED durability: these will only be clear once full reviews arrive after November 6.
  • Demand and shipping delays: no reliable data yet on pre-order backlogs.

If you are interested, open Apple's online store, build your exact configuration, and note the final price and delivery date. Then put it next to the $1,999 M6 model and ask whether touch and OLED are worth the difference for your work. If you are still unsure, wait a week after November 6 for the first full reviews. The laptop will still be there.
