If you are a university student in Korea, or the parent of one, the window that decides how much of next spring's tuition the government will cover opens on Tuesday 24 November 2026. The first round of applications for the National Scholarship for the 2027 spring semester runs until 7 January 2027, and this time the results will look different: the ten income brackets that students have quoted to each other for years are being folded into five, labelled with Korean letters instead of numbers. This explainer covers the dates, who should apply, how the new brackets map onto the old ones, what the money looked like this year, and the household consent step that trips up more applicants than anything else.

The dates that matter

The Korea Student Aid Foundation (KOSAF), the state agency that runs the scheme for the Ministry of Education, takes applications on its website and mobile app. Several university notices and news reports give the same schedule.

Two details are easy to miss. Both deadlines fall at 6 p.m., not midnight. And the student's own application closes a full week before the consent deadline, so a family that leaves everything to the second week of January can find the first door already shut. Applications are accepted around the clock during the window, including weekends and public holidays.

Who should apply in the first round

The scheme is for undergraduate students. That covers current students, incoming freshmen, students returning from a leave of absence, transfer students and those re-admitted to a university.

For current students, the first round is the default. Those who miss it can still be assessed in the second round, but reports and university notices say a returning student can use that late-application relief only twice during their time at university. Spending one of those two chances because of a missed online form is a poor trade.

For high school seniors who have just sat the national college entrance exam, the important point is that you do not need an acceptance letter to apply. Incoming students can file during the first round while admissions results are still pending, and several universities are telling their admitted and prospective students to do exactly that.

The household consent step

A student's bracket is set from the income and assets of the household, not just the student. KOSAF cannot look those up without permission. So after the student applies, the relevant family members must log in and give consent for their information to be checked. For an unmarried student that normally means the parents; for a married student it means the spouse.

Nothing happens until three boxes are ticked: the student's application, document verification and household consent. If a parent never completes the consent, the student's application simply sits there unassessed. That is why the most practical advice for this round is to apply and ask your parents to consent on the same evening, then check the status page a week later.

Most applicants will not be asked for paperwork. Some will, for example where family relationships need to be confirmed or where a multi-child benefit is being claimed. If a request appears on your status page, the documents are due by the same 14 January deadline. Students whose parents live abroad, who are estranged from a parent, or whose household has changed recently should contact KOSAF early; the helpline is 1599-2000 (in Korean).

Ten brackets become five

The structural change this year is in how results are labelled. From the 2027 spring semester, the student aid brackets used for the National Scholarship and related support move from a 1-to-10 scale to five bands named ga, na, da, ra and ma, the first five letters of the Korean alphabet in their traditional order. The separate categories for recipients of basic livelihood benefits and the near-poor stay as they are.

Look at the right-hand column and the logic becomes clear. Brackets 1, 2 and 3 already received the same annual amount, as did 4, 5 and 6. The reform gives one name to groups that were already paid the same. Korean education trade press, including Hankook Daehak Sinmun and Kyosu Sinmun, report that the support amounts themselves are not changing as part of this reform; only the number and names of the bands are.

The 2026 figures above are this year's amounts. KOSAF had not, as far as we could find, published confirmed per-band amounts for the 2027 academic year, and the 2027 budget is still going through the National Assembly. Treat the table as a guide, not a promise.

Why change it

Two reasons appear consistently in coverage. First, the old numbered brackets were constantly confused with the income deciles published by Korea's national statistics agency, which also run from 1 to 10 but measure something different. The deciles simply rank households by income. The student aid bracket is an administrative score that converts assets such as property, cars and savings into an income equivalent and adds them to monthly earnings, then compares the total with the government's standard median income.

Second, with ten narrow brackets, a small change in a family's finances could push a student up or down a step from one semester to the next, making it hard to plan. With wider bands, a move from old bracket 3 to old bracket 2 now happens inside band ga and makes no difference to the award. The boundaries between bands sit where the old ones did, so a student near the line between old brackets 3 and 4 is still near the line between ga and na.

The change was flagged in advance in the Ministry of Education's 2026 basic plan for the National Scholarship, confirmed in February. That plan set total student support for 2026 at 5.1161 trillion won, of which the National Scholarship accounted for 4.367 trillion won. It also widened the work-study programme to 165,000 students and allowed students who re-enter or transfer within the same university to keep receiving support up to a personal limit of eight semesters.

What else to check before you apply

  • Grades. Current students generally need to meet a minimum grade in the previous semester. The exact rule varies by student type and band, so check KOSAF's guidance for your case rather than relying on a single figure from a forum.
  • Multi-child support. In the 2026 academic year, a third or later child from a household in bracket 8 or below was eligible for full tuition. Under the new labels, brackets 1 to 8 correspond to bands ga, na and da. How the 2027 rules will be worded is unconfirmed.
  • Identity verification. You log in with a phone or certificate in your own name. If your phone contract is in a parent's name, sort out a personal certificate before the window opens.
  • Bank account. Make sure the account you enter is in your own name.
  • Work-study. Reports indicate the work-study application runs over the same period; check the details on the KOSAF site.

A simple plan for the next two weeks

  • Tuesday 24 November or soon after: submit the first-round application and screenshot the confirmation page.
  • The same day: send your parents or spouse a short message explaining how to give household consent, and stay on the phone while they do it if they are unfamiliar with the site.
  • About a week later: open the status page and confirm that application, documents and consent all show as complete.
  • Early December: if anything is unusual about your household, call KOSAF while the lines are quiet.
  • Before 7 January: if you are an incoming freshman, make sure your application is in even if your admission results are not.

What is still unknown

Three things had not been officially confirmed when we checked: the per-band support amounts for the 2027 academic year, the dates of the second application round, and when bracket results will be sent out. We will treat the 2026 amounts as the reference until KOSAF says otherwise.

What to do now

Find the bracket number on your last KOSAF notice and translate it into the new band using the table above, so the result you receive in the new year makes sense. Then put two alarms in your calendar: 24 November to apply and ask for household consent, and 5 January as a final check that all three steps show as complete, two days before the student deadline closes at 6 p.m. on 7 January.
