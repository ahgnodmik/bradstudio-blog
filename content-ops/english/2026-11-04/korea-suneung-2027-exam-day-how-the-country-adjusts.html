On Thursday, November 19, 2026, more than half a million people in South Korea will sit the same test on the same day, and much of the country will rearrange itself around them. Offices are asked to open late. Planes stop taking off and landing for 25 minutes. Police cars carry late students to their exam rooms. If you live in Korea, work with Korean colleagues, or are simply curious why a single exam gets this treatment, here is what Suneung is and what changes on the day this year.

This explainer is based on announcements by Korea's Ministry of Education and the Korea Institute for Curriculum and Evaluation (KICE), and on Korean and English-language press coverage as of November 4, 2026. Arrangements can change; the official notices take precedence.

What Suneung is

Suneung is the everyday Korean name for the College Scholastic Ability Test, usually shortened to CSAT in English. It is the national university entrance exam. KICE, a government-funded research institute, writes and administers it, while the Ministry of Education sets national exam policy and coordinates the government's support on the day.

Korean universities admit students through two broad tracks. One looks mainly at school records, essays and interviews, and the other relies heavily on CSAT scores. Even on the first track, many universities require a minimum CSAT grade. That is why a single day carries so much weight, and why parents, schools and the government treat it as an event for the whole society.

This sitting is for the 2027 academic year, meaning students who pass will start university in spring 2027. Korean media, including Etoday and Kyunghyang Shinmun, describe it as the last CSAT under the current elective-subject system before a revised format takes over.

How the country adjusts

The measures below were included in the government's support plan for this year's exam, which the Ministry of Education presented to the Cabinet on October 20, 2026, or have been reported as standing practice in multiple years.

Planes wait for the listening test

During the English listening section, aircraft and helicopter takeoffs and landings are halted nationwide so engine noise does not drown out the recording. This year the window runs for 25 minutes, from 1:10 p.m. to 1:35 p.m. Military training that makes noise, such as artillery fire and tank movement, is also paused. Flights are rescheduled around the window, so travelers flying that afternoon should check with their airline for revised times.

This is a long-standing tradition rather than a one-off. NPR described planes being held for the exam as far back as 2015, and AFP and other wire coverage has reported the flight pause in many years since.

Offices open later

The government asks public offices and private companies to push their start time back to 10 a.m. or later, so roads and trains stay clear for test takers in the morning rush. This is a request for cooperation, not a legal requirement. Many employers follow it, but your own workplace may not, so check with your employer.

More trains, fewer cars at the gate

Subway services in the Seoul metropolitan area are increased between 6 a.m. and 8:10 a.m. Emergency vehicles from police stations and other public agencies are positioned along routes to test sites. Vehicle access is restricted from 200 meters before each exam site, so students arriving by car are dropped off and walk the rest of the way.

Police cars as taxis

Every year Korean news carries stories of students who went to the wrong school or got stuck in traffic and were driven to their exam by police. This is standing practice. Yonhap reported that on the 2024 exam day, police vehicles transported test takers 154 times nationwide, and Seoul Economic Daily reported 134 patrol car transports on the 2025 exam day. For this year, regional police, including South Chungcheong Province's, have announced traffic management around test sites and emergency transport for students at risk of being late, along with checks to reduce construction noise during the listening section.

Horns, construction and quiet

Beyond the formal measures, there is a strong social expectation of quiet near test sites, especially during the listening section. Police in South Chungcheong Province, for example, said they would check construction and other noise sources in advance. If you live near a school that is being used as a test site, expect road restrictions in the morning and plan noisy chores for another day.

New this year: smart glasses are banned

KICE has added smart glasses to the list of devices that may not be brought into the exam. The move follows reports of smart glasses being used in university essay exams earlier this autumn. Korean media report that bringing in any electronic device, including phones and smart glasses, is treated as cheating. A test taker who is found wearing smart glasses during the exam has that year's results voided and loses eligibility to sit the following year's exam as well. Ordinary glasses are fine.

The usual rules still apply. Phones, smartwatches and similar devices must be handed in before the first period begins. Only an analog watch without communication functions is allowed. Test takers need their admission ticket and a physical photo ID.

What is unconfirmed

  • Stock market hours: Past coverage has described Korean markets opening an hour late on exam day. We did not find two sources confirming the arrangement for November 19, 2026, so check the Korea Exchange notice.
  • Preliminary orientation day: Test takers usually visit their site the day before to collect admission tickets and check their room. The exact date and times for this year should be checked with the local office of education.
  • Exact flight numbers: How many flights will be rescheduled this year had not been published at the time of writing.

If you live or work in Korea

Here is how the day is likely to touch you even if you are not taking the test:

  • Commuters: Expect your office to start late, and expect heavier traffic around schools between 7 and 8:10 a.m. If your company has not said anything, ask now.
  • Travelers: If you have a domestic or international flight around midday or early afternoon, check for a revised departure time.
  • Parents of school-age children: Schools used as test sites change their own schedules around the exam. Check your school's notice for that week.
  • Neighbors of test sites: Avoid parking or stopping near the school gate in the morning, and keep noise down in the early afternoon.

What to do now

If you are taking the exam, or helping someone who is, put the 8:10 a.m. entry deadline and the test site address on paper tonight, pack the admission ticket and a physical photo ID, and leave any smart glasses or smartwatch at home. If you are not, check your workplace start time and any flight on November 19 this week, so the day does not take you by surprise.
