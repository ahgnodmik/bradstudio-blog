If you worked in South Korea in 2025 on a modest income and never applied for the government's work incentive in May, you have until Tuesday, December 1, 2026, to claim it. The National Tax Service (NTS) has sent reminders to about 260,000 households that look eligible but have not applied. Applying late costs you 5% of the amount, and missing the deadline costs you all of it. Here is how Korea's version of the Earned Income Tax Credit works, who qualifies, and how to apply before the window closes.

This explainer is based on NTS announcements and Korean press coverage as of November 4, 2026. It is general information, not tax advice. Your actual eligibility and amount are decided by the NTS after review.

What the EITC is in Korea

Korea's Earned Income Tax Credit (geunro janggyeonggeum, often translated as the work incentive) pays cash to low-income households that earn money from work. The idea is to reward working rather than replace work: households need earnings from a job or a business to qualify, and the payment rises with earnings up to a point before tapering off. Alongside it sits the Child Tax Credit (janyeo janggyeonggeum), which supports lower- and middle-income families raising children under 18. Most people apply for both through the same process.

The program was written into Korean tax law in 2007, and the first payments were made in 2009. Korean policy researchers, including the Korea Institute for Health and Social Affairs, describe it as modeled on the US Earned Income Tax Credit. It was later extended from employees to self-employed people.

How it compares with the US EITC

The two programs share a name and a philosophy, but they are run differently.

The biggest practical difference for anyone who has lived in both countries: in Korea you have to apply. It is not paid automatically: eligible households receive a notice, and the money only arrives once someone applies.

The December 1 cutoff follows a standing rule: the late window runs for six months after the regular application period ends. After that, 2025 income can no longer be claimed at all. There is no later window for this year.

Why 10,000 households are hearing about it for the first time

The NTS said that, starting this year, it re-analyzed income data collected after the May notices and added about 10,000 households that now look likely to qualify. In other words, not getting a letter in May does not mean you were ineligible. The NTS has not detailed which additional income data it used.

Eligibility is based on what you earned in 2025, not on your situation today. Someone who has since quit, retired or stopped a part-time job can still qualify if their 2025 income and assets met the limits. Korean coverage has specifically flagged part-time workers and people who have left their jobs as groups that tend to miss out.

Who qualifies

The limits below apply to 2025 income and have been reported consistently by the NTS and Korean media.

Income limits

  • EITC, single-person households: total household income under 22 million won.
  • EITC, single-earner households: under 32 million won.
  • EITC, dual-earner households: under 44 million won.
  • Child Tax Credit: combined income of the couple under 70 million won, with at least one dependent child under 18.

Asset limits

  • The total value of assets owned by all household members, as of June 1, 2025, must be under 240 million won. That includes property, buildings, vehicles and deposits.
  • Debt is not subtracted. A household with a large home loan or rental deposit loan is assessed on its gross assets.
  • If total assets are between 170 million and 240 million won, only 50% of the calculated credit is paid.

For late filers, the 95% rule and the 50% asset reduction can both apply. The exact result depends on the NTS formula, so use the estimate shown on Hometax rather than doing the arithmetic yourself.

How much

The maximum EITC for 2025 income is 3.3 million won, for dual-earner households. The Child Tax Credit is reported as up to 1 million won per child. Because the credit rises with earnings and then tapers off, many households will receive less than the maximum.

How to apply

  • If you got a notice: Scan the QR code on the paper letter, or tap the "apply" button in the mobile notice. You can also apply through the automated phone line at 1544-9944.
  • If you did not get a notice: If you think you meet the requirements, apply directly on Hometax, the NTS website and mobile app. You may need to attach documents showing your 2025 income.
  • If online is not an option: Apply in writing at your local tax office. Lines can grow close to a deadline, so go early.

The NTS cautions that even households that received a notice may end up with a different amount, or nothing, once the financial assets of every household member are reviewed. Treat the figure in your notice as an estimate.

Watch for scams

Any deadline with money attached tends to attract phishing texts. The safe habit: do not tap links in unexpected texts. Open Hometax yourself, or call the number printed on an official letter.

A change coming, but not for this claim

The government's 2026 tax revision proposal would raise the maximum EITC for 2027 income to 1.8 million won for single households, 3.1 million won for single-earner households and 3.6 million won for dual-earner households, and loosen the income limits. As of this writing, we could not confirm that the National Assembly has passed it, so treat it as unconfirmed. It has nothing to do with the late application for 2025 income that closes on December 1.

What is unconfirmed

  • Final law for 2027 income: The higher limits and amounts above are a proposal until the National Assembly acts.
  • Data source for the new 10,000 notices: The NTS has not said which income records it added.
  • Your exact amount: Only the NTS review sets it. Payment on January 15, 2027, is the NTS's stated plan, not a guarantee for every application.

What to do this week

Check your post and your phone's messages for an NTS notice. If you have one, apply through the QR code or the in-app button today. If you do not, log in to Hometax and open the tax credit menu for 2025 income to see whether you meet the income and asset limits. If a parent, a relative who stopped working, or a friend new to Korean paperwork might qualify, sit down with them before December 1 and file together.
