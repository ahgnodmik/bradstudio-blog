# Korea's Youth Future Savings Account: What the "19.4% a Year" Really Means

If you are young and living in Korea, you have probably seen ads promising "up to 19.4% a year" on a savings account and wondered whether any bank really pays that.

No bank does. The figure is real, but it is not an interest rate. It describes the combined effect of a bank rate, a government cash top-up and a tax break. This explainer covers how the Youth Future Savings account (청년미래적금, cheongnyeon mirae jeokgeum) works, who can join, what happened in the second application round, and what is still unclear for foreign residents.

## What the account is

The Youth Future Savings account is a government-backed installment savings product for people aged 19 to 34. The Financial Services Commission (FSC) runs it with the Korea Inclusive Finance Agency. Banks sell it, but the government sets the rules.

The key features:

- Term: 3 years.
- Deposits: up to 500,000 won a month.
- Flexible payments: you choose how much to pay each month, up to the cap. Skipping a month does not close the account.
- Government matching contribution: 6% of what you deposit on the standard tier, 12% on the preferential tier.
- Tax: interest income is exempt from tax.
- Bank interest: a 5% base rate, fixed for 3 years, plus a preferential add-on of 2 to 3 percentage points depending on the bank. That puts the maximum bank rate at about 7% to 8% a year.

The bank add-on is not automatic. It depends on conditions such as salary transfers, card use or automatic transfers at that bank. Each bank sets its own list.

## What "19.4%" actually means

The FSC compared the account with an ordinary taxable savings account that pays simple interest. It asked a simple question: what rate would a normal account need to pay to leave you with the same money at the end of 3 years?

Its answer:

- Standard tier: equivalent to a simple-interest savings account paying about 13.2% to 14.4% a year.
- Preferential tier: equivalent to about 18.2% to 19.4% a year.

So 19.4% is the top of the range. It assumes the preferential tier, the 12% government match, the highest bank add-on and the full tax exemption. Most savers will land lower.

A worked example helps. Reports describe someone on the preferential tier at an 8% bank rate who pays in the full 500,000 won every month for 36 months. That is 18 million won of their own money. The government adds about 2.16 million won. Interest adds roughly 2.39 million won. The total at maturity is about 22.55 million won. That is a strong return. It is still a 3-year commitment with a 500,000-won monthly ceiling, not a 19.4% return on any sum you choose to deposit.

## Who can join

The basic rules, as reported across several outlets:

- Age: 19 to 34 on the day you sign up. Time spent in military service, up to 6 years, can be deducted from your age.
- Personal income: annual gross salary of 75 million won or less. Small business owners qualify with annual sales of 300 million won or less. Some guides also cite a comprehensive-income cap of 63 million won (unconfirmed; check the official notice).
- Household income: at or below 200% of the standard median income. Income from everyone registered in the same household counts.
- Exclusion: some guides say people taxed under comprehensive financial income taxation in the previous 3 years cannot join (unconfirmed; check the official notice).

The government match has its own, tighter thresholds:

- Standard tier (6% match): gross salary of 60 million won or less, or comprehensive income of 48 million won or less, or a small business with sales of 300 million won or less.
- Earners between 60 million and 75 million won can still join. They get the tax exemption but no government match.
- Preferential tier (12% match): employees of small and medium-sized enterprises earning 36 million won or less, small business owners with sales of 100 million won or less, and certain new hires at small and medium-sized enterprises. Some reports give a tighter household limit for this tier (150% of the median); treat that as unconfirmed.

In May 2026 the FSC eased the household test for married couples. For a household made up only of the applicant and a spouse, reports put the limits at 250% of the median for the standard tier and 200% for the preferential tier.

## The second round: dates and how it worked

The first round ran in June and July 2026 and drew about 2 million applicants or more, depending on the report. The second round followed in October.

- Applications: October 7 to 16, 2026, through bank and Korea Post mobile apps. The window closed at 6:30 p.m. on October 16.
- First two days: October 7 was for applicants whose birth year ends in an odd number. October 8 was for even numbers. From October 12 to 16, anyone eligible could apply.
- Birth-date range for this round: November 17, 1991 to November 27, 2007.
- Screening: October 19 to November 13.
- Account opening: approved applicants can open the account from November 16 to 27.

If you applied, you are now in the screening stage. Applying does not open the account. You still need to open it during the November window.

Reports put the number of participating institutions at 14 or 15. They include KB Kookmin Bank, Shinhan Bank, Hana Bank, Woori Bank, NH NongHyup Bank, Industrial Bank of Korea, KakaoBank and Korea Post. Check the current list with your bank before you plan around it.

## How it relates to the Youth Leap Account

The Youth Future Savings account replaces the Youth Leap Account (청년도약계좌, cheongnyeon doyak gyejwa), a 5-year product launched in 2023. You cannot hold both at once.

Existing Youth Leap Account holders were allowed to switch again in the second round. The order matters:

- Apply for Youth Future Savings and pass screening.
- Open the new account.
- Then request a special early termination of the Youth Leap Account for the purpose of switching.

Done in that order, you keep the government contributions and the tax-free interest already earned in the old account. Close the Youth Leap Account first and you lose those switching benefits. Switching is not always better, either. Depending on when you joined, how much you saved and your rate, staying put can pay more. Compare the projected payouts first.

## A change that may be coming

The government's draft 2027 budget would raise the preferential-tier match from 12% to 15%. Reports say workers at small and medium-sized enterprises outside the Seoul metropolitan area could get 25%. This is a proposal. The National Assembly still has to approve the budget, which is expected in early December. Whether a change would apply to existing account holders is not confirmed by more than one report.

## Can foreign residents join?

This is unconfirmed. We found no official notice or news report that states whether foreign nationals can open a Youth Future Savings account. The published rules talk about age, personal income, household income and tax status. None of the sources we checked mention nationality or residence status.

Two practical points follow from the published rules, though neither settles the question. The household test relies on Korean household registration data. The income tests rely on Korean tax records. A foreign resident without a Korean tax filing history or registered household may struggle to prove eligibility even if nationality is not a bar. Ask before you count on the account.

## What to check before you apply or open the account

- Your tier. Check your gross salary from last year and your employer's size. The difference between a 6% and a 12% match is large.
- Your household income. Find out who is registered in your household. Their income counts.
- The bank's add-on conditions. A bank advertising 8% may require a salary transfer or card spending you do not want.
- Your monthly budget. The cap is 500,000 won, but you do not need to hit it. Pick an amount you can keep paying for 36 months.
- Your Youth Leap Account. If you hold one, do not close it before your new account is open.
- Your residence status, if you are not a Korean national. Ask the bank or the Korea Inclusive Finance Agency directly whether you qualify.
- Your dates. If you applied in October, watch for your screening result and open the account between November 16 and 27.

If you missed this round, follow the FSC and Korea Inclusive Finance Agency announcements for the next one. Then run your own numbers before you sign up.
