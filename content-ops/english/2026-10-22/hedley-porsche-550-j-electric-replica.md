# Porsche 550 J: The 80% Electric Spyder That Is Neither a Toy Nor a Road Car

You have seen photos of a "mini Porsche" online and want to know whether it is a collector's toy, a real car, or something in between.

It is something in between. Hedley Studios, a small British workshop, has built the 550 J. It is an 80%-scale, fully electric version of the Porsche 550 Spyder, the 1950s race car. Porsche granted an official licence and opened its corporate archive for the project. Only 250 will be made. Each costs GBP 69,500 before local taxes. This guide explains what the car is, where the original came from, who should consider one, and what to check before you order.

## What the 550 J actually is

The 550 J is a real, driveable two-seater. It is not a scale model for a shelf. It is not a ride-on toy for children.

Here are the confirmed facts:

- Maker: Hedley Studios, Bicester, United Kingdom
- Licence: official Porsche licence, with access to the Porsche archive
- Occasion: 75 years of Porsche Motorsport
- Scale: about 80% of the original 550 Spyder
- Size: roughly 2.9 m long and 1.2 m wide
- Seats: two
- Production: 250 units, built by hand in Bicester
- Drivetrain: 48-volt electric system, about 8 kW (around 11 hp) as standard
- Price: GBP 69,500 plus local taxes, with orders open now

For scale, 2.9 m is shorter than most city cars. Two adults still fit side by side.

Porsche says the top speed is about 80 km/h (roughly 50 mph). Reports disagree on whether the standard car reaches that figure or only the upgraded version. Carscoops lists 40 mph for the base car and 50 mph with the upgrade.

The optional RS package raises output. The exact number is not settled. Porsche's English-language release and several English outlets give 16 kW. Porsche's Spanish-language release gives 12 kW. Ask Hedley Studios for the final figure in writing before you pay a deposit.

## Not road legal, by design

This is the single most important fact for buyers. The 550 J is not certified for public roads. Porsche and the press coverage describe it as a car for private land and closed circuits.

That shapes everything else. You cannot register it for your commute. You cannot drive it to a café. You need a private estate, a club circuit, or a track-day venue that accepts it.

The same rule applies in every country. A car that lacks road approval in the UK will not gain it simply by crossing a border. Local rules may be even stricter.

## The original 550 Spyder in brief

The 550 was Porsche's first purpose-built race car. Earlier Porsche racers were modified road cars. The 550 was designed for competition from the start.

The key dates are well documented:

- 1953: Porsche showed the 550 at the Paris Motor Show
- 1953: the car took its first race win at the Eifel race at the Nürburgring
- Production: Porsche built only about 90 examples of the original 550
- 1955: actor James Dean died in a crash in California while driving his 550 Spyder to a race
- 1956: Umberto Maglioli won the Targa Florio outright in a 550A, a later development

The 550 was small, light, and mid-engined. It often beat larger cars in its class. That giant-killer reputation is a big part of its appeal today. Original cars now sell for millions at auction. Bonhams sold a 1956 550 RS Spyder for about GBP 4.6 million in 2016.

That price gap explains the 550 J. It offers the shape and the story of a car that almost nobody can own.

## Who makes it

Hedley Studios used to be called The Little Car Company. It renamed itself in August 2024 after its founder, Ben Hedley. The firm is based at Bicester Motion, a heritage motoring site in Oxfordshire.

The company has built licensed scale cars before. Its range includes junior versions of the Aston Martin DB5, the Bentley Blower, the Ferrari Testa Rossa, and the Bugatti Type 35. The 550 J is its first project with Porsche.

## How it compares with the Bugatti Baby II

The best-known earlier car from the same workshop is the Bugatti Baby II. It makes a useful reference point.

- Scale: Baby II is 75% of a Bugatti Type 35; the 550 J is 80% of a 550 Spyder
- Run: Baby II was limited to 500 units; the 550 J to 250
- Occasion: Baby II marked Bugatti's 110th anniversary; the 550 J marks 75 years of Porsche Motorsport
- Price at launch: Baby II started at about EUR 30,000, with upper trims above EUR 43,500; the 550 J starts at GBP 69,500

The Baby II also has a story behind it. Ettore Bugatti built a half-scale Type 35 for his son in 1926. That car became the original "Bugatti Baby." The 550 J has no such history. It is a modern tribute rather than a revival.

The 550 J sits higher in price and lower in volume. It also seats two. That makes it closer to a small track car than a children's heirloom.

## Who it is for

The 550 J suits a narrow group of buyers:

- Porsche collectors who want a 550 shape they can actually drive
- Owners of private estates or land with space for a short circuit
- Members of clubs or venues that run closed-course days for small electric cars
- Families who want something adults and older children can share on private ground

It does not suit anyone who needs road transport. It is also a poor fit for buyers who expect strong resale growth. A run of 250 is small, but the price already reflects that.

## Delivery timing and what is still unconfirmed

Several reports say first deliveries are planned for 2027. Some give a spring 2027 window. Porsche's own material does not confirm a delivery date, so treat 2027 as reported, not promised.

Battery capacity and driving range are also unsettled. One outlet published range figures. No second independent source matched them. Ask the maker directly.

## What to check before you order

If you are serious, work through this list first:

- Where you can drive it: confirm you have legal access to private land or a circuit that allows it. Check insurance for that use.
- Import rules: if you live outside the UK, ask a customs broker how your country classifies a non-road electric vehicle. Duty, VAT, and paperwork vary widely.
- Final specifications: get the RS output, top speed, battery size, and range in writing.
- Delivery date: ask for a target month and what happens to your deposit if it slips.
- Servicing: ask who maintains the car in your region. A handmade car from a single UK workshop may need to travel back for major work.
- Charging: confirm the charger type and the plug standard for your country.
- Transport: plan how you will move it to a track. You will likely need a trailer or van.

Start by emailing Hedley Studios with your country and intended use, and ask them to answer every item on this list before you sign.
