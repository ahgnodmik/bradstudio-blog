Winter in Korea is cold enough that heating is not optional. For a low-income household the gas, electricity or kerosene bill for the coldest months can take a serious bite out of a tight budget. The Korean government's main tool for this problem is the Energy Voucher program, and November is when it starts doing most of its work.

This explainer covers how the program works in the 2026 cycle, who qualifies, how much money is involved, how households actually spend it, and what is still unclear, including whether foreign residents can take part.

Who runs it

The Energy Voucher program (에너지바우처) is a central government subsidy that pays part of the energy costs of vulnerable households. The ministry in charge has changed recently. The program used to sit under the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy, but the energy policy functions moved to the newly reorganized Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment. The government welfare portal Bokjiro describes the program as led by the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy (now the Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment), and English-language coverage from Herald Business and Nocut News names the Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment as the ministry announcing the 2026 round.

Day-to-day operations are handled by the Korea Energy Agency, a public agency that runs the program's website and hotline. Applications themselves go through local community service centers (the neighborhood offices Koreans call 행정복지센터) or the Bokjiro online portal.

How much households receive in 2026

The voucher is a fixed annual amount based on household size. Financial News, the Kyunghyang Shinmun, Newsis and Newspim all reported the same figures for 2026, and Bokjiro's notice gives the same range.

1 person295,200 wonAbout $211

These are totals for the whole 2026 program year, not monthly payments. The dollar column uses a round exchange rate purely so non-Korean readers can gauge the scale; actual rates move every day.

The money is not paid in cash. It arrives either as a deduction on utility bills or as credit on a payment card, as described below.

Who qualifies

A household has to pass two tests at the same time.

The income test. The household must receive benefits under the National Basic Living Security Act, Korea's core public assistance system. That covers the livelihood, medical, housing and education benefits. A low income on its own is not enough if the household is not enrolled in that system.

The household member test. At least one person in the household must belong to one of these groups:

  • Older adults aged 65 and over
  • Young children below school age (sources describe the cutoff slightly differently, so check the exact birth-date rule locally)
  • People with registered disabilities
  • Pregnant women
  • People with severe, rare or intractable illnesses
  • Single-parent families and child-headed households, including children in foster care
  • Households with two or more children under 19

The multi-child category is relatively new. Korea's policy news service and the Kyunghyang Shinmun reported in November 2025 that households on basic livelihood benefits with two or more children were added to the program.

When to apply and when the money can be spent

Applications for the 2026 round opened on June 15, 2026 and close on December 31, 2026. That leaves two months for anyone who has not yet applied. Bokjiro's notice says households that received the voucher in the previous year and have had no change in eligibility are registered automatically, but anyone who has moved or whose household has changed should apply again rather than rely on that.

SummerJuly 1 to September 30, 2026Deducted from electricity bills only

Application deadlineDecember 31, 2026Community service center or Bokjiro

A change for 2026 matters for the winter. Financial News and Herald Business reported that the separate summer and winter spending limits were dropped, so households can use their annual amount freely across the whole period. A household that used none of its voucher over the summer can spend the full amount on heating. A household that used it all on summer cooling does not get a second winter payment.

The deadline at the end of the period is firm. The official voucher website says only bills issued within the use period are covered, so any balance left after May 31, 2027 is lost.

Bill deduction or card: choosing the right method

Households living in apartments with city gas or district heating can choose to have the voucher taken straight off their monthly bills. Nothing needs to be done at the checkout.

Many older homes, especially in rural areas, heat with kerosene boilers, LPG or coal briquettes. Those fuels are bought from a dealer, not billed by a utility, so bill deduction does not work. For the winter period, those households can choose to receive the voucher on a National Happiness Card (국민행복카드), a government-linked payment card, and use it to buy kerosene, LPG or briquettes directly. Multiple Korean outlets and the Korea Energy Agency's guidance describe this option.

New for this winter

Two additions in 2026 target households that the old design missed.

Coal briquette conversion support. A new voucher aims to help households that heat with coal briquettes move to another fuel. Energy News and Electric Times both reported its introduction. The exact amount and conditions were described differently across sources, so they are unconfirmed here.

Advance exception payment. Some people pay rent that already includes utilities. Residents of gosiwon (small single-room lodgings) and similar housing never get a bill in their own name, so they had no practical way to use the voucher. The new advance exception payment is aimed at them. Energy News, Electric Times and English coverage in Herald Business describe a payment for households that cannot use the voucher through bills and have not used it by the end of October. Per-household amounts and how applications after October are handled were not confirmed by two sources, so check with the local office.

Reports based on official guidance also say households that already received certain other winter fuel help, such as emergency welfare winter fuel support or coal coupons, cannot receive the winter voucher on top of it. If your household gets any other heating aid, ask the community service center how the two interact.

What about foreign residents?

This is the question many expat readers will have, and the honest answer is that it is unconfirmed. None of the official notices or news reports reviewed for this article stated, in two or more independent sources, whether foreign nationals living in Korea can receive the Energy Voucher.

What is clear is that the voucher's income test depends on receiving benefits under the National Basic Living Security Act. Several Korean sources describe a limited exception in that law, Article 5-2, that allows some foreign nationals, such as marriage migrants raising children who are Korean citizens, to be covered by parts of the basic livelihood system. Whether that automatically carries through to the Energy Voucher is not something the sources state directly.

If you are a foreign resident and already receive any basic livelihood benefit, ask your local community service center directly whether your household can apply for the voucher. Bring your residence card and any documents showing your benefit status. If your Korean is limited, bring a Korean-speaking friend or ask in advance whether the office can arrange interpretation, since eligibility questions often turn on small details in your documents.

Gaps and open questions

The program does not reach everyone it is meant for. During the October 2026 parliamentary audit, Newspim reported that about 610,000 of roughly 2.01 million basic livelihood recipients did not receive the voucher, largely because the household member test screens them out. That figure came from a single outlet and is unconfirmed, but the structural point stands: being on basic livelihood benefits is necessary, not sufficient.

The size of the total budget is also reported inconsistently. One English-language report put the 2026 budget at 494 billion won, while Bokjiro's summary gives 517.2 billion won. The exact budget is unconfirmed.

Still unconfirmed

  • Whether foreign residents are eligible for the Energy Voucher.
  • Per-household amounts for the advance exception payment and handling of requests made after October.
  • Amounts and conditions for the coal briquette conversion voucher.
  • The exact age cutoff for young children.
  • The 2026 program budget (494 billion won vs. 517.2 billion won in different sources).
  • The figure of about 610,000 eligible-income recipients who did not receive the voucher.
  • The 2027 program's amounts and schedule, which have not been announced.

What to do this week

If you or a family member might qualify, call the Energy Voucher hotline at 1600-3190 or check the Korea Energy Agency's voucher website to see whether a voucher is already registered and how much balance is left. If nothing is registered and the household receives basic livelihood benefits, take an ID card to the local community service center and apply before the December 31 deadline, ideally by mid-December when offices are less crowded. If the home heats with kerosene or LPG, ask at the same visit to receive the voucher on a National Happiness Card so it can be used at a fuel dealer.
