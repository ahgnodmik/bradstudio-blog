Anthropic, the San Francisco company behind the Claude family of AI models, has filed publicly to become a listed company. Its registration statement on Form S-1 went up on the SEC's EDGAR system on Monday, October 26, 2026. The company plans to list Class A common stock on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker ANT.

The filing is the first time the public has seen Anthropic's audited financials. Two numbers jumped out in nearly every headline: about $16.3 billion of revenue in the first half of 2026, and a net loss of about $80.7 billion over the same six months. Both are real. They also describe very different things, and reading one without the other gives a distorted picture.

This article summarizes public filings and press reports. It is not investment advice and is not a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold any security.

What was actually filed

An S-1 is the standard document a U.S. company files when it wants to sell shares to the public for the first time. It contains a description of the business, audited financial statements, a long list of risk factors, and details about who controls the company. It does not, by itself, mean shares will start trading on a particular day.

Several things in Anthropic's version are settled and were reported the same way by Yahoo Finance, CNBC, The Next Web and Korean outlets including Seoul Economic Daily:

  • Exchange and ticker: Nasdaq Global Select Market, symbol ANT.
  • Security offered: Class A common stock.
  • Lead underwriters: Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley.
  • Corporate form: Anthropic is a public benefit corporation, registered as Anthropic, PBC.

Several things are not settled. The number of shares on offer and the expected price range are blank in the current filing. Companies usually fill those in with a later amendment, shortly before the roadshow where bankers pitch the deal to institutional investors. Until that amendment appears, any figure for the offering size or the company's market value at listing is an estimate made by someone outside the company.

Revenue: the growth story

The top line is what most investors will focus on. According to the filing as reported by Yahoo Finance, Crypto Briefing and JoongAng Economy News, Anthropic booked about $16.3 billion of revenue in the six months to June 30, 2026. A year earlier, the same period brought in about $1.2 billion. That is roughly a fourteenfold increase.

For a full-year comparison, reports citing the filing put 2025 revenue at about $4.6 billion. In other words, the first half of 2026 alone came to more than three times what the company earned in all of 2025.

Revenue that grows this quickly raises an obvious follow-up question: how much of it comes from a small number of large customers? The filing does address customer concentration, but outlets have reported the figure differently. One account says roughly a fifth of first-half revenue came from two unnamed customers. Another, carried by Bernama, puts the share at about 23 percent. Because the reported numbers do not match, this article treats the exact concentration figure as unconfirmed.

The $80.7 billion loss, explained

A net loss five times larger than revenue sounds like a company in trouble. The details in the filing tell a more specific story.

Of the roughly $80.7 billion first-half net loss, about $72.4 billion is a non-cash accounting charge. It comes from re-measuring the fair value of convertible notes, derivative liabilities and SAFEs (simple agreements for future equity, a common startup funding contract). These instruments convert into shares later. When the company's valuation rises, the value of what those holders are owed rises too, and accounting rules require that increase to be recorded as a loss on the income statement.

The filing on EDGAR describes the change as primarily driven by an increase in the fair value of outstanding convertible notes resulting from a higher enterprise valuation. The Next Web, HackerNoon and the Korean startup outlet Wowtale all reported the same $72.4 billion figure. Put simply, the company's value went up so sharply that the paper cost of its earlier financing contracts went up with it. No cash left the building for that line.

The number closer to day-to-day business performance is the operating result. For the first six months of 2026, Anthropic reported an operating loss of about $693 million, according to the summary financial table in the S-1. Reports put the comparable figure a year earlier at around $2.1 billion. Several outlets also reported that the second quarter on its own produced an operating profit of about $974 million, which would be the company's first operating profit as a standalone quarter.

None of this means the loss is meaningless. Those convertible instruments will still turn into shares, which affects how much of the company each future shareholder owns. And the S-1 itself lists the possibility that the company may not achieve or sustain profitability among its risk factors, as filings of this kind almost always do.

Who controls the company

Anthropic's governance is unusual for a company heading to the public market. In 2023 it set up the Long-Term Benefit Trust, a Delaware purpose trust whose stated mission is tied to the responsible development of advanced AI. The trust holds a special class of stock, Class T common stock, which gives it a role in electing directors. Both the S-1 on EDGAR and a 2023 post on the Harvard Law School Forum on Corporate Governance describe this structure.

For prospective shareholders, the practical point is that buying Class A stock would not come with the same degree of influence over the board that shareholders in a conventional company might expect. Commentators have noted that the S-1's risk factors acknowledge the trust's interests may differ from those of Class A holders. Exact voting percentages for founders and the trust have been reported in secondary coverage, but this article has not been able to confirm them against two independent sources.

Valuation: what is known and what is guessed

The last valuation the company has confirmed came from a funding round in May 2026. Bloomberg Law and other outlets reported that Anthropic raised money at a $965 billion post-money valuation, which at the time was above OpenAI's last disclosed private valuation.

Reports since then have floated a public-market valuation somewhere between $1.5 trillion and $2 trillion, with some pieces narrowing that to $1.8 trillion to $2 trillion. These numbers come from anonymous people described as familiar with the plans. Anthropic has not published a price range, so the listing valuation is unconfirmed. Claims that the deal will be the largest IPO on record are also unconfirmed, because nobody can rank an offering before its size is fixed.

When could shares start trading?

The date has moved several times in press coverage. Earlier Reuters reporting pointed to marketing in mid-October and a listing shortly before the U.S. midterm elections in early November. More recent reports, citing Bloomberg, describe a roadshow starting the week of November 9 and trading possibly beginning before Thanksgiving on November 26. Anthropic has not announced a listing date, so the timing is unconfirmed.

Retail investors who want to follow along should know the usual sequence. An amended S-1 with a price range comes first. The roadshow follows. The SEC then declares the registration effective, the underwriters set the final price, and shares begin trading, usually the next morning. Watching for that amendment is the most reliable signal that a listing is close.

What remains unconfirmed

  • Listing date: reported as mid-to-late November; not announced by the company.
  • Offering price and number of shares: blank in the current filing.
  • Valuation at listing: reported estimates of $1.5 trillion to $2 trillion are unconfirmed.
  • Offering size and any "record IPO" label: unconfirmed until the price is set.
  • Customer concentration: reported as about 20 percent and about 23 percent of H1 revenue from two customers; exact figure unconfirmed.
  • Third-quarter revenue: one report cited a figure, while other outlets said the filing does not include it; unconfirmed.
  • 2025 net loss and syndicate size: different numbers circulated in coverage; unconfirmed.
  • Founder and trust voting percentages: reported in secondary sources only; unconfirmed.

How to read the filing yourself

The S-1 is public and free to read. Search for "Anthropic, PBC" on SEC EDGAR and open the registration statement. Three sections are worth the time:

  • Summary consolidated financial data, near the front, where operating loss and net loss sit a few lines apart. Comparing them is the quickest way to see how much of the headline loss is accounting rather than cash.
  • Risk factors, which run long but list every major concern the company and its lawyers chose to disclose, including governance and competition.
  • Description of capital stock, which explains the different share classes and the role of the Long-Term Benefit Trust.

If you are considering any investment around this listing, wait for the amended filing with a price range, read the risk factors in full, and talk to a licensed financial professional who knows your situation. Your next practical step is simple: set an EDGAR alert or check the company's filing page for an S-1/A, because that amendment will answer the price, size and timing questions that no news report can settle today.
