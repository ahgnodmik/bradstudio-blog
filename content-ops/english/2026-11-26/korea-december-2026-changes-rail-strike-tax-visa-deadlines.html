December in Korea brings a planned rail strike, a property tax deadline, a small change to labour law and two travel rules that expire on 31 December. If you live in Korea, or have family and friends planning to visit, missing one of these can cost you a train seat, a late-payment charge or a wasted trip to the airport. This explainer covers what changes in Korea in December 2026, the dates to put in your calendar, and what is still unconfirmed.

Rail: work-to-rule now, a strike planned for 10 December

The National Railway Workers' Union has called an indefinite general strike from 9 am on Thursday 10 December 2026, according to Hankook Ilbo, News1, Etoday and Kyongbuk Ilbo. The union announced the date on 11 November. It is asking for more safety staff and changes to the promotion system.

Before the strike date, the union has started what it calls a safe-operation campaign. From 9 am on Thursday 26 November to 4 December, crews follow every work rule and operating regulation to the letter. Seoul Shinmun, Weekly Hankook and a Yonhap report carried by Nate all said this would cause train delays. Korail has set up an emergency transport headquarters, and Korean reports said it is waiving refund and change fees on tickets affected by the action. Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that people with time-critical trips were advised to consider other transport.

Will the strike go ahead? Nobody knows yet. Korean reports said talks had broken down after a third round and no new date had been set. Treat 10 December as a real risk, not a certainty.

Seoul subway: strike avoided

One piece of good news. The Seoul Metro unions, which run Lines 1 to 8, had threatened a strike from the first train on 25 November. Management and the unions reached a deal in the early hours of 25 November after about 18 hours of talks, and trains ran normally, according to iNews24 and several Yonhap reports. The deal includes about 500 new hires and a pay rise of up to 3.5%. That removes one source of December disruption, though Korail-run sections of Seoul's network, such as parts of Lines 1, 3 and 4, would still be hit by a rail strike.

Comprehensive Real Estate Holding Tax: pay by 15 December

The Comprehensive Real Estate Holding Tax is a national tax on property whose combined official value passes a set threshold. Under the law, the payment period runs from 1 to 15 December. 15 December 2026 is a Tuesday, so there is no weekend extension this year. In 2025, the National Tax Service sent bills in the last week of November with the same 15 December deadline, SBS Biz reported.

Foreign residents who own property in Korea are taxed on the same basis as Korean nationals. You can check and pay through Hometax. If your bill is large, instalment payment is available above a threshold. Korean guides give the threshold as 2.5 million or 3 million won depending on how it is counted, so check your own notice. The 2026 National Tax Service announcement on bills had not been confirmed in two sources when we wrote this.

Labour law: four-hour shifts can skip the break from 10 December

Under Korea's Labor Standards Act, a worker on a four-hour shift must get at least 30 minutes' break. From 10 December 2026, a worker whose working day is exactly four hours can ask to waive that break and leave 30 minutes earlier, according to Law Times, Tax and Finance Media and a labour column in the Dental News. The waiver only applies if the worker asks for it. Without a request, the employer must still give the break.

This matters most for part-time staff in cafés, shops and clinics, including students on part-time work permits. A separate change, allowing annual leave to be taken in hourly blocks, takes effect later, on 10 June 2027, Law Times and SBS Biz reported. It does not start in December.

Travel rules that end on 31 December

K-ETA exemption for 22 countries

Visitors from 22 visa-waiver countries, including the United States, Japan, Canada, Australia and Germany, have not needed a Korea Electronic Travel Authorization (K-ETA) since a temporary exemption began in April 2023. The current extension runs to 31 December 2026, according to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, EY and Fragomen. Travellers can still apply voluntarily, and doing so means they skip the paper arrival card, but the fee applies.

Whether the exemption will be extended into 2027 has not been announced in the sources we found. If someone is flying to Korea in early January, they should check the official K-ETA website a week before departure.

China visa-free entry for Korean passport holders

China lets ordinary Korean passport holders enter without a visa for up to 30 days for tourism, business, family visits and transit. In November 2025 China extended that policy to 31 December 2026, The Asia Business Daily and other outlets reported. We did not find any announcement of a further extension. Korean nationals planning a trip to China for New Year or early 2027 should confirm the rule before booking non-refundable travel. Expats of other nationalities should check China's rules for their own passport, which may differ.

Still unconfirmed

  • Whether the 10 December rail strike will go ahead: depends on talks.
  • Train operating rates during a strike: Korail figures in earlier reports differ, so we do not quote one.
  • 2027 extensions of the K-ETA exemption and China's visa-free policy: not announced in sources we found.
  • The 2026 National Tax Service notice on Comprehensive Real Estate Holding Tax bills: not confirmed in two sources.

What to do now

If you have a train trip booked between now and mid-December, check the Korail app the evening before and allow extra time on 26 November to 4 December. If you must travel on or after 10 December, book a bus or flight backup you can cancel free of charge. If you own property, log in to Hometax in the first week of December and pay by Tuesday 15 December. If you run a café or shop with four-hour shifts, decide by 10 December how staff will request a break waiver in writing. And if friends or family are visiting from a K-ETA-exempt country in January, ask them to check the K-ETA website before they fly.
