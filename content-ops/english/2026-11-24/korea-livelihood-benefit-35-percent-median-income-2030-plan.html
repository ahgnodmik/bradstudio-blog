South Korea has a guaranteed minimum income, but whether a household qualifies comes down to a single percentage that most people have never heard of. On 23 November 2026 the Ministry of Health and Welfare said it will raise that percentage for the country's main cash benefit, drop a long-criticised family support rule for households with a person with a severe disability, and pay emergency cash faster to families who suddenly lose their income. This explainer walks through what was announced, how the system actually calculates who gets money, and what is still only "under review."

What was announced

The ministry released the 4th Comprehensive Plan for Basic Livelihood Security, covering 2027 to 2029, after the 81st meeting of the Central Livelihood Security Committee approved it. Korea's National Basic Livelihood Security Act requires a new plan every three years, and each plan sets the direction for the four main benefits: livelihood (cash), medical, housing and education.

The headline change concerns the livelihood benefit, the monthly cash payment. Its eligibility line is currently set at 32% of the "standard median income." Under the new plan it stays at 32% in 2027, then rises one percentage point a year to 33% in 2028, 34% in 2029 and 35% in 2030. Kyunghyang Shinmun, News1, the Financial News, Segye Ilbo and Digital Times all reported the same schedule.

What "standard median income" means

The standard median income is a reference figure the government sets each year to anchor welfare eligibility. It is based on the middle of the national household income distribution, adjusted by household size. The Central Livelihood Security Committee decides the next year's figure each summer.

For 2027 the committee set it on 28 July 2026. A four-person household's standard median income is 6,929,885 won a month, up 6.70% from 2026. The ministry and the government's Korea.kr policy news service described this as the largest increase since the current system began in 2015. Kyunghyang Shinmun's English edition reported the same figures, putting the one-person figure at about 2.73 million won.

Each benefit uses a different share of that median, which is why a household can miss out on cash support but still qualify for help with rent or school costs.

For a single person, the 2027 livelihood line is 875,533 won a month. For comparison, the 2026 four-person line was 2,078,316 won, so the 2027 line is roughly 139,000 won higher even though the percentage did not move. That increase comes entirely from the larger median.

How the cash benefit is calculated

The eligibility line is not a flat payment. Korea's livelihood benefit is a top-up: it pays the gap between the line and what the government counts as the household's income. That counted figure is called the "recognised income" (sometimes translated as income-recognition amount).

Recognised income has two parts:

  • Assessed income: wages, business income and other earnings, minus set deductions. Part of earned income is disregarded so that taking a job does not wipe out the benefit one-for-one.
  • Income converted from assets: housing, savings and cars, minus a basic allowance, multiplied by conversion rates that turn the asset value into a notional monthly income.

Take a single person in 2027 with a recognised income of 300,000 won a month. The benefit would be about 875,533 minus 300,000, or roughly 575,000 won. Someone with zero recognised income would receive the full 875,533 won. This simple example ignores other rules, and real assessments can differ.

The top-up design explains why raising the percentage matters twice. Households just above the line become eligible, and households already receiving the benefit get a larger top-up. At the 2027 median, one percentage point for a four-person household is worth about 69,000 won a month, and the three-point gap between 32% and 35% is about 208,000 won. The actual 2030 amounts will depend on the medians set in those years.

The family support rule and severe disability

Korea's welfare system has long applied a "family support obligation" rule: the income and assets of parents or adult children could disqualify a low-income person, even if that family gave no help. Korea.kr and the ministry announced in 2021 that this rule had been largely abolished for the livelihood benefit. An exception stayed in place for relatives with high income or large assets, and the medical benefit still applies its own version of the rule.

Under the new plan, from the second half of 2027, households that include a person with a severe disability will no longer be subject to that remaining exception for either the livelihood or the medical benefit. The ministry estimates that about 20,000 households previously shut out by the family rule will become eligible. Kyunghyang Shinmun, Etoday and News1 reported the same timing and estimate. The exact start date in the second half has not been announced.

Reports also say the ministry will review phasing out the family rule for households of people aged 75 and over by 2030. That is a review item, not a decision.

"Priority Dream": 300,000 won before the paperwork clears

The plan adds a fast-track for households in sudden crisis, such as job loss or a business closure. Officials at the local community service centre can check the household's situation on site and pay 300,000 won of the emergency livelihood support before the city or district formally decides the case. The ministry says the current process takes about three days on average from application to decision. The scheme, nicknamed "Useon Deurim" (roughly "Priority Dream"), is due to start in 2027.

Two points are easy to miss. The 300,000 won is an advance on emergency support the household would receive anyway, not an extra payment on top. The detailed eligibility criteria and on-site judgement rules will come in later guidance, so they are unconfirmed for now. The Financial News and Segye Ilbo both carried these details.

The criticism

The 35% target is not new. The 3rd plan, covering 2024 to 2026, also aimed for 35%, as Kyunghyang Shinmun reported in 2023. The new plan pushes the deadline to 2030. The civic group People's Solidarity for Participatory Democracy said in a statement that the government had postponed its promise by four years and filled the plan with items that are only "under review." Supporters of the stepwise approach point to budget pressure; the ministry's own cost estimate should be checked in the official release before it is quoted.

What this means if you live in Korea

  • Already receiving the livelihood benefit: compare your January 2027 payment with December's. If your recognised income has not changed, the higher line should mean a larger top-up.
  • Turned down in the past: rerun the numbers with the 2027 table. The online Bokjiro portal has a benefit simulator, and local community service centres take applications.
  • Households with a person with a severe disability who were refused because of a relative's income: keep your refusal notice and watch for the start date in the second half of 2027.
  • Sudden loss of income: ask about emergency welfare support first. The Ministry of Health and Welfare call centre is 129.

What to do this week

If you or a family member is near the line, gather three things: household size, monthly earnings, and a rough list of assets including any car. Enter them in the Bokjiro simulator using the 2027 figures above, then book a visit to your local community service centre in early January with the printout.
