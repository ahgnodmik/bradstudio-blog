If you plan to fly out of Korea this winter, the date you buy your ticket could cost or save you tens of thousands of won.

Korea's two full-service carriers, Korean Air and Asiana Airlines, are raising fuel surcharges on international tickets issued from Nov. 1, 2026. The surcharge level moves from stage 23 to stage 27. This is the third monthly increase in a row. The surcharge is fixed on the day the ticket is issued, not the day you fly. A ticket issued by Oct. 31 keeps the October rate, even if you travel in December or next spring. This guide explains the new numbers, how Korea's surcharge system works, and what to check before the deadline.

What changes on Nov. 1

Korean Air and Asiana Airlines announced their November surcharges on Oct. 16. The new rates apply to international tickets issued in Korea from Nov. 1 to Nov. 30. Domestic flights go up as well.

The biggest jump hits North America. Korean Air's New York surcharge rises from KRW 362,600 to KRW 408,100 one way. That is KRW 45,500 more per leg, or about KRW 91,000 more per round trip. Europe goes from KRW 322,000 to KRW 370,100 one way.

The surcharge sits on top of the base fare and airport taxes. A family of four flying Korean Air to New York and back pays KRW 3,264,800 in fuel surcharges alone at the November rate. At the October rate, the same family pays KRW 2,900,800. The gap is KRW 364,000.

Asiana Airlines uses its own distance bands, so the same stage gives different numbers. Its Bangkok surcharge is reported at KRW 178,900 one way in November. Its long-haul ceiling for the Americas and Europe is KRW 351,100.

How Korea's fuel surcharge system works

The benchmark: Singapore jet fuel

Korean airlines tie international surcharges to the average spot price of jet fuel in Singapore, known as MOPS (Mean of Platts Singapore). Each month, airlines take the average from the 16th of the month before last to the 15th of the previous month. That number decides next month's stage.

For November, the window ran from Sept. 16 to Oct. 15. The average was USD 173.89 per barrel. That is 9.7% higher than the USD 158.57 average in the previous window.

The stage table

No international surcharge applies when the average is below 150 U.S. cents per gallon. At 150 cents, stage 1 starts. Each additional 10 cents adds one stage. The table tops out at stage 33, which covers 470 cents per gallon and above. There is no stage higher than 33.

A barrel holds 42 gallons. USD 173.89 per barrel works out to about 414 cents per gallon. That lands in stage 27, which matches the announced level. (This conversion is our own arithmetic, shown to explain the table.)

Earlier in 2026, jet fuel prices spiked. Korean reports said stage 33 applied for the first time since the current system began in 2016. Prices then eased before climbing again from September.

Distance-based pricing and government approval

Korea switched to distance-based international surcharges in 2016. Before that, airlines charged one flat amount per region. That meant some nearer destinations paid as much as farther ones. Under the new system, each airline sets its own distance bands. The Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport (MOLIT, Gukto Gyotongbu) approves the scheme. At launch, Korean Air used 10 bands, from under 500 miles to 10,000 miles and above. Asiana Airlines used nine bands. Current band counts may differ; check each airline's notice.

Domestic flights use a different rule

Domestic surcharges follow a separate formula. They use the Singapore jet fuel average for the full previous calendar month. They apply only when that average reaches 120 cents per gallon or more.

Why the ticketing date matters

The surcharge is set on the day the ticket is issued. Your travel date does not change it. Korean Air states that it does not collect extra money if the surcharge rises after you buy. It also does not refund the difference if the surcharge falls.

So a ticket issued on Oct. 31 for a Dec. 20 flight carries the October rate. A ticket issued on Nov. 1 for the same flight carries the November rate.

What to check before Oct. 31

  • Make sure the ticket is issued, not just reserved. A held booking without payment is not a ticket. Look for an e-ticket number after you pay.
  • Mind the weekend. Oct. 31, 2026 falls on a Saturday. If a travel agency issues your ticket, ask when they can process it. Do not assume weekend handling.
  • Compare refund rules with the savings. A cheap promotional fare may carry high cancellation fees. If your plans are not fixed, a penalty could cost more than the surcharge gap.
  • Leave issued tickets alone. A ticket you already hold does not get the November rate. Changing dates or reissuing it may trigger a new calculation. Ask the airline before you change anything.
  • Check where the ticket is issued. These figures apply to tickets issued for flights departing Korea. Tickets bought in another country follow that market's surcharge rules.

What we do not know yet

Low-cost carriers. We could not confirm November surcharges for Korea's budget airlines from two independent sources. In October, Jeju Air, Jin Air, Air Busan, Air Seoul and Eastar Jet were reported to charge KRW 22,000 one way on domestic routes. Check each carrier's website notice for November.

December rates. December is unconfirmed. It will depend on the Singapore jet fuel average from Oct. 16 to Nov. 15. Airlines usually announce the next month's level around the middle of the month. The stage could rise or fall.

Your exact route. Airlines publish full band tables in their notices. Your city may fall in a band not listed here.

Your next step

If your winter trip from Korea is fixed, open the Korean Air or Asiana Airlines app today. Compare the fare, the refund rules and the surcharge line. If the numbers work, finish payment and confirm your e-ticket number before Friday, Oct. 30, so the weekend does not catch you out.
