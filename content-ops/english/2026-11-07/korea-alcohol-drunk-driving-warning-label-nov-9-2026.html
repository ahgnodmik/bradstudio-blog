Pick up a bottle of soju or a can of beer in South Korea after this weekend and you may notice something new on the front label: a warning against drinking and driving, either as text or as a red "no" symbol. From Monday, November 9, 2026, alcohol containers sold in the country must carry it. This explainer covers what the rule requires, why many shop shelves will still look the same for months, who is pushing back, and which details are still unsettled.

This article is based on Korean-language news coverage cross-checked as of November 7, 2026. Points reported by at least two outlets are stated as fact. Points we found in only one report are marked unconfirmed, and anything the government has not announced is marked as not yet decided. For the binding text, check the official notice from the Ministry of Health and Welfare.

What changes on the label

Korean alcohol labels already carry mandatory health warnings, mainly about the harms of excessive drinking and about drinking during pregnancy. The new rule adds an explicit message against drunk driving. Manufacturers and importers can meet it with a written warning or with a pictogram. The pictogram described by Korean media is a road-sign-style prohibition symbol: a red circle with a diagonal line through it.

Anyone who has seen Korean cigarette packs will think of their graphic health images, but this is different. The drunk-driving pictogram is a symbol, not a photograph. The revision also gives a legal basis for using images alongside health warnings, such as those about liver cancer risk, according to Kyeonggi Ilbo and Dailian. Which designs have been finalised, and whether any image is mandatory rather than optional, is described differently from one report to the next, so we treat those details as unconfirmed.

Placement matters as much as content. The warning has to sit on the front, directly below the main label, not tucked into a corner of the back. The aim is that anyone reading the brand name sees the warning at the same moment.

Text size by container volume

To stop warnings from shrinking into fine print, the rules set minimum font sizes that rise with container size:

  • 300 ml or less: at least 8 points
  • Over 300 ml, up to 500 ml: at least 10 points. A standard 360 ml soju bottle and a 500 ml beer can both fall here.
  • Over 500 ml, up to 1,000 ml: at least 14 points. A typical 750 ml wine or 700 ml whisky bottle falls here.
  • Over 1,000 ml: at least 16 points

Containers with coated surfaces, such as cans, must use larger text than the standard for their volume. Some reports put the increase at 2 points or more, while others simply say "larger," so the exact figure is unconfirmed. If you see different numbers elsewhere, the draft rules published in November 2025 proposed bigger sizes, starting at 10 points, and some older articles still quote them.

Why most shelves will not change overnight

The most practical detail for shoppers and bar owners is the transition rule. Any product that left the factory, or completed its import declaration, before November 9 can be sold with its existing label until May 8, 2027. For the next six months, you will likely see old and new labels side by side, sometimes for the same brand. An old label after November 9 does not mean a product is illegal or counterfeit.

The timing has also shifted once already. When the plan was first floated in November 2025, outlets including Financial News reported a start date of September 2026. The final announcement on May 8, 2026 set November 9, giving the industry about six months to prepare. If you had September in your notes, update it.

Pushback from importers, collectors and craft producers

Not everyone is on board. Herald Corporation and The Korea Economic Daily reported concerns from importers of wine and whisky, who must now add a front-of-bottle warning to premium and collectable bottles produced for global markets. Producers of traditional Korean liquors, who treat bottle design as part of the product's value, raised similar objections. Several outlets reported that a petition calling for the rule to be withdrawn or reconsidered was filed on the National Assembly's online petition system. Dailian also reported broader industry worries about falling consumption and rising costs.

Importers are expected to rely on stickers applied after arrival in Korea, since changing labels at overseas production lines is impractical, but company-by-company plans have not been announced. As of November 7, we found no announcement of a delay, and late-October coverage still assumed the November 9 start.

Context: Korea's drink-driving limit

The label change sits alongside some of the strictest drink-driving rules in the region. Since June 2019, under revisions widely known as the Yoon Chang-ho Act, a blood alcohol concentration of 0.03 percent is enough for a licence suspension, down from 0.05 percent, and 0.08 percent or more leads to licence revocation, as reported by The Korea Times and summarised by Korean legal guides. At that threshold, alcohol left over from the night before can be enough to put a morning driver over the limit. If you are visiting Korea and plan to rent a car, the safest assumption is that even one drink rules out driving.

Still unconfirmed or undecided

  • Exact penalties for non-compliance: reports mention possible criminal penalties, but we found specific fines and terms in only one source.
  • Personal imports, overseas online orders and duty-free purchases: one outlet reported that these are not covered, raising fairness concerns. Unconfirmed; check with the Korea Customs Service or the Ministry of Health and Welfare.
  • Size increase for cans and coated containers: "2 points or more" in some reports, unspecified in others.
  • Final image designs for health warnings, and whether any are mandatory: unconfirmed.
  • Outcome of the National Assembly petition: not decided as of November 7.
  • How individual importers will comply: not announced.

What to do now

If you are simply buying a drink, there is nothing you need to do; mixed old and new labels are normal until May 2027. If you run a bar, restaurant or shop, sort slow-moving stock and plan to sell anything with an old label well before May 8, 2027. If you make or import alcohol, keep shipping and import-declaration records for every batch, and compare new label proofs against the official notice text on the Korean Law Information Center before sending them to print. And if you have year-end gatherings coming up, decide how you are getting home before the first round: a designated-driver service, a taxi or the last subway.
