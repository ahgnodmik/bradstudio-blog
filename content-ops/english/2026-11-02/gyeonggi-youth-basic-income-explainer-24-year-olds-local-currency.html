If you are 24 and live in Gyeonggi Province, the ring of cities and counties that surrounds Seoul, the provincial government will give you up to 1 million won a year with almost no strings attached. You do not have to be unemployed, a student or low-income. You do have to apply on time, meet a residency test, and spend the money locally. The fourth-quarter application window for 2026 opened on November 2 and closes on December 1.

This explainer covers how the Youth Basic Income works, who qualifies, who does not, and what changed this year. It is written for anyone trying to make sense of the program in English, whether you are a Korean resident who prefers reading in English, a parent abroad, or someone curious about one of Korea's best-known basic income experiments.

What the program is

The Youth Basic Income (청년기본소득) is run by the Gyeonggi Provincial Government together with its cities and counties. It pays 250,000 won per quarter to eligible 24-year-olds, for a maximum of 1 million won across a year. The program launched in 2019 under then-governor Lee Jae-myung, who made basic income a signature policy.

Three features make it unusual among Korean welfare programs:

  • No means test. Employment status, income and assets do not matter. A 24-year-old working full time qualifies on the same terms as one who is job hunting.
  • Paid in local currency. The money arrives as Gyeonggi Local Currency, usually on a prepaid card or mobile app tied to your city or county. It cannot be withdrawn as cash.
  • Spent where you live. The currency works only at participating merchants in the city or county listed on your resident registration.

Note the closing time. The window shuts at 6:00 p.m. on December 1, not at midnight. The birth date range also shifts every quarter, so a friend who was eligible last quarter may not be eligible this one, and vice versa.

Who is left out

Foreign residents

The program is not open to foreign nationals. Fourth-quarter notices reported by Korean outlets list foreigners among those who cannot apply. The same exclusion appeared in English-language coverage of the second-quarter round in June 2026 by the Seoul Economic Daily and The Asia Business Daily, which listed foreigners and people of unknown residency status as ineligible.

Eligibility rules for foreign residents differ from one Gyeonggi program to another, so a benefit you or a friend received elsewhere is not a guide. For this program, if you hold a foreign passport, even with long-term residency, you should assume you are not eligible unless the provincial government announces a change.

Seongnam and Goyang

Young people registered in Seongnam or Goyang, two large cities in the province, cannot apply. CWN and other Korean outlets reported the exclusion for the fourth quarter, and English coverage of the second quarter described the two cities as not participating in the program this year. Because the program is co-funded by the province and each city or county, a city that does not take part effectively removes its residents from eligibility.

Several reports and guides say Seongnam stopped after repealing its local ordinance and Goyang did not budget its share for 2026. That explanation is widely repeated but could not be matched to an official press release in the research for this article, so treat the reasons as unconfirmed. Whether either city will rejoin in 2027 is also undecided.

What changed in 2026: automatic renewal is no longer the default

In earlier years, many recipients agreed to automatic application, which carried them into the next quarter's review without reapplying. That has changed. Gwacheon and Siheung both said that people who applied for the first time in the third quarter of 2026 are not enrolled automatically and must apply in each quarter's window themselves.

For people who agreed to automatic application in the first or second quarter, local guidance varies. Paju said they would be included in the next review without a new application. Ansan said support would continue without reapplying through the first quarter of 2027. A province-wide rule could not be confirmed, so the safest assumption is that you should log in and check.

Missed a quarter? You may be able to claim it now

The province and several cities say people who were eligible in an earlier quarter but did not apply can file a retroactive claim during this window if they meet the conditions. Gwacheon, for instance, said the retroactive option covers young people who qualified for any of the first three quarters of 2026 but did not apply. Missed payments are not sent automatically; you have to choose the retroactive option in the application. How many quarters you can recover depends on your birth date and the wording of each notice, so check the application screen or ask your local youth policy office.

Before you apply

  • Set up Jobaba early. You need an account and identity verification. Traffic tends to spike on the first day and in the last hours.
  • Resident registration abstract. Reports say it is submitted automatically if you consent to the public MyData service. If you do not consent, you will need to upload it yourself.
  • Check your residency history. If you moved in and out of the province, you may not meet the three-year continuous test. Add up your total years in Gyeonggi to see whether you reach ten.
  • Basic livelihood recipients. Reports say recipients of the national basic livelihood benefit can submit proof and receive the payment as a lump sum rather than quarterly. If this applies to you, gather the paperwork in advance.
  • Know the spending rules. The local currency only works in your registered city or county. If you are planning to move, check how that affects your card before you apply.

How long this quarter's local currency stays valid is not clear. English coverage of the second-quarter round mentioned a validity period, but that detail appeared in only one source and may differ by quarter or city, so confirm it in your city's notice.

Why the program matters beyond Gyeonggi

Korea has debated universal basic income for years, and Gyeonggi's youth payment is one of the few long-running, real-world versions of it in the country. It is narrow, since it covers a single age group and modest amounts, but it has paid out continuously since 2019. Its budget pressures and the decisions of individual cities to drop out are now part of that story, and they are worth watching for anyone interested in how basic income works in practice.

What is still unconfirmed

  • A single province-wide rule for how long earlier automatic-application consent stays valid. City notices differ.
  • The official reasons Seongnam and Goyang are not participating, and whether they will return in 2027.
  • The validity period of the fourth-quarter local currency.
  • The exact payment date in each city, beyond the planned December 20 start.

If you or someone in your family was born between October 2, 2001 and October 1, 2002 and is registered in Gyeonggi outside Seongnam and Goyang, log in to Jobaba before 6:00 p.m. on December 1. Submit the fourth-quarter application, tick any retroactive quarters you missed, and take a screenshot of the confirmation. If the screen leaves you unsure whether you are already enrolled, call your city or county youth policy office before the deadline.
