If you live in Korea and plan to order from US or European shops on Black Friday, the discount on the checkout page is only half the calculation. Korean customs decides what you finally pay, and this year two things matter more than usual: an eight-week enforcement campaign by the Korea Customs Service (KCS), and a new rule that makes your Personal Customs Clearance Code expire. This explainer covers the dates, the duty-free limits, how combined taxation works, and what to do before you pay.

How we checked: each date, figure and rule below appears in at least two independent sources, including the KCS's own notice and Korean outlets such as Money Today, Asia Economy, Law Times, BizWatch and Chungcheong Times. Most sources are in Korean. Anything only one source reported is marked "unconfirmed." Tax outcomes depend on the item and the clearance route, so treat this as orientation, not tax advice.

What the customs crackdown targets

The KCS announced on 2 November that it would run a special crackdown on people who abuse the direct-purchase system, timed to the shopping peak created by China's Singles' Day on 11 November and Black Friday. Money Today, Asia Economy and Law Times all reported the same eight-week window, ending on 27 December.

The agency named four priority offences:

  • Smuggling goods for resale while declaring them for personal use. Personal-use items get a duty exemption; reselling them is the abuse.
  • Using someone else's Personal Customs Clearance Code without permission. Splitting orders across other people's codes is a common way to dodge the duty-free limit.
  • Smuggling illegal food and medicines that could harm public health.
  • Importing counterfeit goods, including fakes of Korean brands.

Cosmetics, food and medicines, bags, shoes, clothing, and electrical and electronic goods are the focus categories. The KCS also said it would work with major e-commerce platforms to monitor listings and ask for illegal sales posts to be removed.

For scale, the KCS said it caught 38.5 billion won worth of direct-purchase abuse between January and September 2026. Of that, 32.4 billion won involved goods declared for personal use but meant for sale, 3.2 billion won involved intellectual-property infringement, and 2.9 billion won involved illegal food and medicines, according to Money Today and BizWatch. Those figures cover the nine months before the crackdown, not its results. Individual cases described in news reports may not have been settled in court.

Duty-free limits: 150 dollars, or 200 dollars from the US

Goods bought for personal use are exempt from customs duty and VAT when the item price is 150 US dollars or less. For goods shipped from the United States, the limit is 200 dollars. Money Today's report on the crackdown restated these thresholds, and consumer guides from Toss Bank and others give the same numbers.

Three details catch people out:

  • Go one dollar over and the whole amount is taxed. The exemption is all-or-nothing. A 151-dollar item from a non-US seller is taxed on 151 dollars, not on one dollar.
  • Origin means where the parcel ships from. A US website that dispatches from a warehouse in another country may fall under the 150-dollar limit.
  • Some categories follow different rules. Health supplements and medicines are excluded from the simplified list clearance route, and several guides say the 150-dollar limit can apply to them even from the US. That point is unconfirmed for every case, so ask the KCS before you buy.

Consumer guides generally say the item price includes local sales tax and domestic shipping in the seller's country, but not the international freight to Korea. If your basket sits close to the limit, leave a margin: exchange rates and last-minute coupons can push you over.

Combined taxation: when separate orders count as one

Combined taxation stops shoppers from splitting a large order into small parcels to stay under the limit. The current test is simple: items bought from the same seller on the same day can be added together and assessed against the limit as one.

The rule used to be broader. Until November 2022, parcels that simply arrived in Korea on the same day could be combined even if they came from different sellers. The KCS removed that arrival-date test from 17 November 2022, according to reports at the time by The Fact and BizWatch. Different sellers on the same day, or the same seller on different days, are now assessed separately.

Package forwarders add a wrinkle. If you ask a forwarding address in the US to consolidate several orders into one box to save on shipping, that box may be treated as a single shipment. Add up the item prices before you consolidate.

The Personal Customs Clearance Code now expires

Every direct purchase into Korea needs a Personal Customs Clearance Code, a number issued by the KCS that you enter at checkout in place of your resident registration number. Until recently the code never expired, which meant a stolen code could be misused for years before anyone noticed.

The KCS amended its notice so that codes now carry a one-year validity period, applied in earnest from 2026. According to the KCS notice and reports by Chungcheong Times and WBCB, codes issued from 2026 are valid for one year from the issue date. Codes issued before 2026 expire on the holder's birthday in 2027. One report says foreign residents who obtained a code with a passport have until 31 December 2027 to renew; that detail is unconfirmed, so check UNI-PASS for your own date.

You can renew within 30 days either side of the expiry date, online through UNI-PASS, the KCS electronic clearance portal, or in person at a customs office. Renewal keeps the same code and extends it by a year. Miss the window and the code is cancelled, so you would need to apply for a new one. Changing your address or phone number also restarts the one-year clock.

For most existing users the first expiry falls in 2027, so this Black Friday is unlikely to be blocked by it. It is still the right moment to log in, note the date and check that your details are current.

Scam shops and payment habits

Customs is not the only risk. The Korea Consumer Agency has repeatedly warned during past shopping seasons that fake shops impersonating brands are often reached through social media ads, and has advised shoppers to confirm they are on an official store, to be suspicious of extreme discounts, and to pay by credit or debit card. Those warnings date from earlier years; we did not find a 2026-specific advisory, so treat the advice as standing guidance rather than new news.

Paying by card matters because it gives you a dispute route through your card issuer if goods never arrive. Paying in the shop's own currency rather than in won also avoids dynamic currency conversion fees that some sites add. Keep screenshots of the order page, the price and the seller name.

What to do before 27 November

This week, log in to UNI-PASS, check your Personal Customs Clearance Code's expiry date and contact details, and make sure the code and name saved at each shop and forwarder match. Then sort your wish list by seller, add up each seller's total, and decide which baskets you will keep under 150 dollars (200 dollars from the US) and which you will accept paying tax on. If anything clears customs under your code that you did not order, reissue the code and report it to the KCS on 125, option 10; for questions about tax on a specific item, call the KCS customer centre on 125, and for disputes with overseas shops, use the Korea Consumer Agency's 1372 Consumer Counseling Center or its cross-border consumer portal.
