If you run vans for a living, the hardest thing about going electric is usually time: time lost on the charger, and range that runs out before the working day does. The new Renault Trafic E-Tech is Renault's attempt to fix both. It is the brand's first vehicle built on an 800-volt architecture, it is quoted at about 20 minutes to charge from 15% to 80%, and UK pre-orders opened in November 2026 at £38,000 before VAT. Customer deliveries begin in 2027. Here is what is confirmed, what is not, and how to judge whether it suits your fleet.

How we checked: every specification, price and date below appears in at least two independent sources, including Renault's own UK and group materials, heise, Van Reviewer, electrive, Auto Express, What Car?, Fleet News, Motor1 and InsideEVs. Where outlets give different numbers, or only one source carries a figure, we say so and mark it "unconfirmed."

What is the Renault Trafic E-Tech 800V electric van?

It is a mid-size electric van, and it is not a diesel Trafic with batteries bolted in. It sits on a dedicated 800V "skateboard" platform developed by Flexis, the electric light commercial vehicle company set up by Renault Group, Volvo Group and shipping firm CMA CGM. A skateboard platform puts the battery flat under the floor and builds the body on top, which keeps the load area clear. Motor1 and InsideEVs report that the same platform also underpins the taller Estafette delivery van and the Goelette chassis cab and box body.

Ownership of Flexis has since changed. CMA CGM Group and Van Reviewer both report that Renault Group is buying out Volvo Group and CMA CGM to take full ownership. Exactly when that deal completes is worded differently across reports, so we treat the timing as unconfirmed.

Production is due to start at Renault's Sandouville plant in France in late 2026. Panel vans come first, with chassis cabs and other conversion-ready bodies to follow.

Battery, motor and WLTP range

Every version drives the rear wheels through a motor rated at 150 kW and 345 Nm. Buyers choose between two batteries:

  • 81 kWh NMC (nickel manganese cobalt): the long-range pack. Early reports from New Mobility and others quoted about 450 km WLTP. Renault UK now advertises up to 292 miles, roughly 470 km, and the German site elektroquatsch also lists up to 470 km.
  • 60 kWh LFP (lithium iron phosphate): the cheaper pack aimed at urban work, quoted at about 350 km WLTP by web.de and others.

The spread between 450 and 470 km reflects the timing of homologation and the market, not a contradiction. Use the figure on the official configurator in your country when you quote for a contract.

The van is also designed to be managed as software. It receives over-the-air updates and can stream operating data to fleet management providers. Renault materials and the New Zealand site Driven both list vehicle-to-load (V2L), which powers tools or laptops from the traction battery, and vehicle-to-grid (V2G), which can send energy back to the grid where local tariffs and hardware allow it.

How fast does it charge? 15–80% in about 20 minutes

Renault quotes about 20 minutes to take the 81 kWh battery from 15% to 80%, adding roughly 260 km of range in that window, according to heise and Van Reviewer. Renault UK's site states that the time was measured on a DC charger of around 240 kW.

The peak DC charging power is unconfirmed. Outlets have reported 190 kW, 240 kW and 250 kW, and Renault has not published one clear number that every report agrees on. The on-board AC charger rating for the new model is also unconfirmed; heise said it had not yet been disclosed.

Why 800 volts matters for a van

Power equals voltage times current. Doubling the voltage lets a car take the same power at roughly half the current. Lower current means less heat in cables and cells, so the vehicle can hold a high charging rate for longer instead of slowing down after a few minutes. For a driver, that is the difference between a coffee-length stop and a lunch-length one.

The jump is large compared with the previous Trafic Van E-Tech, shown in 2023. That model used a 52 kWh battery with up to 297 km WLTP and a 90 kW motor. It charged at 7 kW or 22 kW AC as standard, and 50 kW DC rapid charging was an option, according to Renault Group's press release and InsideEVs.

One caution when comparing vans: check the charging window. Renault quotes 15–80%; many rivals quote 10–80%. A five-point difference in the starting level means two "20-minute" claims are not directly comparable.

Price and release date: UK from £38,000, Germany from €41,900

United Kingdom. Pre-orders opened in November 2026. electrive and Auto Express both report a starting price of £38,000 excluding VAT for the 60 kWh L1H1 panel van in Advance trim. What Car? lists two trims, Advance and Extra, in panel van and crew van bodies and two lengths, L1 and L2. We did not find a confirmed UK price for the 81 kWh version.

Germany. Elektroauto-News and elektroquatsch both report the 60 kWh model from €41,900 before VAT. A price for the 81 kWh model appears in only one German source we found, so it is unconfirmed.

Delivery timing. Fleet News puts first UK deliveries in the second quarter of 2027, while Autotrader says mid-2027. Both point to spring or summer 2027, but exact dates will depend on your dealer and spec.

Conversions and campervans. Renault has not announced a factory campervan. EV Fleet World reports that Renault is extending its Converted by Renault upfitter programme, already used for the Master, to the Trafic E-Tech. That makes approved conversions likely, but any specific camper model is unconfirmed.

Is it right for your fleet? Practical checks before you order

  • Map your real daily mileage. Pull a month of telematics or logbook data. If most routes stay well inside 250 km with margin for winter and heavy loads, the 60 kWh LFP pack may be enough and cheaper.
  • Check your chargers, not just the van. The 20-minute claim depends on a high-power DC unit of around 240 kW. A 50 kW depot charger will take far longer. Look at which public hubs on your routes actually support 800V vehicles at high power.
  • Ask about payload and towing in writing. Battery size changes kerb weight. Get the exact payload for the body, length and battery you want before committing.
  • Price the whole contract. Compare lease or finance quotes including maintenance, insurance and energy costs per kilometre, not just the list price before VAT.
  • Plan for upfitting. If you need racking, refrigeration or a camper build, confirm that your converter has approval under Converted by Renault and check delivery lead times for the base van.
  • Treat V2G as a bonus. It needs compatible hardware and an energy tariff that pays for exported power. Do not build a business case on it until both are in place.

If the Trafic E-Tech is on your shortlist, book a call with your Renault commercial vehicle dealer this month, bring your route data, and ask for a written quote that states the battery, WLTP range, payload, delivery quarter and the charger power used for the 15–80% figure. That gives you a firm basis to compare it with any rival van before 2027 slots fill up.
