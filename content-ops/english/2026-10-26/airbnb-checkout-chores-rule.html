If you have ever spent the last hour of a holiday stripping beds and hauling bin bags for a host who also charged you a cleaning fee, Airbnb has just changed the rules in your favor.

Airbnb told hosts this month that they can no longer require guests to do heavy checkout chores. The new house rules policy applies to reservations made on or after November 20, 2026. TechCrunch, Euronews, ABC News (Australia), Skift and Time Out all reported the change around October 21. Korean outlet Digital Today carried the same details. This guide covers only the points that several outlets agree on.

The core rule in one sentence

Hosts may not ask guests to do tasks that take significant effort or time, or that a cleaner would normally do. Skift reported that Airbnb sent hosts an email titled as an important change to house rules. Coverage notes the policy wording says such tasks are "generally not allowed," so Airbnb keeps some room for judgment.

What hosts can no longer require

  • Stripping the beds
  • Doing laundry
  • Washing, drying or putting away dishes
  • Taking trash out of the property to an outdoor or offsite bin

What hosts can still ask

  • Putting used dishes in the sink
  • Gathering used towels in one place
  • Sorting trash and recycling into indoor bins
  • Locking the door when you leave

There are exceptions. Airbnb said it may allow other requests based on the type of property or local conditions. The example several outlets cited is bear country. A host there can still ask you to put trash in an animal-proof container.

Three changes that matter for guests

1. No fees or fines for skipped chores

Hosts cannot charge an extra fee because you did not complete a checkout task. They cannot set their own fines either. ABC News, Euronews and Digital Today reported this point. Under the old system, some listings warned of deductions for unwashed dishes or unstripped beds. That approach is now off the table for new bookings.

2. Rules must be visible before you book

Every house rule and request has to appear on the listing at the time you book. Hosts cannot add new requirements afterward. That covers messages sent after booking, signs inside the property and verbal instructions at check-in. A note on the fridge saying "laundry required before departure" has no force if it was not on the listing.

3. A tighter house rules menu

Hosts now pick from seven standard house rules. Anything else becomes a "request," and requests must be reasonable. Outlets described the exact list of seven slightly differently, so it is not reproduced here. Reports say Airbnb will flag requests that break the policy, and hosts will need to remove them or appeal.

Cleaning fees are not going away

This is the part many readers will miss. ABC News and Euronews both reported that cleaning fees stay. What the policy targets is the practice of charging a cleaning fee and then handing the cleaner's work to the guest. No outlet reported that cleaning fees will fall. You can still find listings where the cleaning fee is higher than one night's rate.

Why hosts are pushing back

TechCrunch led its story with host backlash. Some hosts said they would move to other booking platforms. Others said they would change their pricing. Small hosts raised concerns about more work and higher running costs. There is no data yet on how many listings have left Airbnb or how much cleaning fees have changed.

How this differs from 2022 and 2023

Airbnb has raised this issue before. In November 2022, CEO Brian Chesky said checkout requests should not be "unreasonable." He said stripping beds, doing laundry and vacuuming should go, while turning off lights, throwing out food and locking the door were fine. TechCrunch noted that Airbnb had no concrete way to enforce that guidance at the time.

Airbnb's 2023 Summer Release added checkout instructions. Hosts could pick tasks from a list, and guests got an automatic reminder the night before checkout. Airbnb also said guests could flag excessive requests in reviews.

The 2026 policy adds teeth. It lists what hosts cannot require. It bans fines. It blocks rules added after booking. It lets Airbnb flag non-compliant requests itself. A three-year-old recommendation has become written policy.

What is still unconfirmed

  • Penalties for repeat violators. Skift quoted policy text listing possible steps against hosts who repeatedly break the rule, such as a notice on the listing and lower search visibility. We could not confirm the full list in a second independent report. Treat it as unconfirmed.
  • Bookings made before November 20. Reports tie the rule to the booking date, not the stay date. Whether it applies to trips booked earlier for stays after November 20 has not been confirmed in an official Airbnb document. Treat retroactive coverage as unconfirmed.

What to do before your next booking

  • Check the booking date. The rule clearly applies to reservations made on or after November 20, 2026. If you booked a December trip in October, do not assume it is covered.
  • Read the house rules and checkout instructions before you pay. Under the new policy, only rules shown at booking count. Take a screenshot. It is your evidence if a dispute comes up.
  • Compare total price, not nightly rate. Add the cleaning fee and service fee before you compare listings.
  • Scan reviews for checkout complaints. If several guests mention long chore lists, pick another place.

Planning a holiday trip? Wait until November 20 to book if you can, then screenshot the listing's house rules before you hit "Reserve."
