A standard wheelchair stops at the first flight of stairs, and Toyota has just shown video of three concepts built to get past that wall.

Toyota calls them the "me" series: Walk Me, Boost Me and Challenge Me. All three debuted as static exhibits at the Japan Mobility Show 2025 in Tokyo. Now Toyota has released footage of people actually riding them. Before you get excited, know this up front: they are concepts. Toyota has announced no price, no launch date and no production plan.

What is new this time

Design site Core77 pointed out the key difference. At the show, the chairs just stood on display. The new video shows riders using them. A rider climbs stairs. Others play ball sports. Another heads down a forest trail.

The footage comes from Toyota's "TO YOU" brand advertising campaign, in a 60-second spot titled "me." The ad's description calls the series concept models meant to let anyone go where they want to go, regardless of disability or age.

Malaysia's national news agency Bernama reported this month from a Toyota exhibition of the series. A representative there said all three devices remain at the concept stage.

The three concepts, one by one

Walk Me: legs instead of wheels

Walk Me has no wheels. It stands and walks on four folding robotic legs. The goal is to handle stairs and uneven ground. Toyota Times, the company's own media site, says it can move freely even in tight indoor spaces where a wheelchair would struggle to turn.

Each leg lifts, bends and repositions on its own. That lets the seat stay level even when one leg is on a higher step. Several outlets compared the motion to a crab or an animal's legs.

Seat height is adjustable. Designboom and Yanko Design both reported that Walk Me can raise the rider to car-door height to help with getting into a vehicle. Both also said it can lower the rider close to the floor. The legs fold, so the chair can be stowed.

Of the three, Walk Me is the only one pitched as a direct alternative to an everyday wheelchair.

Boost Me: sport without hand controls

Boost Me is built for sport. In the ad, riders play tennis and basketball. It has no hand controls in the usual sense. Toyota says the rider steers by shifting body weight, leaning and twisting the torso. That frees both hands for a racket or a ball.

Challenge Me: an off-road power chair

Challenge Me is an electric chair for rough terrain. The ad shows it on a fishing trip and on forest trails. Toyota also highlights its ability to climb steps and curbs. Bernama reported that Walk Me and Challenge Me target daily movement and outdoor activity, while Boost Me targets sport.

A quick correction on how it was summarized

Some social media summaries described the set as "one for stairs, one for rough ground, one for daily travel." That is not quite right. There is no separate daily-travel model. Walk Me covers both indoor and outdoor everyday movement. Boost Me covers sport. Challenge Me covers rough terrain.

What Toyota has not said

  • No price. Toyota has published no pricing for any of the three.
  • No launch date. There is no release window or pre-order page.
  • No production plan. Outlets describe Walk Me as a prototype or design study, not a finished product.
  • No confirmed specs. Battery life, top speed, weight and sensor types vary from outlet to outlet. We found no Toyota specification confirmed by two independent sources, so we left the numbers out.

Why it matters even as a concept

Stair-climbing chairs are not brand new. Swiss startup Scewo, for example, makes a power wheelchair designed to climb stairs on tracks. What Toyota adds is scale and a different mechanism. Walking legs, rather than tracks or wheel clusters, aim to handle stairs, uneven ground and transfers in one device. A carmaker of Toyota's size showing working footage signals that the idea has moved past the sketch stage. It does not mean a product is coming soon.

What to do if you need mobility help now

  • Do not wait for the me series. There is nothing to order.
  • If stairs are your main barrier, ask your local mobility equipment supplier about stair-climbing chairs already on sale in your country. Availability differs by market.
  • Check what your public health insurer or disability support program covers before you buy. Many programs require a prescription and approval first.

Watch Toyota's "TO YOU" me ad to see the concepts move, then talk to your therapist or equipment supplier about what you can use today.
