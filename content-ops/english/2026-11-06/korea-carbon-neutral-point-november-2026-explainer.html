South Korea will pay you, in cash, for some of the small green choices you may already make: bringing your own cup to a café, asking for an e-receipt instead of a paper one, riding a shared bike instead of taking a taxi. The program is called the Carbon Neutral Point, and November 2026 is an unusually busy month for it. New ways to earn are being added, two bonus promotions end on November 30, and a new pledge campaign for electric vehicles opens to the public on November 27. If you live in Korea, here is what changes, what is still unclear, and what to do before the month runs out.

This explainer is based on government announcements and Korean media reports as of November 6, 2026, checked across at least two sources where possible. Rates and details that have not been officially published are marked unconfirmed. Program rules can change at short notice, so check the official site before relying on any figure.

What the Carbon Neutral Point is

The Carbon Neutral Point is a government incentive scheme that rewards individuals for everyday actions that cut carbon emissions. The part covered here is the green-living track, known in Korean as the 녹색생활실천 program. You sign up once on the official website, link the services you use, and earn points each time a participating business reports an eligible action. Points are paid out as cash or through mobile payment services.

The program is run by the Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment. That ministry launched on October 1, 2025, when the former Ministry of Environment took over energy policy functions previously handled by the trade and industry ministry, according to The Korea Times and the Korea JoongAng Daily. Older English-language articles about the Carbon Neutral Point refer to the Ministry of Environment; they are describing the same program.

Three basic rules apply across the board:

  • Annual cap: each person can receive up to 70,000 won a year in total. The higher earning rates in November do not raise this ceiling.
  • Payment timing: points for a given month are paid out in stages from the end of the following month. What you earn in November should start arriving from late December.
  • Linking is required: signing up on the website is not enough. Each participating service, whether a bike-share app or a coffee chain, usually needs its own opt-in.

New in November: zero-emission taxis and reusable delivery boxes

Korea's official policy portal, Korea.kr, listed two additions in its guide to changes for the second half of 2026: taking an electric or hydrogen taxi, and receiving a delivery in a reusable shipping box. The logistics trade paper KLNews also reported that reusable delivery boxes would be added from November.

Several details are still missing:

  • How much each action earns: unconfirmed. We found no official per-ride or per-delivery rate as of November 6. One guide reported that the ministry was still finalizing rates.
  • Which taxi apps and shopping or delivery companies take part: unconfirmed. Coverage suggests you will need to opt in to point linking inside each participating platform once the list is published.
  • The total number of eligible actions: sources disagree. Korea.kr describes the list growing from 17 to 19 items, while KLNews counts 14 rising to 16. Both agree that two items are being added; they appear to count the existing list differently.

Ending November 30: four times the points for shared bikes

From October 12 to November 30, 2026, shared-bike rides earn 400 won per kilometre instead of the usual 100 won. SBS Biz, ZDNet Korea and Digital Daily all reported the same dates and rates. The promotion covers three services:

  • Ttareungyi, Seoul's public bike-share system.
  • Gcoo, the electric bike service run by GBike.
  • Nubija, the public bike-share system in Changwon.

The calculation uses a recognized distance rather than every metre you ride, so short detours may not count. At the promotional rate, a recognized 5 km is worth 2,000 won and 10 km is worth 4,000 won. Both new and existing members are eligible. The order of steps is: register for the Carbon Neutral Point, opt in through the bike-share service, then ride. The official notice warns that the promotion can end early or change if the budget runs out.

Also ending November 30: double points on everything else

The official Carbon Neutral Point website lists a promotion that doubles points for all green-living actions, running from August 1 to November 30, 2026. SBS Biz also reported doubled rewards for bringing a tumbler at participating cafés. According to the official notice, the end date was moved forward from December 31; we found that change on the official site only, so treat the original date as background.

Whether the double-points offer stacks with the four-times bike-share rate is unconfirmed. We found no official statement either way. It is safer not to count on a combined multiplier.

From November 27: the EV pledge campaign

The ministry launched an electric vehicle pledge campaign on November 2, starting with the minister and staff. Members of the public can join from November 27, according to the Herald Business and Aju Business Daily, through the integrated Korea Climate Action and Carbon Neutral Point website or the Carbon Pay app.

  • Signing the pledge: 2,000 points.
  • Buying an electric vehicle afterwards: an additional 20,000 points, for a possible total of 22,000.
  • What you pledge: to use public transport first, and to choose a zero-emission vehicle such as an EV when you next rent or buy a car.

The pledge points are expected to be paid at the end of the month after you sign. The criteria and process for the 20,000-point purchase reward have not been announced and are unconfirmed. It has also not been announced whether people who bought an EV earlier in 2026 qualify.

Practical tips before you start

  • Check how close you are to the cap. If you are already near 70,000 won for the year, the November bonuses add little.
  • Confirm your linking, not just your membership. Many people sign up and then never connect the bike app or café account, so nothing is recorded.
  • Do not budget with the money yet. November earnings arrive from late December at the earliest.
  • Keep an eye on notices. Promotions can be shortened if funds run out, and the new taxi and delivery-box rates are still to be published.
  • Foreign residents: sign-up involves identity verification, so check on the official site whether your details are accepted before you plan around the bonuses.

Here is what to do this week. Log in to the official Carbon Neutral Point website, cpoint.or.kr (in Korean), and check your membership status and this year's running total. If you use Ttareungyi, Gcoo or Nubija, confirm in the app that Carbon Neutral Point linking is switched on, and try swapping part of your commute to a bike before November 30. Put November 27 in your calendar if you plan to sign the EV pledge, and wait for the official partner list before setting up taxi or delivery-box linking.
