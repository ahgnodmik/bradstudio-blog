Anyone who has used a mini projector knows the awkward moment that comes right after unboxing: where do you put it? On a stack of books, balanced on a mug, wedged against a pillow at the foot of the bed. Aurzen's answer with its ZIP line is to make the projector its own stand. The body is split into three hinged sections that fold flat for a pocket or bag and unfold into a Z shape that props itself up. The new ZIP Pro, unveiled at IFA 2026 in Berlin, keeps that idea and pushes the picture to 1080p. Here is what is confirmed, what is still only a maker claim, and how it lines up against the original ZIP and Samsung's The Freestyle.

Most specifications in this article are manufacturer claims. Independent brightness measurements and long-term reviews are limited. Prices and shipping dates are as reported by at least two outlets; anything that appeared in only one source is marked unconfirmed. This is not a sponsored post.

The idea: a projector that folds into a stand

The fold-out design is not new. Aurzen first showed the original ZIP at CES 2025 and ran a Kickstarter campaign at the same time, according to TechRadar and New Atlas. Retail sales in the US and Canada followed in April 2025. Two hinges split the body into three parts, and by changing the angle of the panels you raise or lower the image. New Atlas and Aurzen's own materials describe the unfolded body as a free-standing support that can be tilted from roughly 0 to 90 degrees.

That matters more than it sounds. Most small projectors either need a tripod, come with a separate stand, or rely on you finding a flat object of the right height. Building the stand into the chassis removes a piece of kit and makes the setup almost instant: unfold, aim, play.

What's new in the ZIP Pro

The headline upgrade is the picture. Notebookcheck, Gizmochina, Android Authority and Aurzen's IFA press release agree on the core numbers, all of which are maker claims:

  • Resolution: native 1080p (1,920 × 1,080), up from 720p on the original.
  • Brightness: 200 ANSI lumens, double the original's 100.
  • Battery: 38Wh, with up to about 2.5 hours of playback in Eco mode.
  • Weight: 586 g.
  • Setup aids: IntoMobile reports time-of-flight autofocus and four-point keystone correction. We found this detail in one outlet only, so it is unconfirmed.

There is a catch on brightness worth flagging. Notebookcheck's spec sheet breaks the 200-lumen figure down by mode, with lower output in Eco and standard modes and the full 200 only when plugged in. We could not find that breakdown in a second independent source, so treat it as unconfirmed, but it is a reminder to read a battery projector's brightness figure alongside the conditions it is measured under. Gizmochina also reports 65W USB-C power delivery charging; that is a single-outlet report and is unconfirmed here.

PMA Research describes the ZIP Pro as built for dark rooms and evening use rather than bright spaces. That matches common sense for any projector in the 200-lumen range: it will look good with the lights off, in a tent, or outdoors after sunset, and washed out in a sunny living room.

The Basquiat edition

Aurzen is also selling a ZIP Pro Basquiat Special Edition, licensed from the estate of Jean-Michel Basquiat. According to Aurzen's press releases, it was revealed with a takeover in New York's Times Square, timed during Paris Fashion Week, and Germany's netzwelt also covered the design. A review published by Gizmochina on October 31 listed a Basquiat bundle at $679.99, but we found only that one source for the price, so it is unconfirmed.

Lined up side by side, the trade-off is clear: you get a sharper, brighter image, and you pay for it in size and weight.

The weight roughly doubles, from 280 g to 586 g. The folded volume grows too. Aurzen lists the ZIP Pro at 487 cc, and Projector1 describes the folded unit as nearly three times the size of the original. If you liked the first ZIP because it vanished into a jacket pocket, the Pro is a different proposition: still small, but closer to a palm-sized gadget than a pocket one.

In exchange, the jump to native 1080p is a meaningful upgrade for watching films, where 720p on a large wall can look soft. Doubling the rated brightness also widens the range of rooms where it is usable, though only independent testing will show how close it gets to the rated figure.

How it compares with Samsung's The Freestyle

The closest well-known rival in spirit is Samsung's The Freestyle, which also folds its stand into the product. The approach is different. The Freestyle sits in a cradle that lets the body rotate up to 180 degrees, so you can swing the image from wall to ceiling. Spec listings and reviews gathered by Projector Central and What Hi-Fi give the first-generation model these figures:

  • Native 1080p resolution.
  • 550 LED lumens, a rating Projector Central notes is not directly comparable with ANSI lumens.
  • About 830 g.
  • US launch price of $899.99, though it has frequently sold for much less since.

Because Samsung quotes LED lumens and Aurzen quotes ANSI lumens, you cannot simply say one is brighter than the other from the spec sheets. Sources also disagree on how the second-generation Freestyle is rated, so we have left it out of the comparison. What can be said is that the ZIP Pro is lighter and folds smaller, while The Freestyle is built around flexible aiming, letting you point the image at the ceiling without touching the stand. We did not find any test that measured the two side by side.

Price and availability

The list price is $629.99. Notebookcheck, Android Authority and Gizmochina also reported a launch price of $579.99. Notebookcheck and Projector1 both reported that shipping was due to start on October 30, 2026, and the product is sold through Aurzen's own website. Regions mentioned in coverage include the US, the UK and Europe.

When the launch discount ends is less clear. Projector1 reported that the $579.99 early-bird price ran until October 15, while a review published on October 31 still listed it at that price on Aurzen's site. Because the two reports conflict, the current price at checkout is unconfirmed; check the store before you buy. Availability on Amazon and sales outside the US, UK and Europe are also unconfirmed.

Who it suits, and who should wait

  • Good fit: travellers, campers and people who want a big picture on a hotel wall or a bedroom ceiling without packing a stand.
  • Good fit: anyone who owns the original ZIP and found 720p too soft for films.
  • Think twice: if pocketability was the main reason you liked the original. The Pro is about twice as heavy.
  • Think twice: if you mainly watch in daylight. No 200-lumen projector will compete with a TV in a bright room.
  • Wait: if you want independent brightness and battery testing before spending more than $500.

If you are weighing it up today, do two things before ordering. Open Aurzen's product page and note the price shown at checkout, not on the banner, along with the estimated shipping date for your country. Then look up the return window and warranty terms for your region, so you know your options if the brightness in your own room does not match the spec sheet.
