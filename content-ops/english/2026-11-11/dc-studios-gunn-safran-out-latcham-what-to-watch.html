If you follow the DC Universe, the people who planned your next few years of superhero movies just lost their jobs. On 9 November 2026, Skydance, the company formed when Paramount Skydance absorbed Warner Bros. Discovery, announced that James Gunn and Peter Safran are stepping down as co-heads of DC Studios. Their replacement is Jeremy Latcham, a Marvel Studios veteran, who starts on 1 December. This piece sets out what is confirmed, what is not, and what fans should keep an eye on over the coming months.

How we checked: every date and number below appeared in at least two independent outlets, including Forbes, IndieWire, CBC, Screen Rant, CBS News and NBC News. Anything reported by only one outlet is labelled "unconfirmed." Release dates are listed only where two or more sources agree. Details are current as of 10 November 2026.

What happened

Skydance said Gunn and Safran will leave their posts as co-chairmen and co-CEOs of DC Studios. The two were named to run DC in October 2022, according to Bloomberg and Variety reports from that month, so their tenure lasted roughly four years. Some social media summaries say they took charge in 2023; the appointment was announced in 2022, and the studio formally reorganised around them shortly after.

Jeremy Latcham takes over on 1 December as president and chief creative officer of DC Studios. He will oversee film, television and animation, and he will report to Dana Goldberg and Josh Greenstein, who co-chair Skydance's film division. Forbes and CBC both reported that reporting line.

Gunn and Safran are not leaving DC entirely. Gunn will still direct Man of Tomorrow, the sequel to his 2025 Superman, and Safran will still produce it. CBC and Screen Rant reported the same arrangement. Gunn has also said he is writing another DC project, but what it is and whether it will be made are unconfirmed.

Who is Jeremy Latcham?

Latcham was one of the early producers at Marvel Studios. He started as an associate producer on Iron Man (2008) and went on to serve as an executive producer on The Avengers, Guardians of the Galaxy and Spider-Man: Homecoming. He left Marvel in 2017 to set up his own production company, whose credits include Dungeons & Dragons: Honor Among Thieves. In other words, DC is now led by someone who helped build the model it has spent a decade trying to match.

Why it happened now

The change follows one of the biggest deals in Hollywood history. Paramount Skydance completed its takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery on 6 October 2026 in a deal valued at about $110 billion, according to CBS News, NBC News and Bloomberg. The merged company is called Skydance and is led by David Ellison.

There was a twist along the way. In early October, several outlets reported that Ellison had asked Gunn and Safran to stay on, even as Warner Bros.' film chiefs were replaced. A month later, the DC leadership changed too. Skydance did not give a reason. One outlet reported a disagreement over creative autonomy, but that account has not been matched elsewhere, so treat it as unconfirmed.

The box office record behind the decision

The new DC Universe has had mixed results. Superman (2025) opened above $100 million in North America, a strong start reported by SBS Biz and GamesRadar+. Supergirl (June 2026) opened at roughly $37 million to $38 million domestically, depending on when the count was taken, A budget of about $170 million has been reported, but we could not match that figure in two sources, so it is unconfirmed. Clayface, released on 23 October 2026, opened at about $24 million in North America and about $43 million worldwide, according to AP and TheWrap. Some coverage suggests its smaller budget puts it in a better position than Supergirl, though no final profit figures have been published.

What fans should watch for next

1. The first slate announcement under Latcham

New studio heads usually set out their priorities within their first months. Watch for any statement after 1 December on whether the connected DCU plan that Gunn and Safran mapped out stays in place, gets trimmed or is replaced. Nothing has been announced yet.

2. Whether Man of Tomorrow keeps its date

The only firm date on the DCU calendar is 9 July 2027 for Man of Tomorrow. Since Gunn is staying on as director, the simplest reading is that it continues as planned. Even so, a change of studio leadership can bring reshoots, edits or date moves. If the date shifts, it will be the clearest sign of how much the new team wants to change.

3. A firm date for The Batman Part II

Matt Reeves' Batman films sit outside the main DCU continuity. Reports currently disagree on whether the sequel arrives in October 2027 or February 2028. Until the studio confirms a date, treat both as unconfirmed.

4. What happens to TV and streaming

Latcham's remit covers television and animation as well as film, so series planned under Gunn are now under the same review. Where DC films and HBO shows stream in your country depends on local licensing deals, which the merger may or may not change. No changes have been announced.

What to do now

Put 1 December in your calendar and check DC Studios' and Skydance's official channels that week for the first statement from Latcham. Save 9 July 2027 as the Man of Tomorrow date, but recheck it after any slate announcement. If you have missed earlier DCU titles, search your streaming apps this week to see which are available where you live, before any licensing changes take effect.
