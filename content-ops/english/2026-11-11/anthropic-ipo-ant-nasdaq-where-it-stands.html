The five stages of a US IPO

It helps to know the usual sequence before looking at the details. A company first submits a draft registration statement to the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) in confidence. It then files a public Form S-1, which discloses its business, financial statements and risks. Next comes the waiting period, when the SEC reviews the filing and the company files amendments, known as S-1/A, in response to comments or to add missing terms. Once a price range appears, the company and its banks go on a roadshow to gauge demand from large investors. Finally, the deal is priced, usually the evening before trading begins, and the shares list on the exchange.

What the original S-1 showed

The 26 October filing named the Nasdaq Global Select Market as the venue and ANT as the ticker for Anthropic's Class A common stock. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley lead the underwriting group. At that point the number of shares and the price range were left blank, which is normal for a first public filing.

The filing also published Anthropic's financials for the first time. Revenue for the first half of 2026 was about $16.3 billion, up from about $1.16 billion a year earlier. The net loss for the same half was about $80.7 billion, a headline number that drew a lot of attention. Most of it, roughly $72.4 billion, came from non-cash changes in the fair value of convertible notes, derivatives and SAFE agreements, which grow on paper as the company's value rises. The operating loss, a closer guide to the underlying business, was about $693 million, down from about $2.1 billion a year earlier.

The latest stage: a price range

On 10 November, Anthropic filed an amended S-1 that sets an expected price of $70 to $78 per Class A share, with about 825 million shares in the offering. UPI, Gizmodo and Invezz all reported the range, and several reports said the roadshow began the same day. A price range is a guide, not a promise. Final pricing can land above, inside or below it, depending on demand during the roadshow.

Some details around the range are less settled. Quartz, citing Reuters, reported that Anthropic retracted an earlier set of terms that had been emailed to banks, describing them as inaccurate. That account comes through one chain of reporting, so we treat it as unconfirmed. Estimates of total proceeds and implied valuation also vary widely, because they depend on the final price, whether existing shareholders sell, and how fully diluted share counts are calculated. We do not repeat any single figure here.

When could ANT start trading?

UPI reported that pricing is planned for 18 November, and other outlets mentioned trading starting around 19 November. Anthropic has not announced either date. Until the company or Nasdaq confirms, the listing date is unconfirmed. Earlier talk of a valuation between $1.5 trillion and $2 trillion came from people speaking anonymously and is also unconfirmed. The last official valuation figure is $965 billion after the May 2026 funding round, reported by Bloomberg Law and others.

Practical points before listing day

Newly listed shares can swing sharply in their first days of trading. The IPO price is generally available to institutions and some retail programmes, while most individual investors buy on the open market once trading begins, often at a different price. Market orders placed at the open can fill far from the last quoted price. If you trade from outside the United States, check currency conversion fees, tax forms such as the W-8BEN, and whether your platform supports US IPO allocations at all.

Read the risk factors section of the S-1 yourself rather than relying on headlines. It sets out the company's own view of what could go wrong, including competition, the cost of computing power and the chance that it does not reach profitability.
