Mac users have asked for a touchscreen for years, and Apple finally answered in late October 2026 with an OLED touch MacBook Pro that starts at $2,899. That is a lot to spend if the laptop you already own works fine. A small US company called Intricuit is offering a different route: a glass touch panel that snaps onto the screen of a MacBook you already have. Yanko Design described it on 9 November as a $179 touchscreen for MacBooks. Here is how the Magic Screen works, which Macs it fits, why its price has been reported at several different figures, and what is still unclear before you pre-order.

How we checked: details below appear in at least two independent sources, including Intricuit's own site and coverage from MacRumors, 9to5Mac, AppleInsider, The Gadgeteer, Yanko Design and others. Details found in only one place are marked "unconfirmed." We have not tested the product, and we did not find an independent review with measured touch accuracy or latency. Details are current as of 10 November 2026.

What the Magic Screen actually is

The Magic Screen is not a second display. It does not show any image of its own. It is a thin sheet of tempered glass that sits in front of your MacBook's display. You keep looking at the Mac's own screen through the glass. Inside the panel is a digitizer, the layer that turns the position of a finger or pen tip into coordinates the computer can understand. MacRumors, 9to5Mac and AppleInsider described this design the same way when the product was shown at CES in January 2026, and The Gadgeteer and Yanko Design repeated it in November coverage.

How it attaches and connects

MacBook lids already contain magnets. According to The Gadgeteer and Yanko Design, Intricuit uses those magnets to hold the panel flush against the display, so there are no clips or adhesive. The panel connects to the Mac over a single USB-C cable. Basic touch input is reported to work without extra software, while a companion app for macOS Sonoma and later unlocks more settings.

The stylus

A pressure-sensitive pen is included. Intricuit's site and review coverage list hover, where the cursor follows the pen before it touches the glass, and palm rejection, so your hand can rest on the panel while you draw. Some third-party articles state 4,096 pressure levels, but we could not find that figure on Intricuit's own pages, so treat it as unconfirmed. MacTech and The Gadgeteer both report that you can detach the panel and lay it flat on a desk to use it like a drawing tablet.

Which MacBooks it fits

According to multiple reports, the Magic Screen supports 14-inch and 16-inch MacBook Pro models from the M1 generation onward, and 13-inch and 15-inch MacBook Air models from M2 onward. The M1 MacBook Air is not on the list, and Intel-based MacBooks are not supported. Each screen size has its own panel, so you need to order the one that matches your machine. Yanko Design and Intricuit's site reportedly mention support for MacBook Neo and a total of five sizes; that detail appeared in limited sources, so check the official compatibility list before ordering.

Not sure which chip you have? Open the Apple menu in the top-left corner and choose About This Mac. The chip name and the model year appear on the first screen.

Crowdfunding and shipping status

The Kickstarter campaign ran in summer 2026. BackerKit's tracker lists it from 30 June to 9 August, and The Gadgeteer also says funding closed in August. The number of backers differs between sources (1,382 on BackerKit, 1,588 according to The Gadgeteer), so the exact figure is unconfirmed.

Shipping targets have moved more than once. At CES the company aimed for the first quarter of 2026. Later reports mention an early third-quarter target and a backer update about worldwide shipping starting in late August. We could not confirm from public sources whether backers have received units, or when a pre-order placed today would arrive. heise made the general point that applies to any crowdfunded hardware: you are paying in advance and trusting the maker to deliver.

How it compares with the alternatives

Apple's touchscreen MacBook Pro. Announced on 28 October 2026, it builds touch into an OLED panel. US pricing starts at $2,899 for the 14-inch and $3,399 for the 16-inch model. It is the cleanest option, with no cable, no overlay and no extra step before closing the lid, but it means buying a new laptop.

iPad with Sidecar. If you already own an iPad and an Apple Pencil, Apple's Sidecar feature lets you use the iPad as a second Mac display you can draw on. It works well, but you need the iPad, and the drawing happens on a separate screen.

Pen tablets and pen displays. A traditional pen tablet makes you draw on the desk while looking at the monitor, which takes practice. A pen display solves that but is another device to buy, power and carry.

The Magic Screen sits between these: you keep your current MacBook and add touch and pen input directly on its screen.

Trade-offs reported so far

  • You must remove it before closing the lid. Lifehacker and The Gadgeteer both noted this, and a stopper on the lower edge reportedly prevents the lid from closing while it is attached.
  • It uses a USB-C port. Reviewers listed the cable as a downside, which matters on models with fewer ports.
  • Limited testing data. A Tom's Guide writer used it for three weeks and came away wondering why Apple had not made something similar years ago, but we found no review that measured touch accuracy or response time with numbers.

Who it makes sense for

The clearest fit is someone with a supported M-series MacBook who wants to sketch, annotate PDFs or mark up screenshots directly on the screen, and who does not want to buy an iPad or a new laptop to do it. If you travel a lot and close your laptop many times a day, the remove-before-closing step may become tiresome. If you need certainty about delivery dates, waiting until more units are in buyers' hands and more reviews appear is a reasonable choice.

Before you spend anything, check your chip and screen size in About This Mac, then open Intricuit's official page and confirm that a panel exists for your exact model. Look at the latest estimated delivery date and the return policy, add shipping and any import charges for your country to the $179 price, and decide whether you are comfortable paying now for a product with a moving ship date or would rather wait for independent reviews.
