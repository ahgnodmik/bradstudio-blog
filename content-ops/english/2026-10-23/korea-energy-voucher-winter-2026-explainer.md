# Korea's 2026 Energy Voucher: How Low-Income Households Can Cover Winter Heating Bills

Heating bills in Korea jump in winter, and many low-income households do not know they can get state help with them.

The help is called the Energy Voucher (eneoji baucheo). It is a government subsidy for heating and cooling costs. The Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment sets the policy. The Korea Energy Agency runs the program. Winter use opened in early October 2026. New applications close on Dec. 31, 2026. This guide explains who qualifies, how much you get, and which dates matter this autumn. It also covers what foreign residents should check before they apply.

## What the Energy Voucher is

The voucher is a fixed annual amount of energy credit. It is not cash in your bank account. You spend it on electricity, city gas, district heating, kerosene, LPG or coal briquettes.

The 2026 usage period runs from July 1, 2026 to May 31, 2027. That one period covers last summer and the coming winter.

The amount depends on household size:

- 1 person: KRW 295,200 per year
- 2 people: KRW 407,500 per year
- 3 people: KRW 532,700 per year
- 4 or more people: KRW 701,300 per year

These are annual totals. They are not monthly payments. A four-person household gets KRW 701,300 in total for the whole period.

## The big change for 2026: one pot, no seasonal caps

Until last year, the voucher was split into a summer portion and a winter portion. Each had its own cap. That split is gone in 2026.

Households now spend freely from one annual total across the whole usage period. This gives more flexibility. It also creates a trap. Any amount you spent on summer electricity has already come out of your total. Your winter balance is whatever is left.

If your household ran air conditioning heavily in July and August, check your balance now. Plan your winter heating around the real number, not the full annual figure.

## Winter use started in October

There are two ways to use the voucher. You choose one.

- **Bill deduction (virtual card).** This started on Oct. 1, 2026. The voucher is taken off your bill automatically. It applies to one energy type only: electricity, city gas or district heating.
- **Kookmin Haengbok Card (physical card).** This started on Oct. 3, 2026. You pay with the card at the point of purchase. It covers electricity, city gas, kerosene, LPG and coal briquettes.

The physical card matters for homes that heat with kerosene or LPG. Many older detached houses and rural homes in Korea use these fuels. Bill deduction cannot cover them.

You can switch between the two methods at your community service center until the usage period ends.

## Who qualifies

A household must meet two conditions at the same time.

First, the household must receive at least one benefit under the National Basic Living Security Act (gukmin gicho saenghwal bojang beop). The four benefits are livelihood, medical, housing and education benefits.

Second, the household must include at least one of these members:

- an older person aged 65 or over
- an infant aged 7 or under
- a person with a disability
- a pregnant woman
- a person with a severe, rare or intractable illness
- a single-parent family
- a child-headed household, including children in foster care
- a multi-child household, meaning a parent with two or more children under 19

Low income alone is not enough. A household with no qualifying member does not get the voucher, even if it receives basic living benefits.

## How to apply

New applications run from June 15 to Dec. 31, 2026. There are two routes:

- In person at the eup, myeon or dong community service center (haengjeong bokji senteo) for your registered address.
- Online through Bokjiro, the government welfare portal, at bokjiro.go.kr.

A family member can apply on your behalf. A public official can also file an application for you ex officio. This helps older people and people with limited mobility.

The Energy Voucher help line is listed as 1600-3190, with the program website at energyv.or.kr. This number appears in a government news release. We could not confirm it in a second independent source, so treat it as unconfirmed and check it on the official site before you call.

## Key dates this autumn

- **Oct. 1, 2026:** Bill deduction for winter use began.
- **Oct. 3, 2026:** Kookmin Haengbok Card use for winter fuels began.
- **Oct. 25, 2026:** Last day to use any balance left on last year's separate kerosene/LPG support prepaid card. Any money left after this date is forfeited. This card is a 2025 measure. It is separate from the 2026 voucher.
- **End of October 2026:** Reference date for the new advance exception payment (see below).
- **Dec. 31, 2026:** Deadline for new 2026 applications.
- **May 31, 2027:** Last day to use the 2026 voucher.

If you still hold last year's kerosene/LPG card, use the remaining balance within the next few days.

## Two new support types in 2026

**Advance exception payment (sajeon yeoe jigeup).** Some people live in goshiwon rooms or jjokbang single rooms. Their rent often includes energy costs, so they cannot use a voucher on any bill. The government now pays such households part of the support separately. It applies to eligible households that have not used the voucher at all by the end of October 2026. Some blogs quote exact amounts and a payment date. Official sources have not confirmed those figures, so we leave them out. Ask your community service center for the amount that applies to you.

**Coal-briquette conversion voucher (yeontan jeonhwan baucheo).** This helps vulnerable households that replaced a coal-briquette boiler with a different boiler this year. It supports the cost of the new fuel. A per-household amount appears in only one outlet. We treat that amount as unconfirmed.

## What foreign residents should know

The voucher is tied to the National Basic Living Security system. So the first question for a foreign resident is whether you can receive those benefits at all.

Two local government guides in English confirm that some foreign nationals can. The main group is registered foreigners married to a Korean citizen. This covers those who are pregnant by, or raising a minor child with, a Korean spouse. It also covers some divorced or widowed former spouses who are raising a Korean child, and those living with a Korean spouse's parent. Recognized refugees are also covered. These people must still pass the income test and the family-support test.

Here is the gap. We found no two independent sources that confirm how the Energy Voucher treats foreign nationals who receive basic living benefits. The general rules do not mention nationality. They also do not say foreign recipients are covered. This point is unconfirmed.

Do not assume you are excluded. Do not assume you are included either. Take these steps:

- Confirm whether your household receives a livelihood, medical, housing or education benefit.
- Check that at least one household member falls into a qualifying group, such as a child aged 7 or under or a member aged 65 or over.
- Bring your Alien Registration Card and your benefit details to your local community service center. Ask staff directly whether your household can receive the 2026 Energy Voucher.
- If you speak limited Korean, ask a Korean family member to come with you or to apply on your behalf.

## Before the cold sets in

Check your remaining balance today. Spend any money left on last year's kerosene/LPG card before Oct. 25. If you have not applied yet, visit your community service center or Bokjiro before Dec. 31, 2026. If you are a foreign resident on basic living benefits, ask the center about your eligibility this week rather than waiting for the deadline.
