You live in Korea, or you are flying in for the winter, and you want to know whether this El Niño means a mild season or a brutal one.

The honest answer is: probably milder than normal on average, with real cold snaps still possible. Forecasters give odds, not promises. This guide explains what the Korea Meteorological Administration (KMA, 기상청 Gisangcheong) and other agencies have said so far, why a warm winter can still freeze your pipes, and what to do in a Korean apartment before December.

Where the El Niño stands in late October

El Niño is a warming of the central and eastern tropical Pacific. It shifts weather patterns around the world. This year's event is unusually strong.

  • The World Meteorological Organization (WMO) said on 8 October that Pacific sea-surface temperatures are forecast to reach record levels in October to December 2026.
  • The WMO expects the event to keep strengthening toward a peak around December. Reporting on the update puts the chance that it lasts through February 2027 near 100%.
  • The US Climate Prediction Center (CPC) said on 8 October that the chance of a "historically strong" event in October to December is 83%. That was up from 75% a month earlier. "Historically strong" means a three-month index value of +2.5°C or more, stronger than any event since 1950.
  • The WMO forecast for October to December is about +3.7°C above average in the monitoring region. The previous record was about +2.6°C, set in late 2015 to early 2016. This is a model forecast, not an observation.

The CPC publishes its next update on 12 November 2026.

What the KMA has said about Korea

The KMA issues a three-month outlook around the 23rd of each month. Each month gets three probabilities for temperature and rainfall: below normal, near normal, or above normal.

The outlook issued on 23 September covered October to December:

  • November: 50% chance of above-normal temperature, 40% near normal, 10% below normal.
  • December: the same split, 50/40/10.
  • The KMA linked the warm lean in November to El Niño.
  • It also warned that low sea ice in the Barents and Kara Seas, north of Russia, could let cold air push south for short periods.

A 50% chance of "above normal" is a lean, not a lock. It still leaves a one-in-two chance of a normal or cold month.

A new KMA outlook covering November to January is scheduled for today, 23 October. At the time of writing, its figures had not appeared in two independent sources, so this guide does not quote them. Check the KMA long-range forecast page linked below. The outlook covering the core winter months will follow in the next monthly cycle.

Korean media have framed the season as a "roller-coaster" winter. The Hankook Ilbo and The Korea Times both reported in mid-October that mild spells, sudden cold snaps and heavy precipitation could alternate. Earlier, in September, the KMA said a strong El Niño had raised the odds of a warmer-than-normal winter, while noting that the Indian Ocean, the western Pacific and Arctic sea ice also shape Korean winters.

Japan's weather agency points the same way. Its three-month outlook, issued on 20 October, gives above-normal temperatures the higher odds across most of Japan from November to January, and cites El Niño.

Why "warm on average" does not mean "no cold wave"

The last super El Niño is a useful warning. The 2015-16 winter was warmer than normal across much of Asia. Then, in late January 2016, a sharp cold outbreak hit East Asia.

  • Seoul dropped to about -18°C on 24 January 2016. It was the capital's coldest day since 2001.
  • Jeju had its heaviest snow in roughly three decades. The airport closed for more than 40 hours, and tens of thousands of travellers were stranded.
  • A 2017 study in Scientific Reports linked that record cold event to a sudden change in Arctic and North Atlantic circulation that rapidly strengthened the Siberian High, the cold air mass that feeds Korean cold waves.

A monthly average can sit above normal and still contain three days of extreme cold. Your pipes and your boiler only care about those three days.

Heating basics: ondol and the gas boiler

Most Korean apartments use ondol (온돌), or floor heating. A gas boiler heats water. Pumps push that water through pipes under the floor. The same boiler usually supplies hot water for your shower and kitchen.

The floor slab stores heat. It warms slowly and cools slowly. That has practical effects:

  • Do not switch the boiler fully off when you leave in winter. Cold pipes can freeze, and reheating a cold slab takes a lot of gas.
  • Use the "away" mode (외출 oechul) or a timer mode (예약 yeyak) instead. Away mode keeps a minimum temperature to protect the system.
  • Leave the power on even if you travel for several days. Set away mode before you go.
  • Run the boiler once before the first cold spell. Listen for odd noises and check under it for leaks. If anything looks wrong, call your landlord or building management office now, not during a cold wave when repair crews are busy.

Button labels differ by brand. Photograph your controller and ask your landlord which button does what.

Frozen pipes and water meters

Water meters in Korea often sit in a small box in the outside corridor or in the ground. Seoul says nearly half of its frozen meters are in older apartment buildings with open outdoor corridors.

The Seoul Metropolitan Government gives three rules: fill, run, thaw.

  • Fill: pack the meter box with dry insulation, such as old clothes or foam. Wet insulation from last year loses its effect, so replace it.
  • Run: when a cold wave lasts two days or more, or overnight and when you go out, leave the bathtub tap running at a thin trickle.
  • Thaw: if a pipe freezes, warm it slowly with a towel soaked in 50-60°C water. Do not use a torch or a hair dryer. Both are fire risks.

Seoul reports that meters start to freeze when temperatures stay below -10°C for two days or more, and that frozen meters rise sharply below -15°C. If your meter glass cracks or bulges, call 120, the city's public service line, or your local waterworks office. In Seoul, the city covers replacement of a meter broken by a cold wave, but tenants pay if they skipped insulation or damaged the meter cover.

Alerts: cold waves and fine dust

The KMA issues two levels of cold wave alert (한파특보 hanpa teukbo). These are the long-standing thresholds:

  • Cold wave advisory: morning low of -12°C or below, expected for two days or more.
  • Cold wave warning: morning low of -15°C or below, expected for two days or more.
  • A sudden drop from the previous day can also trigger an advisory or warning.

Government emergency texts arrive in Korean on your phone. The Ministry of the Interior and Safety runs the Emergency Ready App for people who do not read Korean. It delivers disaster alerts in English and Chinese, and a ministry notice lists further languages. It also lists shelters and embassy contacts.

Winter is also fine dust season. Korea runs a "seasonal management system" (계절관리제) from December to March, when high particulate levels are most common. Measures include limits on coal power plants and, in many cities, weekday bans on the oldest, most polluting (Grade 5) diesel vehicles, with fines for violations. Check the next day's air quality before you plan outdoor exercise.

Your pre-winter checklist

  • Bookmark the KMA long-range forecast page and read today's three-month outlook.
  • Install the Emergency Ready App and set your language.
  • Run your boiler once and learn the away and timer buttons.
  • Pack your water meter box with dry insulation.
  • Save 120 in your phone if you live in Seoul or Busan.
  • Buy shoes with good grip. Snow that melts and refreezes is more slippery than fresh snow.

Do the first three today. When the first cold wave advisory lands on your phone, you will be ready instead of reading a manual in a cold room.
