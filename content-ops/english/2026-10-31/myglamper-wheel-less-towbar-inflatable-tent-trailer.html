Most camper trailers solve the space problem by adding wheels, axles, and a registration plate. An Australian startup called MyGlamper went the other way. Its product has no wheels at all. It rides on the back of your vehicle on the towbar, and at camp it unfolds and inflates into a tent that, in the largest versions, sleeps up to 12 people and includes a bathroom.

The company calls it a "wheel-less tent trailer." New Atlas, Yanko Design, HiConsumption, autoevolution, and the Australian site caravancampingsales.com.au all covered it in October 2026. Here is how it works, what the three models offer, what they cost, and the questions to ask before you put down a deposit.

How a tent can ride on a towbar

The packed module mounts behind the vehicle using what the company describes as a patented hitch-pin system. caravancampingsales.com.au compared the travel position to a towball-mounted bike carrier: a compact box that sits close to the rear of the car instead of trailing behind on its own wheels.

At the campsite, you unlatch the module and fold it out from the towball. It forms a base floor supported on pull-out legs, and the tent structure above it is inflated with air rather than pitched with poles. MyGlamper says a full setup takes about six minutes, a figure New Atlas linked to the entry-level Lite model. Larger models may take longer. No independent reviewer has published a timed setup yet.

Because there is no separate chassis, you avoid many of the familiar trailer chores: no reversing a trailer into a tight site, no separate tyres to check, and no second vehicle body to store at home. The trade-off is that everything you sleep in is fabric and air, not a hard shell.

What is inside the bigger models

The Plus and Pro add a kitchen area and an ensuite. HiConsumption and the company's own product page describe a hot recirculating shower and a 30-litre water tank, along with a composting toilet. HiConsumption also lists a 100Ah battery with solar charging on the larger models. Which items are standard and which are paid options is not fully clear from public material, so confirm the exact inclusions for the model you want.

Warranty

caravancampingsales.com.au reported a five-year structural warranty, a three-year warranty on the canvas and air tent, and 12 months on fittings. Check the written warranty terms before paying, particularly what counts as wear and tear on inflatable beams.

How it compares with the Thule Outset

The closest existing product is the Thule Outset, a hitch-mounted tent from the Swedish brand Thule. It slides into a standard 2-inch hitch receiver and unfolds into a raised sleeping platform. U.S. retailers including RealTruck list it at $4,699.95, and Thule says it sleeps up to three people.

The Outset is a sleeping pod for a couple or a small family. MyGlamper is pitched as a whole base camp for a group. They share a mounting idea but solve different problems, and the price gap reflects that.

What exists today, and what is still a render

This is the most important point for anyone thinking about a deposit. HiConsumption said only the Lite physically exists today, and that the Plus and Pro remain renders. New Atlas noted that all real photos and footage published so far show the Lite. autoevolution went further and reported that none of the three configurations is available yet. The company's website presents all three as products, so their status may change, but at the time of writing the 12-person versions with a bathroom had not been shown as finished hardware.

There is also a small conflict about inflation. HiConsumption wrote that each room has its own air valve, so a puncture would deflate only one section. A blog post from the founder on the National 4x4 Outdoors Show site described a single inflation and deflation point. Both may be partly true for different models, but it is worth asking the company directly.

Practical questions before you buy

  • Can your towbar carry it? Every towbar and vehicle has a maximum towball download, the downward weight it can hold. A 110 kg module sitting on the ball is a static load that never leaves. Check your vehicle manual and your towbar's rating, and remember that bike carriers and cargo racks count toward the same limit.
  • Will it block lights or the number plate? Many jurisdictions require rear lights and plates to stay visible. If the packed module hides them, you may need a light board. Ask your local road authority what applies.
  • Does it need registration? Some online videos suggest no trailer registration is needed in Australia because there are no wheels. We could not confirm this from two independent sources, and rules differ by state and by country.
  • Where will you set up? An inflatable footprint for 12 people needs a large, flat site. Check pitch sizes at the campgrounds you use most.
  • Can you ship it? MyGlamper is an Australian company selling via deposits on its own site. Shipping, import duty, and compliance outside Australia have not been announced.
  • What happens to your deposit? Read the refund terms and the expected delivery window in writing before paying.

Still unconfirmed

  • Delivery dates for any model.
  • Post-founders retail prices: one figure for the Pro appears as AU$35,249 and another as AU$35,245.
  • Deposit amounts per model, listed on the manufacturer's site but not independently reported.
  • The Plus model's dry weight, which we found in only one source.
  • Whether the Plus and Pro exist as working prototypes.
  • Registration and legal classification outside Australia.

If MyGlamper interests you, start with two checks this week: look up your vehicle's maximum towball download in the owner's manual, and email the company to ask which model is physically built, when it ships, and how the deposit refund works. Those three answers will tell you more than any render.
