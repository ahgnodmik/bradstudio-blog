On Thursday, October 29, 2026, Alphabet stock had the worst single session in its history as a public company. Shares of Google's parent fell about 12.6%, a drop CNBC described as "nearly 13%," and roughly $500 billion of market value disappeared before the closing bell.

No Alphabet trading day since the company listed in 2004 has been this bad. The trigger was not a scandal or a lawsuit. It was a set of quarterly numbers that showed how much cash the company is pouring into artificial intelligence, and how little of it is coming back so far. Here is what happened, what the numbers say, and what remains unclear.

This article explains reported facts. It is not investment advice and is not a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold any security.

What happened on October 29

Alphabet released its third-quarter results after the market closed on Wednesday, October 28. CNBC reported that the stock was already down around 10% in after-hours trading that evening. When regular trading opened Thursday, the selling continued and the stock finished the day down about 12.6%.

Outlets did not agree on the exact dollar loss, which is normal because they calculate market value at slightly different moments and with different share counts:

  • CNBC and NBC News rounded the loss to about $500 billion.
  • Yahoo Finance published figures of $475 billion and later $482 billion in different versions of its story.
  • Forbes described the loss as "almost $500 billion."

Every version of the number points to the same ranking. It is the second-largest one-day loss of market value for any company in U.S. stock market history. The record still belongs to Nvidia, which lost about $589 billion on January 27, 2025, when the Chinese AI lab DeepSeek rattled investors.

Free cash flow went negative again

Free cash flow is the cash a business generates from operations minus what it spends on buildings, servers, and equipment. It is the money left over for dividends, buybacks, acquisitions, or a rainy day. For most of the past two decades, Alphabet produced enormous amounts of it.

In the second quarter of 2026, that figure turned negative for the first time in Alphabet's life as a public company, at roughly -$5.9 billion. In the third quarter it fell further, to about -$9.1 billion. Two negative quarters in a row turned what looked like a one-off into a pattern.

Where the money is going

The cash is going into AI infrastructure: data centers, custom chips, networking gear, and the power to run them. Third-quarter capital expenditures of $51.4 billion were more than double the $24.0 billion spent in the same quarter of 2025. Alphabet kept its full-year 2026 spending range at $195 billion to $205 billion, and management told investors to expect a significant increase again in 2027.

That last line mattered. Investors had hoped for signs that spending would plateau. Instead, the company signaled that the bill will keep growing for at least another year, without saying by how much.

The good news that did not move the stock

Google Cloud more than doubled its revenue to $30.7 billion, helped by demand for AI computing, including Google's own tensor processing units (TPUs). Search revenue also grew 12%. On a different day, numbers like these would have been the headline. On October 29, the market weighed cash flow and spending more heavily than growth.

Why the reaction was so severe

Three factors made this report land harder than a typical earnings miss.

  • Alphabet was seen as an AI winner. For much of 2026, investors treated Google as one of the companies best positioned to profit from AI, thanks to its Gemini models, its chips, and its cloud business. A sharp fall from that position signals that even leaders are now being judged on cash returns, not just on technology.
  • The spending has no visible ceiling. A company can justify heavy investment if investors can see where it ends. A "significant increase" in 2027, with no figure attached, left analysts to fill in the blanks themselves.
  • Size amplifies everything. Alphabet is one of the largest companies in the world. A 12.6% move on a company that size translates into hundreds of billions of dollars, and that ripples into index funds and retirement accounts that hold the stock.

What this means if you hold U.S. tech through funds

Many people own Alphabet without ever buying a single share directly. Broad U.S. index funds, Nasdaq-100 trackers, and technology sector ETFs often hold it as one of their largest positions. If you hold any of those, part of this move is already in your account balance.

A few general, non-personalized points can help you read the situation calmly:

  • Check your actual exposure. Fund providers publish their top holdings and weights. Knowing whether Alphabet is 2% or 8% of a fund tells you far more than a headline about $500 billion.
  • Separate one day from the long term. A single session reflects how investors reacted to new information. It does not by itself tell you where a stock will be in a year. Nobody can reliably predict that.
  • Read the primary source. Alphabet's earnings release is filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and posted on its investor relations site. The cash flow table shows the free cash flow reconciliation directly.
  • Mind the currency. If you invest from outside the United States, your returns also depend on the exchange rate between the dollar and your home currency.

If you are unsure how this affects your own finances, a licensed financial adviser in your country is the right person to ask. This article does not assess whether Alphabet is cheap or expensive.

What is still unconfirmed

  • Exact market value lost: estimates range from about $475 billion to about $500 billion depending on the outlet and the calculation time.
  • Exact closing price: some reports gave a specific closing price, but we could not match it across two independent sources, so we leave it out.
  • Earnings per share and the size of the miss: outlets reported different per-share figures and expectations (GAAP versus adjusted), so we do not repeat a single number.
  • 2027 spending amount: one analyst estimate of around $300 billion was reported, but Alphabet itself gave no figure. Treat any number as an estimate, not guidance.
  • The previous worst day: claims about which earlier session held the record before October 29 appeared in only one source.

What to watch next

The next markers are Alphabet's fourth-quarter report, expected early in 2027, where the company may put a number on 2027 spending, and whether free cash flow turns positive again. Watch also how other large AI spenders report, since investors are now comparing their capex plans side by side.

If you hold Alphabet directly or through a fund, open your fund's holdings page today, note the Alphabet weight, and bookmark Alphabet's investor relations page so you can read the next cash flow table yourself instead of relying on headlines.
