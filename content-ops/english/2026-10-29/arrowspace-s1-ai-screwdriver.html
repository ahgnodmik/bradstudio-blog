Anyone who repairs laptops, consoles or drones knows the routine: you stare at a tiny screw, guess Phillips or Torx, guess the size, and hope you do not strip the head.

Hong Kong tool maker Arrowmax wants a camera to make that guess for you. Its new Arrowspace S1, now on Kickstarter, photographs a screw head, identifies it with an app, and lights up the matching bit in its case. The company calls it "the world's first AI screwdriver with eyes and a brain." The campaign runs from October 20 to December 4, 2026, and early pledges start at about $119. Here is how it works, what the reported specs say, and what to check before you back it.

How the S1 picks a bit

The idea is simple, and that is its appeal. The process has three steps.

  • Photograph. A small HD camera sits at the back end of the driver. Next to it is a fold-out contact probe. You rest the probe on the screw head, which holds the lens at a fixed distance. Every photo is taken from the same distance, so the size reading stays consistent.
  • Identify. The image goes to a companion smartphone app, which works out the drive type and size. One report says the recognition runs locally in the app on your phone.
  • Light up. The smart case shows the result on a small color screen and lights the slot holding the right bit. Men's Gear reports that the case marks a first choice in green and a backup bit in yellow.

You pull the lit bit, snap it in, and drive the screw. Yanko Design, New Atlas, Men's Gear and the First Backer listing all describe this same camera-to-app-to-case flow.

How many screw types it knows

Coverage is narrow for now. Reports say the model currently recognizes 12 common screw head profiles. The sources reviewed here do not publish the full list, so check whether the heads you meet most often are included. Unusual security screws may not be. One outlet said the model was trained on tens of thousands of real screw photos. That claim appears in a single report and is unconfirmed.

Driving the screw

Identifying the screw is half the job. The S1 also has an Auto mode that sets a safe torque range for the screw it just recognized. The goal is to stop you from overtightening a small screw and cracking a plastic boss or stripping a thread. That is a common way to damage a phone or laptop during a repair.

Arrowmax built its name on motion control. Its 2021 SES PRO used a gyroscope so you could twist your wrist to tell the driver which way to spin. A Newswire press release said that campaign was overfunded by 4,000%, and the SES PRO later appeared in the Red Dot product design awards. The later M1 PRO raised maximum torque to 10 kgf·cm, according to Yanko Design. One report says the S1 keeps that 10 kgf·cm ceiling. That figure has not been confirmed by a second source.

Price, funding and shipping

The starting price depends on where you read it. Yanko Design and First Backer list early pricing from $119. New Atlas gives HK$1,009, or about $129. The most likely explanation is that the cheapest early-bird tier sold out and the entry price moved up, but that is an inference. Check the live tiers yourself.

Funding totals also differ by date. A PledgeBox mirror of the campaign showed about HK$617,776 pledged by 472 backers against a HK$90,000 goal. New Atlas reported more than $100,000 raised, about ten times the goal. These numbers come from different moments of a live campaign, so no single total is confirmed here. What is clear is that the campaign passed its goal quickly.

Yanko Design gave April 2027 as the estimated delivery and $16 for air shipping. No second source confirmed either figure.

Things to know before you back it

  • A pledge is not a purchase. Kickstarter backers fund a project. If production slips or the product changes, refunds are not guaranteed. Delays of several months are common for hardware campaigns.
  • Check your country. Shipping costs, available regions, and import duties or VAT vary. Look at the reward page for your country before you pledge.
  • Think about the app. Recognition needs a smartphone app. Ask how long the company plans to support it, and which phone operating systems it runs on.
  • Match it to your work. If you open the same few devices with the same screws, a standard precision driver set does the job for less. The S1 makes the most sense if you handle many different devices with mixed screws.
  • Wait for real tests. How accurate 12-profile recognition is on worn, painted or recessed screws is still unknown. Hands-on reviews from independent testers will answer that better than launch copy.

What is still unconfirmed

  • Current starting price ($119 versus $129).
  • Battery capacity, runtime, charge time, body length and maximum torque.
  • Training data size and whether all processing is on-device.
  • Final funding total and the April 2027 delivery estimate.
