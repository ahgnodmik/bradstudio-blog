If you live in Korea and plan to make kimchi this winter, the cabbage will cost you less than last year, but the salted shrimp will not.

Late autumn is kimjang season, when Korean households make a large batch of kimchi to last through winter. This year two things matter for the shopping list. Napa cabbage and Korean radish are cheaper than a year ago. Salted shrimp, a core seasoning, is about a third more expensive. To ease the bill, the Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries is running a government-backed seafood sale from October 28 to November 29, 2026. Here is how the discount works and how to plan your purchases around it.

What kimjang is, briefly

Kimjang is the seasonal practice of making and sharing kimchi in large quantities, often with family or neighbors. UNESCO added it to its intangible cultural heritage list in 2013. A typical batch uses napa cabbage brined in sea salt, Korean radish, red pepper flakes, garlic, ginger and green onion. The seafood side matters as much as the vegetables. Salted shrimp (saeujeot) and fermented anchovy sauce (myeolchi aekjeot) give kimchi its depth. Many families also buy fresh oysters to mix in or to eat with boiled pork on kimjang day.

That is why a seafood discount is a kimjang story. Salt, salted shrimp, fish sauce and oysters can make up a large part of the total cost.

This year's prices: cabbage down, salted shrimp up

Several Korean outlets reported the same retail figures from the Korea Agro-Fisheries & Food Trade Corporation (aT), measured on October 16.

The shrimp jump has a clear cause. Salted shrimp output from January to August was about 9,500 tonnes, down 3.5% from a year earlier. Salted shrimp usually ages for three to six months before sale, so smaller catches earlier in the year show up in prices now. The Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries said it does not expect salted shrimp prices to fall much before kimjang season. Some headlines round the rise to 30% or 32%. The underlying figure is the same 32.5%.

An earlier October report put the salted shrimp increase at 23%. That reflects a different survey date, and the October 16 figure is the one most outlets now cite.

The Korea Seafood Festa, in brief

The sale runs under the name "Korea Seafood Festival – Korea Seafood Festa" (대한민국 수산대전–코리아 수산 페스타). It is linked to the government's wider 2026 Korea Grand Festival shopping event.

Korean reports stress that the 50% figure is a ceiling. The real discount depends on each seller's sale items, discount limits and sale period. Two stores may discount the same salted shrimp in different weeks.

How the KRW 10,000 cap works

The government portion is 20%, capped at KRW 10,000 per person. Simple math shows where the cap kicks in.

  • Spend KRW 50,000 and the 20% subsidy reaches the full KRW 10,000.
  • Spend more than KRW 50,000 and the subsidy stays at KRW 10,000. Anything beyond that only gets the retailer's own discount.
  • Buy 1 kg of salted shrimp at the October 16 average (KRW 20,269) and the subsidy is about KRW 4,054. You would still have about KRW 5,946 of cap left.

So one tub of salted shrimp alone does not use the full benefit. Adding fish sauce, sea salt and oysters to the same order gets you closer to KRW 50,000. Whether the cap applies once for the whole event or resets by period was not clear in the coverage reviewed. Check the terms at the store you use.

What the sale does not cover

This is a seafood program. Cabbage, radish, red pepper flakes and garlic are agricultural products handled by the Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs. Korean reports said the two ministries plan to expand supply and support discounts for kimjang ingredients from November 9. The date was reported, but the discount rate, per-person limit and participating stores for vegetables had not been officially confirmed at the time of writing.

A few general tips help too. Store salted shrimp in the fridge or a kimchi fridge once opened. Compare prices per kilogram, not per package, because tub sizes vary. If you make kimchi with relatives, decide who buys what so that each person can use their own KRW 10,000 cap.

Tips for foreign residents

  • Look for the event branding. Korean signs and online banners will read 수산대전 or 코리아 수산 페스타.
  • Ask about payment. Some retailers may apply the discount only with certain payment methods or membership accounts. Ask at checkout or read the event page.
  • Check the seller list. Korean press coverage points to the official Korea Seafood Festival site (fsale.kr) for participating sellers and dates. The site is in Korean, so a browser translation tool helps.

What is still unconfirmed

  • Whether the KRW 10,000 cap applies once per event or resets by period.
  • The discount rate, limits and stores for cabbage, radish and other vegetables (expected from November 9, not yet officially detailed).
  • Whether a traditional-market Onnuri gift certificate refund program, run in past years, returns in 2026.

Your next step: this week, check whether your usual supermarket or online mall is one of the 56 participating sellers. If it is, put salted shrimp, fish sauce and sea salt in one order of around KRW 50,000 and pay within that seller's sale window. Leave the cabbage and radish until the vegetable discounts are announced.
