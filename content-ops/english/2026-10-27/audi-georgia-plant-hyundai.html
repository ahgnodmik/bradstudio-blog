If you follow the US car market, one question now matters: what does Audi's first American factory change, and what is still only a promise?

Audi plans to build its first US vehicle plant in Georgia. President Donald Trump announced the project at a campaign rally in Perry, Georgia, on October 24, 2026. Audi CEO Gernot Döllner joined him on stage and confirmed the plan. The site sits near Macon in Peach County. Hyundai Motor Group already runs its Metaplant in the same state. This guide sticks to the points that several outlets and Audi itself agree on.

The plant in numbers

ItemWhat is confirmed
InvestmentAbout $4 billion (Audi). Trump said $4.2 billion at the rally.
LocationPeach County, near Macon, central Georgia
CapacityUp to 200,000 vehicles a year
ProductsPremium SUVs
JobsAbout 4,500
TimingConstruction from next year, production in the early 2030s (Audi)

Audi's own release uses the $4 billion figure. This article does the same. The $4.2 billion number came only from Trump's speech.

Three things to know

1. It is a plan, not a done deal

Volkswagen Group owns Audi. Its supervisory board still has to approve the spending. Reuters reported this, and outlets that carried the Reuters story repeated it. No source gave a confirmed date for the board vote. Treat the timing of approval as unconfirmed.

Labor is a factor. Half of a German supervisory board represents workers. IG Metall's Christiane Benner is deputy chair of the Volkswagen board. Reuters quoted her calling a US plant "right and necessary" for the market. She also wants firm commitments for workers in Germany. Automotive World reported a backlash in Germany over the plan. Volkswagen is cutting costs at home at the same time, so the vote carries weight.

2. Tariffs explain the timing

Audi has no US plant today. It ships every US-bound car from Europe or Mexico. Reports citing Audi put last year's US tariff cost at about 1.2 billion euros, or roughly $1.4 billion. Cars built in the European Union pay a 15% US tariff under last year's EU-US trade deal. Before Trump's second term, the rate on imported cars was 2.5%. Building in Georgia would take the vehicles made there out of that tariff.

Whether the US government offered Audi tariff relief as part of the deal has not been disclosed. Treat any link between this plant and lower tariffs as unconfirmed. The announcement also came about ten days before the November 3 midterm elections. The Hill and CNBC both noted that timing.

3. The timeline is long and the details vary

Audi says construction starts next year and production begins in the early 2030s. Trump told the rally that work would start sooner. One outlet put full production in 2031. Local coverage of state briefings gave other dates for site work. The construction start date is unconfirmed. Either way, the first Georgia-built Audi is years away.

Which models the plant will build is also unconfirmed. Reuters cited US officials who named the Q7, Q8 and Q9. Döllner spoke of bringing Audi's largest, most premium SUVs to the site. Audi has not published an official model list.

Next door: Hyundai's Metaplant

Georgia already hosts Hyundai Motor Group Metaplant America (HMGMA) in Ellabell, near Savannah. It opened in March 2025 and builds electric SUVs, including the Ioniq 5. Kia has assembled cars in West Point, Georgia, for years. Reports say this existing supplier base helped Georgia win the Audi project. No direct partnership between Audi and Hyundai has been announced.

Hyundai is weighing a bigger Metaplant. CEO José Muñoz told CNBC in August that the company was considering more US output in Georgia. Carscoops and the Atlanta Journal-Constitution reported a possible rise from a planned 500,000 vehicles a year to about 700,000 to 800,000. Hyundai later said no final decision had been made. That expansion is unconfirmed.

South Korean cars also pay a 15% US tariff. Seoul agreed to that rate in exchange for large investment pledges in the United States. Audi and Hyundai are reacting to the same pressure: build more where you sell.

What is still unconfirmed

  • Board approval. Volkswagen's supervisory board has not voted. No confirmed date.
  • Models. Q7, Q8 and Q9 come from officials quoted by Reuters, not from an Audi model list.
  • Construction start. Audi says next year. Other dates exist in coverage.
  • Tariff relief. No terms of any agreement with the US government have been published.
  • Hyundai expansion. Under review, not decided.
  • Supplier contracts. No report confirms that Hyundai or Kia suppliers in Georgia will win Audi work.

This article is news, not investment advice. It makes no call on any company's shares.

What to watch next

  • The Volkswagen board vote. Approval turns a pledge into a budget.
  • An official model list. It tells US buyers which Audis may get cheaper to build locally.
  • Any published tariff terms. They show whether the plant came with conditions.
  • Hyundai's capacity decision. It shows how far Georgia's car cluster may grow.

Shopping for a Q7 or Q8 in the next few years? Check the window sticker for the final assembly point, and bookmark Audi's press page so you see the board decision the day it lands.
