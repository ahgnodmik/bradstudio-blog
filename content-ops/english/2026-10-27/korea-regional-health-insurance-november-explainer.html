If you run a business, freelance or live on savings in Korea and pay health insurance yourself, your November bill may not match what you paid last month.

Every November, the National Health Insurance Service (NHIS, Gungmin Geongang Boheom Gongdan) recalculates premiums for regionally insured households (jiyeok gaipja). These are people whose premiums are not deducted by an employer: self-employed workers, freelancers, retirees and many foreign residents. The new amount then applies for twelve months. This guide explains how the reset works, when you can ask for an adjustment and what foreign residents should check.

Why the bill changes in November

Regional premiums are based on income and property. The NHIS refreshes both data sets once a year.

  • May: You file your global income tax return with the National Tax Service for the previous year.
  • June 1: Local governments fix the property tax base for land, homes and buildings you own on that date.
  • October: The NHIS receives the income data from the National Tax Service.
  • November: The NHIS combines both and sets the new premium, starting with the November bill.

For the 2026 reset, that means income earned in 2025 and property held on June 1, 2026. The new premium runs from November 2026 to October 2027. Maeil Shinmun and Nocut News describe this same schedule.

The key point is the time lag. Your November 2026 bill reflects what you earned in 2025, not what you earn now. If your shop did well last year and closed this year, you can still get a bill based on last year's income.

The NHIS had not published this year's average change at the time of writing. Treat any figure you see before the official release as unconfirmed.

Ask for an adjustment if your income fell

The NHIS does not lower your premium on its own when your income drops. You have to apply for an adjustment.

Who can apply

  • Self-employed people who suspended or closed their business
  • Freelancers whose contracts ended
  • People who retired or left a job
  • People whose income fell sharply
  • People who sold property they owned

How to apply

You can visit an NHIS branch, apply by mail or fax, or call the NHIS customer center at 1577-1000. Maeil Shinmun reported that business owners who already reported a suspension or closure to the National Tax Service can apply online on the NHIS website or app without extra paperwork. Nocut News reported that the NHIS now links real-time tax income data, so some freelancers no longer need a contract termination certificate. Other cases may still need proof, such as a business closure certificate or a retirement certificate. Ask the NHIS which documents fit your case before you go.

The catch: settlement later

An adjusted premium is not final. Once your actual income for the year is confirmed through tax data, the NHIS recalculates. If you earned more than you reported, you pay the difference later. An NHIS official quoted by Nocut News said people who applied falsely end up paying the money back. Apply only if your income really fell.

A bigger change under review: automatic settlement

On October 20, the NHIS told the National Assembly's Health and Welfare Committee it was reviewing automatic settlement based on tax data. Today, settlement mostly depends on the subscriber applying. Under the plan, the NHIS would settle premiums itself using confirmed income.

The NHIS estimate covered about 9.08 million regional households. About 3.94 million would pay more, roughly 3.29 trillion won in total. About 2.32 million would get refunds of about 1.80 trillion won. Kukinews, Maeil Shinmun and News1 reported the same figures.

This is not policy yet. The Ministry of Health and Welfare said in a government briefing that no specific direction has been decided. Any change would need review by the Health Insurance Policy Deliberation Committee and amended rules. It does not affect this November's reset.

What foreign residents should check

Below, only points confirmed by at least two sources are stated as fact.

  • Mandatory enrollment. Since July 2019, most foreign residents who stay in Korea for six months or more must enroll in National Health Insurance. If you have no employer plan, you join as a regional subscriber. The Korea Times and KPMG both reported the rule.
  • The same November reset applies. As a regional subscriber, your premium is based on income and property, like a Korean regional subscriber.
  • An average-premium floor. If your calculated premium is below the average premium of all subscribers, the NHIS charges the average instead. KPMG and Crowe Korea both described this floor. If you hit the floor, an income adjustment may change little.
  • Unpaid premiums have consequences. If you miss payments, health insurance benefits are suspended until you clear the arrears. Arrears can also limit visa extensions. Seoul National University and Yonsei University guides for international residents both state this.

Unconfirmed for foreign residents

  • Which average sets the floor. One guide says the floor uses the previous November's all-subscriber average. We could not confirm this in a second source.
  • This year's floor amount. No official 2026 figure was found.
  • Visa-specific rules. Some guides say certain visa holders enroll from arrival or are assessed differently. These details were not confirmed by two sources.
  • A foreign-language helpline. A university page lists 033-811-2000 for English, Chinese, Vietnamese and Uzbek. The NHIS English site confirms a dedicated center for foreign residents but we could not confirm this number on it.

Three checks before your bill arrives

  • Did your work change this year? A business closure, a contract ending or retirement are all grounds to ask for an adjustment.
  • Did your 2025 income rise? Higher business or financial income in 2025 can raise the November bill.
  • Did you own property on June 1, 2026? Property sold after that date counts in next year's reset, not this one.

If you closed a business or stopped freelancing this year, gather your proof documents now. When the November bill arrives, compare it with October's, then call 1577-1000 or log in to the NHIS website to ask whether you qualify for an adjustment.
