Grand Theft Auto VI has its first official sales number, and it is a big one: Take-Two Interactive says the game brought in $3.79 billion in worldwide retail sales on its first day. Within hours, that figure was being shared alongside older third-party estimates and at least one badly mistyped version. This piece sorts out what the company actually announced, how it compares with GTA V in 2013, why an earlier Sensor Tower estimate was less than half as large, and what remains unknown.

What Take-Two announced

GTA VI launched on Thursday 19 November 2026. The following day, Take-Two and its Rockstar Games studio put out a press release with two headline figures:

  • $3.79 billion in worldwide day-one retail sales, based on company estimates through the end of 19 November.
  • 32.5 million units sold before release.

The Associated Press report carried by ABC News and The Washington Times repeated both numbers and the same time frame, as did Variety, PC Gamer and CBC. Take-Two described the result as a new record for entertainment across all media. That is the company's own characterisation. No independent body has yet compared it with film or music launches on a like-for-like basis, so treat the "biggest entertainment launch" framing as a claim rather than a verified ranking.

Retail sales are not revenue

The most common mistake in early coverage was treating $3.79 billion as money Take-Two earned in a day. It is not. Retail sales describe roughly what consumers paid at checkout, whether in a shop or on a console storefront. Before any of that reaches the publisher, platform holders and retailers take their share.

What Take-Two eventually books as net revenue or net bookings will be smaller, and it will appear in the company's next quarterly results rather than in a launch-week press release. The day-one figure is also an internal estimate. It has not been audited, and the company has not broken it down by edition, platform or the split between pre-orders and purchases made on launch day itself.

One rough division gives a sense of the mix. Dividing $3.79 billion by 32.5 million pre-release units works out to about $117 per unit. That number does not prove anything on its own, because the retail total may include launch-day buyers who were not part of the pre-release count, as well as more expensive editions. Take-Two has not said which, so the composition remains unconfirmed.

The "$37.9 billion" error

Some posts, particularly in Korean-language communities, described the launch as earning the equivalent of $37.9 billion. That is a decimal slip. Korean counts large numbers in units of ten thousand and a hundred million rather than thousands and billions, and moving the decimal one place while converting 3.79 billion turns it into a figure ten times too large. No official statement or major outlet reported anything close to $37.9 billion.

Figures of about $3.8 billion, which appeared in some headlines including The Guardian's, are simply rounding and are fine to use as long as the basis is clear.

Official figure versus the Sensor Tower estimate

Before Take-Two spoke, most coverage relied on market intelligence firm Sensor Tower. Its estimate, reported by FOX 5 New York, 80 Level and others, put GTA VI at about 19 million copies and $1.84 billion in sales by the morning of launch day, with PlayStation 5 accounting for roughly three quarters of purchases.

Sensor Tower's snapshot was taken earlier in the day, and several reports describe it as focused on digital console sales, which would leave out physical copies. Outside estimators generally work from indirect signals such as store rankings and sample panels, then scale them up using ratios from past releases. A launch on this scale can break those ratios. The sensible reading is that the estimate pointed in the right direction and undershot the final number, not that sales doubled in a few hours.

How it compares with GTA V

The cleanest comparison is with GTA V, because Take-Two reported that game on the same retail-sales basis. In September 2013, the company said GTA V sold $800 million on its first day and passed $1 billion within three days, as GamesRadar, Push Square and GameSpot reported at the time.

On that basis, GTA VI's first day is about 4.7 times GTA V's. Two caveats matter. First, this is a nominal comparison: it does not adjust for thirteen years of inflation or for higher game prices, so the gap in the number of people buying is smaller than the gap in dollars. Second, Take-Two has not yet released a three-day figure for GTA VI, so the "$1 billion in three days" benchmark has no direct counterpart yet.

Comparisons that set the Sensor Tower estimate against GTA V's official number mix two different methods. They were common in the day before the announcement and are worth discarding now that a like-for-like figure exists.

How the stock reacted

Take-Two shares moved more on the reviews than on the sales record. On Wednesday 18 November, the day before launch, the stock rose more than 6% to close at $216.61 as early reviews arrived, according to Fox Business, while Business Today put the gain above 8%. Reports on launch day itself conflict, with some describing early gains and others a decline. Accounts of the move on Friday 20 November, after the sales announcement, differ by outlet, so we treat the size of that move as unconfirmed. This is not investment advice.

What we still do not know

  • Launch-day unit sales: only the 32.5 million pre-release figure has been published.
  • Composition of the $3.79 billion: no split by edition, platform, region or pre-order versus same-day purchase.
  • Recognised revenue: due with Take-Two's next quarterly results.
  • Three-day and first-week totals: not yet announced.
  • Independent verification of the "entertainment record" claim: none so far.

How to cite the number

If you are writing, posting or making a video about GTA VI's launch, the accurate short version is: "$3.79 billion in day-one retail sales, a Take-Two estimate through 19 November, with 32.5 million units sold before release." Keep the words "retail sales" and "estimate" attached. Label Sensor Tower's $1.84 billion as an earlier third-party estimate. If you see $37.9 billion anywhere, it is wrong by a factor of ten.

Before you share a figure today, open Take-Two's investor relations news page and check it against the original release. Then note the date of the company's next earnings call, because that is when the retail-sales headline turns into reported revenue and the real financial picture comes into view.
