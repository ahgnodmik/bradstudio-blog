If you have a home loan in Korea, or you are about to sign for one, the past week brought two pieces of news that land directly on your monthly payment. The benchmark that sets most floating mortgage rates jumped to its highest level in two years and eight months, and the Bank of Korea meets on Thursday 26 November to decide whether to raise its policy rate again. This explainer walks through what moved, why it matters for your loan, why some fixed-rate loans are now cheaper than floating ones, and what to check before the decision.

This is general information, not financial advice. Nothing here is a recommendation to take out, keep or switch any loan.

What happened this week

On 16 November the Korea Federation of Banks published its October COFIX figures. COFIX, the Cost of Funds Index, is the number Korean banks use as the base for most floating-rate mortgages. The new-loan version of the index rose to 3.62%, up 0.19 percentage points from September. That is the highest reading since February 2024.

It was the second big move in a row. The September figure had already climbed 0.25 points to 3.43%, so the index is up 0.44 points over two months. Banks began passing the October number through to new floating-rate mortgages from 17 November.

The Korea Federation of Banks put the rise down to deposit rates. Market interest rates eased slightly in October, but banks kept paying more on time deposits, and that cost flows straight into the index.

How COFIX turns into your mortgage rate

COFIX is a weighted average of what Korea's major banks pay to raise money: time deposits, installment savings, bank bonds and similar funding. A bank takes that base, adds its own spread, and then subtracts any discounts you qualify for, such as having your salary paid into the account. The result is your floating rate.

There are three versions, and it matters which one your contract uses.

  • New-loan COFIX covers only money the banks raised that month. It reacts fastest when rates move.
  • Balance-based COFIX averages the cost of everything banks currently hold. It moves slowly.
  • New balance-based COFIX is a broader balance measure. It usually sits lowest and changes most gently.

Timing also depends on whether your loan is new or existing. A new floating-rate loan picks up the latest COFIX straight away. An existing loan only resets on the dates in your contract, often every six months. So two neighbours at the same bank can see their payments change in different months.

To get a feel for the size of the change, here is a simple calculation. On a 300 million won balance, a rise of 0.19 points adds roughly 570,000 won a year in interest. The full two-month rise of 0.44 points on the same balance adds about 1.32 million won a year. Both figures assume the principal stays the same and ignore your bank's spread and discounts, so treat them as rough guides only.

Why floating rates are now above fixed rates at some banks

Normally a fixed-rate mortgage costs more than a floating one. The bank is taking on the risk that rates rise during the fixed period, and it charges for that.

This month, several Korean outlets reported the opposite at some of the large commercial banks: floating-rate offers sitting above fixed-rate (usually five-year hybrid) offers. The cause is that the two products follow different benchmarks. Floating loans track COFIX, which went up. Fixed and hybrid loans mostly track bank bond yields, which went down. When the two benchmarks move in opposite directions, the usual gap can close or flip.

The picture differs from bank to bank. Some reportedly showed a full inversion, while at others the gap simply narrowed. If you are comparing products, pull quotes from each bank on the same day rather than relying on a headline.

The Bank of Korea decision on 26 November

The Bank of Korea's Monetary Policy Board holds its last rate meeting of the year on Thursday 26 November. The base rate currently stands at 3.00%. The board raised rates in July and August, then held unanimously at its 22 October meeting.

Some institutions expect another increase. These are forecasts, and the outcome will not be known until the announcement.

  • Citi said in a 17 November note that it expects 25 basis point hikes both this month and in February 2027, taking the rate to 3.50%.
  • Morgan Stanley was reported in October as expecting a rise to 3.25% in November and a peak of 3.50% next year.
  • Other Korean brokerages have been reported as split, with some seeing a pause after October's hold as a slowdown in tightening rather than an end to it.

A base rate decision does not change COFIX overnight. It works through deposit rates and market rates, which then show up in the following month's index. That is why borrowers watch both: the Bank of Korea decision on 26 November, and the November COFIX release that should follow in mid-December (exact date unconfirmed).

Switching from floating to fixed: the general trade-offs

Many borrowers are asking whether to move a floating loan to a fixed one. There is no single right answer, but the questions to ask are the same for everyone.

1. What will it cost to leave your current loan?

Repaying a mortgage early usually triggers an early repayment fee. Since 13 January 2025, Korean regulators have required banks to charge no more than their actual costs. Korean reports put the average mortgage fee at the five largest banks at 0.65% after the change, down from 1.4% for fixed-rate loans and 1.2% for floating. Reports also say the lower rates apply to contracts signed after the change, so an older loan may still carry the previous terms. Many products also reduce the fee as the loan ages. Your loan agreement states the exact rate and when it ends.

2. How long does it take to earn the fee back?

Here is a simple, purely illustrative calculation. Say you owe 300 million won, your floating rate is 5.0%, and a fixed offer is 4.8%. The 0.2 point gap saves about 600,000 won a year. A 0.65% fee on 300 million won is about 1.95 million won. Dividing the fee by the yearly saving gives roughly 3.3 years to break even. If the gap were 0.5 points instead, the saving would be about 1.5 million won a year and the break-even would be around 1.3 years. These rates are examples, not real quotes.

3. Will you qualify for the same amount?

A new loan means a new credit review under current lending rules, including Korea's debt-service ratio limits. Do not repay the old loan until the new one is approved at the amount you need.

4. What are your plans for the home?

If you might sell or move within two or three years, you could pay the fee and leave before the savings cover it. A fixed rate also cuts both ways. It protects you if rates keep rising, and it leaves you behind if they fall.

What to do before Thursday

Open your bank app this weekend and write down four things: your remaining balance, your current rate, which COFIX version your loan uses, and your next reset date. Then find the early repayment fee in your loan agreement. After the announcement on 26 November, get same-day fixed and floating quotes from your own bank and one or two others, and run the break-even sum above with your real numbers before you book a meeting with a loan officer.
