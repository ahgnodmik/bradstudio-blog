In many countries, getting a child into kindergarten means joining a waiting list or showing up early on enrollment day. In South Korea, it means a national online lottery with fixed deadlines that all fall within a few weeks every November. For the school year that starts in March 2027, the first lottery results come out on Monday, November 9, 2026, at 3 p.m., and the families who are selected have only two days to register. If you are raising a young child in Korea, or simply curious how the system works, this explainer covers the schedule, the rules that apply across the country, the places where regions differ, and what is still unclear for parents who are not Korean nationals.

Items found in only one region's coverage or one outlet are marked "unconfirmed." We were not able to read the original education office notices directly, and most sources are in Korean. How kindergarten admission works in Korea

Korean kindergartens, both public and private, take applications for the coming school year through a single government website rather than through each school. For the 2027 school year, applications run through the kindergarten admission menu of the government's integrated early childhood portal, known in Korean as the Yubo Tonghap Portal (유보통합포털). The portal brings daycare enrollment and kindergarten admission together in one place, and it took over the role of the earlier online kindergarten application service that many Korean parents knew as Cheoeum Hakgyoro.

Three features shape the process:

  • It is a lottery, not first come, first served. Reports from several regions say selection is made by computer draw. Applying in the first minute gives no advantage.
  • There are two rounds. A priority round comes first for children who meet specific eligibility criteria. A general round follows for everyone else, including children who were not selected in the priority round.
  • You can apply to several schools. New applicants can list up to three kindergartens. Seoul coverage says children already enrolled at a kindergarten can apply to up to two others; whether the same limit applies in every region is unconfirmed.

Priority round results and the two-day registration window

Results are viewed by logging in to the portal with the same account used to apply and opening the kindergarten admission menu. Because families could list up to three schools, the first thing to check is whether your child was selected at more than one. If so, you register at one kindergarten only. Places you do not register for are treated as declined and go back into the pool for later rounds.

Registration lasts just two days, November 10 and 11. Multiple reports say that a family who is selected but does not register within that window is treated as having given up the place. One guide citing Seoul's admission rules puts the deadline at 6 p.m. on November 11, but we found that in a single source, so treat the exact cutoff as unconfirmed and aim to finish on the 10th.

The general round

Families who were not selected in the priority round are not carried over automatically. They need to apply again during the general round, from November 17 to 23. Results are scheduled for November 26. If a child still has no place after that, kindergartens with vacancies run additional recruitment from December 1, with dates and methods set locally.

Who is eligible, by birth year

Sejong coverage lists eligible children as those born between January 1, 2021, and December 31, 2023. Korean kindergartens group classes by age in years, so the birth year decides which class a child joins.

Regional differences to know about

The national framework is shared, but each metropolitan or provincial office of education sets details. Changes reported for this year include:

  • Seoul. The Seoul Metropolitan Office of Education extended the priority application window from two days to three and allowed each kindergarten to rank its priority criteria in up to 10 tiers. Reports also said an unborn child may count toward multi-child status, but that detail is unconfirmed.
  • Online eligibility checks. Coverage says priority eligibility for groups such as legally designated low-income households, national merit recipients and North Korean defectors can now be checked in the system, with supporting documents submitted online. How widely this applies nationwide is unconfirmed.
  • North Gyeongsang. The Gyeongsangbuk-do Office of Education added a "favorites" feature that lets parents save kindergartens they are considering ahead of the general round.
  • Sejong. Sejong coverage says families without a digital certificate can apply in person at their preferred kindergarten. Whether other regions offer the same option is unconfirmed.
  • Daegu and South Gyeongsang. Both offices announced schedules in line with the national timeline, according to regional coverage.

Because fees, school bus service and after-school hours differ from one kindergarten to the next, and private kindergartens set their own cost structures, read each school's admission guidelines before you register.

A note for foreign-resident parents

We looked for confirmed guidance on how families without Korean citizenship apply through the portal, for example which identification number is used, whether a child's passport can replace a residence card, and whether any step must be done in person. We could not find this confirmed by two independent sources, and most of what appears online comes from individual blogs rather than official notices. We are therefore not describing a procedure here. If you are a foreign resident, contact the kindergarten you are interested in or your local office of education before the general round opens on November 17, and ask specifically how a foreign national child registers on the portal and which documents are required. Tuition support for foreign children also appears to follow a separate process, which is worth asking about at the same time.

Practical tips before the general round

  • Log in to the portal, register your child's details and complete identity verification well before November 17, so the application itself takes minutes.
  • Shortlist three kindergartens by commute time, operating hours and after-school care, since those affect daily life more than reputation.
  • Check each school's admission guidelines for class sizes and the number of places for your child's age group.
  • If you also put your child on a daycare waiting list, decide in advance which you would choose if both come through.

Here is what to do right now. After 3 p.m. today, log in to the portal and check the priority round result. If your child was selected, register at your chosen kindergarten on November 10 rather than waiting until the 11th, and decline any other offers by registering at only one. If your child was not selected, pick your three general round choices this week and set a phone reminder for the morning of Tuesday, November 17, when general round applications open.
