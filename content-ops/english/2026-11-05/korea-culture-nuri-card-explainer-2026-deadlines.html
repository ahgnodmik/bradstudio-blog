Every year, South Korea gives millions of its lowest-income residents a small, use-it-or-lose-it budget for things that tend to get cut first when money is tight: concert tickets, books, a film, a weekend trip, a swimming pool pass. The program is called the Culture Nuri Card. For 2026 it is worth 150,000 won per person, the window to apply closes on Monday, November 30, and any balance left on the card disappears after Thursday, December 31. If you live in Korea and qualify, or you help a parent or neighbour who does, this is the month to check.

This explainer is based on announcements from the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism as reported by Korean media and local governments, checked across at least two sources. Details that appeared in only one source are marked unconfirmed. Plans for 2027 have not been finalised.

What the Culture Nuri Card is

The Culture Nuri Card is the everyday name for a government culture voucher run by the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism (MCST). Korean-to-English translations of the program's formal name vary; The Kyunghyang Shinmun's English edition renders it as the Integrated Culture Voucher, and UNESCO's policy monitoring platform describes it as a culture voucher project, or integrated cultural pass.

The idea is simple. Each eligible person gets a prepaid card loaded with a fixed annual amount, and the card only works at registered merchants in three categories: arts and culture, domestic travel, and sports. It is not cash and it is not a general shopping card. Groceries and everyday household goods are outside its scope.

Who can get it

The card is for people aged six and over (for 2026, born on or before December 31, 2020) who fall into one of two groups defined under Korean welfare law:

  • National Basic Livelihood Security recipients, often called basic livelihood recipients, who receive the country's core public assistance.
  • The legally defined near-poverty group (in Korean, chasangwi), households just above the basic livelihood threshold.

The support is per person, not per household. A family with three eligible members should have three separate cards, each with its own balance. MCST said about 2.7 million people were covered in 2026, roughly 80,000 more than the year before.

Reports put the eligible birth years at 2008 to 2013 for the younger group and 1962 to 1966 for the older group.

The two deadlines people mix up

There are two separate dates, and they do different things.

November 30: last day to apply

This is the cut-off to get a card issued or to have this year's money loaded onto an existing card. Some guides say online applications close at 6 p.m. that day; that time was not confirmed through an official source, so it is safer to finish well before then. Several notices also warn that a city or county can close early if its local budget runs out, and that timing varies by area. If you have not applied yet, ask your community service centre whether your district is still accepting applications.

December 31: last day to spend

Money loaded in 2026 can only be used until the end of the year. The card itself can carry on into next year, but the 2026 balance cannot. Anything left is returned to the national treasury. It does not roll over and it cannot be refunded as cash. A person who received 150,000 won and spent 90,000 won will lose the remaining 60,000 won on January 1.

Automatic recharge, and why 30,000 won matters

MCST lets existing cardholders skip the application in the following year if two conditions are met: they spent at least 30,000 won on the card during the previous year, and they still qualify as a basic livelihood or near-poverty recipient. If both are true, the next year's money is loaded automatically.

Goyang City recently reminded residents that anyone whose 2026 spending stays below 30,000 won could miss out on automatic recharge for 2027 and would have to apply again. Gwangmyeong City described the same rule earlier in the year. If a card has barely been used this year, one or two purchases before the end of December could save a trip to the community centre in 2027. Whether the 2027 rules will keep the same threshold has not been announced.

Where the card works

The card is accepted at registered merchants in culture, travel and sports. Typical uses include:

  • Performances, exhibitions and cinema tickets
  • Books, including at registered bookshops
  • Domestic accommodation and transport for travel within Korea
  • Sports facilities and related activities

Online payments only work with online merchants registered on the Culture Nuri website. The list of merchants changes by region and over time, so check the merchant search on mnuri.kr before you plan a purchase. A few details circulating on blogs, such as a phone balance-check line and a December 15 deadline for card reissue, could not be confirmed through official sources and should be checked directly with the program.

What may change in 2027

The government's 2027 budget proposal for MCST, announced in September, totals 9.6345 trillion won, according to MTN and SBS Biz. The proposal includes these Culture Nuri items:

  • Expanding coverage from about 2.7 million to about 2.86 million people
  • An extra 50,000 won for around 390,000 residents of 89 depopulation areas

No change to the standard per-person amount has been announced. All of this is a proposal that still has to pass the National Assembly, so it is unconfirmed. The 2027 application dates have not been published either. In past years MCST has announced the new year's schedule early in the year.

Practical tips before the deadlines

  • Check every family member. Each eligible person has their own card and balance. It is easy to remember a parent's card and forget a teenager's.
  • Spend on fixed-price items first. A book, a cinema ticket or a train fare is easier to plan than a variable bill, and helps you finish the balance without overspending your own money.
  • Do not leave it to the last week. Year-end travel and performance bookings fill up, and some merchants may not process a payment on the final day the way you expect.
  • Help older relatives. Older cardholders are the most likely to have unused balances. A short call to check theirs can be the most useful thing you do this month.

What to do this week

Log in to mnuri.kr or the Culture Nuri app today and check, for each eligible person in your household, whether a 2026 card has been issued and how much is left. If someone has not applied, visit your community service centre or use the app before November 30. If a card has money on it, pick two or three specific purchases now, such as a book order, a December performance or a train ticket home, and complete them before December 31. If this year's spending is still under 30,000 won, make at least one purchase this month to stay on track for automatic recharge next year.
