Three-wheeled roadsters have always lived on the fringe of the car world: too much car to be a motorcycle, too few wheels to be taken seriously as a sports car. A new brand called MTPEAK wants to change that impression with the One All, an open two-seat trike with a mid-mounted 2.0-litre turbocharged engine, a carbon-fibre body and carbon-ceramic brakes. It made its public debut this week at EICMA 2026, the motorcycle show in Milan. Here is what the company has actually announced, what independent outlets agree on, and what is still missing.

Almost every performance figure in this article is a manufacturer claim. No independent road test has been published. Price, production numbers and sales markets are unconfirmed.

Who is MTPEAK?

MTPEAK is a brand nobody had heard of until this autumn. According to the company's press release and Japan's Young Machine, it is backed by Guangyu Industrial, a Chinese manufacturer that both sources describe as having roughly three decades of experience in precision manufacturing and CNC machining. The One All is the brand's first vehicle, and the name itself is labelled as provisional. Some outlets write it as "One-All."

The vehicle is on display at EICMA, which runs from November 3 to 8, 2026, in Hall 24 at stand O54E. The press release and Japan's Kuruma News give the same hall and stand number, and EICMA published its own article about the vehicle.

What the maker claims

The layout is the familiar "reverse trike" arrangement: two wheels at the front, one at the back. What sets the One All apart is where the engine sits and how much of it there is. MTPEAK says:

  • Engine: a 2.0-litre turbocharged unit mounted behind the cockpit, driving the single rear wheel.
  • Transmission: a nine-speed automatic with reverse.
  • Output: 165 kW and 385 Nm. English-language outlets convert that to about 221 hp, while Spanish and Italian outlets give around 224 CV. These are the same figure in different units, not a disagreement.
  • Power-to-weight: 370 hp per tonne, a number repeated by Yanko Design, designboom and several European outlets.
  • Chassis: a chromoly steel spaceframe with carbon-fibre body panels.
  • Brakes: ventilated carbon-ceramic discs.
  • Balance: 50:50 front-to-rear weight distribution, with a 305 mm-wide rear tyre.
  • Production: limited production planned for 2027.

A few things are missing from that list. MTPEAK has not published a curb weight, which means the 370 hp-per-tonne figure cannot be checked against anything. There is no official 0-100 km/h time. A top speed of 180 km/h appears in some coverage, but earlier reports said top speed had not been disclosed, so it is treated as unconfirmed here. Young Machine noted that detailed output figures, Japanese sales plans and pricing had not been announced.

The company frames the design around stability and grip despite having only three wheels. That is the maker's stated goal, not a tested result. Three-wheelers behave differently from cars under hard braking and cornering, and nobody outside the company has driven this one on a road or track yet.

How it compares with the trikes you can actually buy

The two best-known three-wheel roadsters on sale are the Morgan Super 3 from Britain and the Polaris Slingshot from the US. Both share the One All's two-front, one-rear layout. On paper, the One All's main differences are the mid-engine layout and the turbocharger. The Super 3 is the light, low-power choice: Motor1 and SlashGear both cite 118 hp from its Ford three-cylinder, and reviewers lean heavily on its low weight. The Slingshot has more power, and Polaris replaced its earlier GM-sourced engine with an in-house 2.0-litre four-cylinder for 2020, according to Fox News and other coverage. The One All's claimed 221 hp would put it at or slightly above the Slingshot's sportiest trim. But power figures mean little without a weight, and MTPEAK has not given one.

There is also a regulatory point. In the US, three-wheelers are often classified as motorcycles under federal rules, and The Drive cited that classification as part of what made it possible for Morgan to bring the Super 3 into the American market. Whether the One All would be classified the same way, in the US or anywhere else, depends on where and how MTPEAK chooses to sell it, which has not been announced.

Price and availability

The honest summary is that the One All is a show vehicle with a production plan, not a product you can order.

  • Confirmed by two or more sources: debut at EICMA 2026 in Milan, November 3-8; limited production planned for 2027; Guangyu Industrial as the company behind the brand; the 2.0-litre turbo, nine-speed automatic, 165 kW and 385 Nm figures (as manufacturer claims).
  • Single source, so unconfirmed: one Italian outlet reported a run of 100 units. No other source repeated that number.
  • Conflicting reports: top speed of 180 km/h.
  • Not announced: price, curb weight, acceleration, sales regions, ordering process, dealer network.

Should you pay attention?

If you follow niche performance vehicles, the One All is interesting for its combination of ingredients. Mid-engine, rear-drive three-wheelers with carbon bodies and carbon-ceramic brakes are rare, and a manufacturer with a machining background entering this space is a story in itself. Treat the spec sheet as a statement of intent until someone independent weighs it and drives it.

If you are actually shopping for a three-wheeler, the comparison is simpler. The Morgan and the Slingshot exist, have published prices and have been reviewed by many outlets. The One All has none of those yet. Before putting any money toward a new brand's limited-production vehicle, it is sensible to wait for a confirmed price, a written delivery timeline and clear information on whether the vehicle can be registered where you live.

For now, the practical step is to look at the manufacturer images in the Yanko Design and designboom articles, then check MTPEAK's official site at mtpeak.cn periodically for pricing and production announcements. If you are considering importing any three-wheeler, check your local vehicle classification and licensing rules first, because a trike that counts as a motorcycle in one country may need full car certification in another.
