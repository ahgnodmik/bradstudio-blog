If you live in Korea and carry a won-denominated loan or keep savings in a Korean bank, the Bank of Korea rate hike to 3.25% on Thursday 26 November 2026 will reach your account, though not on the same day and not by the same amount. The Monetary Policy Board raised the Base Rate by 0.25 percentage points, from 3.00% to 3.25%, in a 5–2 vote. This explainer covers what was decided, how the won reacted, when your loan or deposit rate is likely to move, and what to check this week. It is general information, not investment or financial advice.

What did the Bank of Korea decide on 26 November?

The Base Rate is now 3.25%. The Bank of Korea announced the 25 basis-point increase on 26 November, and Bloomberg, Reuters (via Yahoo Finance), SBS and the New Straits Times all reported the same move. It is the third hike of 2026, after increases in July and August, with a pause in October. Bloomberg counted 75 basis points of tightening this year. Reuters reported that 32 of 34 analysts it surveyed had expected the decision, and that the rate is now at its highest level since November 2024.

The vote was not unanimous. Five of the seven board members backed the hike. Board members Hwang Kunil and Lee Soohyung preferred to hold at 3.00%, according to SBS and several Korean outlets.

Why the Bank of Korea raised rates

The short answer is strong growth plus sticky inflation. Reuters and Bloomberg both linked the decision to a semiconductor export boom driven by AI demand, alongside inflation that remains above the 2% target. The bank lifted its 2026 growth forecast from 3.3% to 3.9% and its 2027 forecast from 2.9% to 4.1%, according to Reuters and Trading and Investment News.

Governor Shin Hyun Song said upstream cost pressures were the main driver of inflation, with robust domestic demand also contributing, Reuters reported. He argued that acting early could reduce the total amount of tightening needed. The board's statement said it would weigh the timing of further increases, according to SBS.

Will rates rise again?

Nothing is decided. The board's dot plot, which shows where each member expects the Base Rate to be in six months, moved its median up to 3.50%, with dots spread between 3.25% and 3.75%, according to Aju Business Daily, Dnews and Asia Times Korea (all in Korean). Shin told reporters the chart should be read as it appears. Several brokerages expect one more hike in the first half of 2027, but that is a forecast, not a policy decision. Each meeting will be decided on the data available at the time.

What it means for the Korean won

The won barely moved. Reports put it near 1,340 per US dollar after the decision: one outlet said it briefly firmed below 1,340, and another cited a level of about 1,343. The Bank of Korea described the exchange rate as broadly stable, supported by a large current account surplus, according to coverage of the decision. Officials did not frame the hike as a defence of the currency.

For foreign residents, the practical takeaway is modest. A higher Base Rate can make won assets slightly more attractive to investors, but exchange rates also respond to US interest rates, the dollar's broader direction and equity flows. Do not plan a large currency transfer around one rate decision. If you send money home regularly, compare the total cost (spread plus fee) across your bank and licensed remittance services rather than trying to time the market.

Korean loans: when will my interest rate go up?

Variable-rate mortgages linked to COFIX

Most variable-rate home loans in Korea follow COFIX, a funding-cost index published monthly by the Korea Federation of Banks, not the Base Rate itself. October's new-loan COFIX rose 0.19 points to 3.62%, its highest in two years and eight months, and KB Kookmin, Woori and NH NongHyup banks raised variable mortgage rates from 17 November, according to Korean reports. COFIX is usually published around the middle of each month, so the November figure is expected in mid-December. Its exact value and date are unconfirmed until the federation posts them.

If you already have a loan, your rate changes on the reset date in your contract (often every three, six or twelve months). Until then, your current rate usually stays in place. When the reset comes, the bank applies the index value at that time plus your fixed spread.

Fixed-rate loans and credit loans

Fixed-rate mortgages are priced off longer market rates such as bank bond yields. Markets had largely expected this hike, so some of the increase may already be in new fixed-rate offers. Credit loans and overdraft lines vary: some track short-term bank bond yields, others the CD rate. Check which index your loan uses in your bank app or loan agreement.

Points for foreign borrowers

  • Visa and loan terms. Some banks tie loan maturity to your visa expiry. If your residence permit renews before your loan resets, ask whether the renewal affects the terms.
  • Early repayment fees. If you plan to leave Korea, check the early repayment fee schedule now. Fees often shrink or disappear after a set period.
  • Refinancing. Switching from variable to fixed can raise your payment immediately. Compare written offers from at least two banks, including fees and registration costs.

Korean deposits: will savings rates rise?

Deposit rates may follow, but the size and timing are unconfirmed. Banks set deposit rates themselves, product by product. Before you lock in a time deposit, compare products on the Korea Federation of Banks' consumer portal and read the early-withdrawal terms carefully. A higher rate is worth less if you might need the money before maturity.

Deposit protection is another point to check. Since 1 September 2025, Korea's deposit insurance cap has been 100 million won per person per financial institution, covering principal and interest, according to the Financial Services Commission and Asia Economy. Investment products such as funds are not covered. The sources we checked do not address whether coverage depends on the depositor's nationality, so confirm your own case with the Korea Deposit Insurance Corporation or your bank.

What to do this week

  • Open your bank app and note three things for each loan: the index it uses (COFIX, bank bond yield or CD rate), your spread, and your next reset date.
  • Put a mid-December reminder in your calendar to check the November COFIX release on the Korea Federation of Banks website.
  • If a time deposit matures soon, compare at least three banks' current rates before rolling it over.
  • If you hold more than 100 million won at one bank, consider whether to spread it across institutions, and confirm coverage with the bank.
  • Read the official monetary policy statement in English on the Bank of Korea's website (bok.or.kr) rather than relying on summaries.
