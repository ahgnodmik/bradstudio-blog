If you bought an iPhone Duo, Apple's first foldable, you may have seen the photos: an inner screen split along the fold, sometimes within days of unboxing. Online it has already been nicknamed "Crackgate." Apple has acknowledged a problem, sent guidance to its stores, and says affected phones will be replaced. What it has not said is how many units are involved or what is causing it. Here is what is on the record as of November 3, 2026, and what to do if your own phone shows the symptom.

This article summarizes Apple's statements and press reports. The cause of the cracking and the number of affected phones have not been confirmed by Apple, and nothing here should be read as a finding that the device is defective.

The short version

  • Launch: The iPhone Duo reached Apple Stores and customers on Friday, October 23, 2026. The 256GB model starts at $1,999 in the US.
  • The symptom: Owners describe a crack or line on the inner display, running along the center fold just above the hinge.
  • Apple's statement (October 26): Apple says it is aware of an issue affecting the inner display on "a small number" of units and that owners should contact AppleCare for a replacement.
  • The store memo (reported October 30): Apple told its stores and Authorized Service Providers how to diagnose the problem and to repair or replace affected phones under standard procedures.
  • AppleCare+: Multiple outlets report it is not required to get this replacement.
  • Unknown: the cause, the number of phones affected, and whether Apple will launch a formal repair program.

What Apple has said publicly

Apple stayed quiet through launch weekend while posts piled up on Reddit and other forums. That changed after Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reported on October 26 that several buyers had contacted him with photos and accounts of cracks shortly after purchase. Apple then gave outlets including MacRumors and MacTrast a short statement. It said it was aware of an issue impacting the inner display on a small number of iPhone Duo units, and that anyone experiencing problems should contact AppleCare for a replacement.

Apple also reportedly said that most units are not susceptible, and that phones which do show the problem tend to do so early, during unboxing or initial setup. That detail matters for owners. If your phone has been folding and unfolding normally for a week, the reports so far suggest you are past the window when this particular failure has typically appeared. It is not a guarantee, because Apple has not published data on timing.

Apple did not describe the problem as a defect, did not give a cause, and did not announce a recall or a named service program. Everything beyond the statement itself comes from reporting and independent testing.

What the store memo says

On October 30, MacRumors reported that it had obtained an internal memo Apple sent to Apple Stores and Authorized Service Providers. iDrop News covered the same memo. According to those reports, the memo gives technicians guidance on diagnosing the inner display cracking. When a customer reports the symptom, technicians are told to inspect the phone and repair or replace it in line with their standard procedures.

The memo repeats the public language that the issue affects a small number of units. MacRumors noted that the existence of a formal diagnostic memo suggests the problem is common enough to need official handling in stores. That is an inference from the outlet, not something Apple said.

In practice, the memo means the frontline staff you will meet have instructions for this exact symptom. You should not have to argue that the problem exists.

How replacements work, step by step

Apple has not published a dedicated support page for this issue, so the process below combines Apple's statement, the memo reporting, and ordinary Apple service steps. Check the details with Apple when you contact it.

  • Document it first. Take clear photos of the inner display in good light, with the phone fully open. Note the date the crack appeared and what you were doing, such as opening it for the first time.
  • Contact AppleCare. Use the Apple Support app, apple.com/support, or phone support, and describe the inner display issue specifically. Mention that Apple has acknowledged it.
  • Book an inspection. Apple will likely route you to an Apple Store or an Authorized Service Provider. Under the memo, a technician inspects the phone before deciding on repair or replacement.
  • Back up before you go. A replacement phone means restoring from a backup. Run an iCloud or computer backup the night before.
  • Ask about timing. Launch supply of the iPhone Duo has been tight. Ask whether a replacement unit is in stock, and how long you will be without a phone if it is not.

Several outlets report that AppleCare+ is not required for this replacement. The technician's inspection still decides the outcome. A phone with signs of a drop or other impact damage could be assessed differently, so do not assume every crack will be covered.

What it costs if the crack is not covered

Before launch, Apple posted out-of-warranty repair estimates for the iPhone Duo, which 9to5Mac and Notebookcheck reported. Replacing the inner display without coverage was listed at $999, roughly half the phone's starting price. The outer display was listed at $379. These are Apple's estimates, and the final fee is set after inspection. They apply to damage outside warranty, not to the acknowledged issue Apple says it will handle through AppleCare.

How many phones, and why

No one has a reliable count. Apple has said only "a small number." 9to5Mac counted roughly ten unique cases over launch weekend. PhoneArena has argued that the small number may not be so small. Some viral posts put the figure in the thousands, but no source has verified that, so treat any large number as unconfirmed.

The picture is muddied by fakes. Gulf News and Cybernews reported that at least one widely shared crack image appeared to be AI-generated, and MacRumors flagged similar concerns. Real cases exist, which Apple's own statement makes clear, but some of the images circulating are not evidence of anything.

On cause, the most detailed theory comes from iFixit. Using CT scans and a 3D model of the hinge, it suggested that parts of the hinge mechanism might press on the underside of the inner panel near where the cracks appear. iFixit presented this as a possible explanation, not a diagnosis, and Apple has not commented on it.

Durability tests point in different directions. A Polish YouTuber folding a single phone by hand saw a vertical line appear on the inner screen at around 75,000 folds, according to Notebookcheck and iPhone in Canada. That is one unit under artificial stress, and there is no evidence it is the same failure as cracks appearing on day one.

What to watch next

  • Whether Apple publishes a support page or a named service program for the iPhone Duo inner display.
  • Whether replacement units show the same problem.
  • Any update from Apple or iFixit on the cause.
  • How long replacements take while supply is tight.

If you own an iPhone Duo, open it today under a bright light and look along the fold for a hairline crack or a vertical line. If you see one, photograph it, back up the phone, and contact AppleCare to book an inspection. Ask directly whether the replacement is covered and whether a unit is in stock before you hand your phone over.
